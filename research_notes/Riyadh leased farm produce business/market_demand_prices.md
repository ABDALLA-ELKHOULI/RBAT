## 1. Current wholesale and retail prices in Riyadh (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Official per-kg retail averages exist for tomatoes, roughly 4.9–7.3 SAR/kg over 2025–26. For most other products I found only supermarket listings or undated news figures. Crate prices for tomatoes swing between about 15 and 65 SAR depending on season. Fresh whole chicken sells for about 15–21 SAR/kg retail. A Naimi or Harri sheep costs about 1,700–2,800 SAR a head.

### Inferences
- Local tomatoes at retail mostly sit between about 5 and 7.5 SAR/kg. A 10 kg crate wholesale at 15–20 SAR in a glut works out to 1.5–2 SAR/kg. In a spike, 40–65 SAR works out to 4–6.5 SAR/kg. The farm-gate price is therefore far more volatile than the retail average suggests.
- Commodity chicken (about 16 SAR/kg retail from large integrators) and commodity eggs (about 6–15 SAR per tray, often on promotion) are dominated by industrial players. A small farm cannot compete on price. Only a differentiated product such as بلدي or free-range could be viable.
- Sheep have the highest unit value, about 1,700–2,800 SAR a head, and prices peak around Eid al-Adha.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 published crate prices for the Azizia central market. No wholesale prices for cucumbers, peppers, zucchini, eggplant, okra, jarjeer, coriander, mint, lettuce, mulukhiyah, strawberries, watermelon, melon, onions or potatoes; snippets did not surface GASTAT per-kg values for these.
- No sourced price for بيض بلدي / free-range eggs in Riyadh, and none for strawberries (including Al-Kharj strawberries), mushrooms, microgreens or dates.
- No 2025–26 data on goat prices beyond the GASTAT April 2025 تيس بلدي figure.
- The primary sources to check by hand are GASTAT "Average prices of goods and services" (stats.gov.sa, monthly CSV, per kg by product, local vs imported), GASTAT's Wholesale Price Index, and the Sahem/MEWA wholesale-market bulletins.

## 2. Seasonality: when prices crash and when they spike

### Takeaway
Saudi vegetable prices follow the local open-field season. Supply is heavy and prices are low from roughly late autumn to spring. Prices spike in the hot months, and again whenever a regional season ends and imports fall (for example, tomatoes −21% m/m in Dec 2025, then crate spikes of 140–300% in 2026). Greenhouse growers who produce through the summer gap capture those spikes.

### Cited Findings
- December 2025: local tomato wholesale prices fell 21.4% month on month (GASTAT WPI) — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/business/saudi-inflation-edges-up-to-21-on-higher-rents-gastat-2629392)
- Traders expect summer tomato prices of up to 40 SAR (per crate) once the open-field season ends and the market depends entirely on greenhouses and imports. This is a trader's estimate, not official data — reported in search results for [Aleqt](https://www.aleqt.com/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1/%D9%85%D8%A7-%D8%B3%D8%A8%D8%A8-%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AC%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%82-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B6%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-7197). The snippet may come from a related Saudi news page.
- A vendor named import stoppages and higher supply as the causes of price declines (opinion) — same [Aleqt](https://www.aleqt.com/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1/%D9%85%D8%A7-%D8%B3%D8%A8%D8%A8-%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AC%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%82-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B6%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-7197)
- Eastern Province (2026): a tomato crate went from 15–20 to about 65 SAR in four days as production wound down; supply had shifted to Tabuk and Al-Jouf. Other headlines report crates at 25→60 SAR (+140%) and a 300% rise — [Alyaum](https://www.alyaum.com/articles/6665415/); [Alyaum](https://www.alyaum.com/articles/6622564/)
- GASTAT monthly local-tomato retail prices show the swing at retail: 6.74 (Nov 2025) → 4.9 (Mar 2026) → 7.3 SAR/kg (Jul 2026, itself −10% m/m, so June 2026 was about 8.1 SAR/kg) — [CEIC](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/saudi-arabia/average-prices-of-goods-and-services/prices-of-goods-and-services-avg-vegetables-tomatoes-imported); [Alyaum Mar](https://www.alyaum.com/articles/6656229/); [Alyaum Jul](https://www.alyaum.com/articles/6678291/)
- Riyadh price surge of more than 100%, with a 10 kg tomato crate at 38–45 SAR, blamed on climate (undated) — [Al-Riyadh](https://www.alriyadh.com/2155714)
- Egg prices rose year on year in Jan–Jun 2026 and fell in Jul–Aug 2026 — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/ar/article/articledetail/id/1937273)
- Sheep prices peak before Eid al-Adha (Riyadh Harri up to 2,600 and Naimi up to 2,800 SAR) — [Al-Riyadh](https://www.alriyadh.com/2189693)

### Inferences
- A rough calendar, inferred from the reports above rather than from an official seasonal index: retail local-tomato prices bottom around Feb–Apr (4.9 SAR/kg in March) and peak around Jun–Sep (about 7–8 SAR/kg). Wholesale crate prices move much more, often 2–4× within weeks.
- Nov–Dec can also bring sudden gluts as open-field harvests start (Dec 2025 −21% m/m).
- A small farm 50–90 km from Riyadh that sells open-field crops in winter will sell into the low-price window. Shade-net or cooled greenhouse production for May–October would hit the high-price window, but cooling costs in Riyadh's summer are significant (not quantified here).
- Leafy herbs are reported as especially heat-volatile in nearby markets (Kuwait, Egypt), which suggests a similar summer scarcity in Riyadh. I found no Saudi data to confirm it.

### Gaps
- No official month-by-month seasonal price index for Riyadh, and no seasonality data for cucumbers, peppers, herbs, okra or watermelon. GASTAT monthly CSVs would allow one to be built.
- No data on how much of the summer supply in Riyadh comes from greenhouses versus imports.

## 3. Self-sufficiency rates (where the gaps are)

### Takeaway
GASTAT 2024: dates (121%), table eggs (103%), dairy (131%), eggplant (105%), okra (102%), cucumbers (101%) and zucchini (100%) are saturated or in surplus. The real import gaps are onions (72%), peppers (78%), tomatoes (83%), potatoes (93%) and poultry meat (72%), plus red meat (sheep) and fruit in general.

### Cited Findings
- GASTAT Food Security Statistics 2024: potatoes 93%, tomatoes 83%, onions 72%, table eggs 103%, poultry meat 72%, dates 121%, dairy 131% — [Argaam EN](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1867020); [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/gcc/saudi-arabia-surpasses-100-self-sufficiency-in-key-food-products-led-by-dairy-dates-and-eggs-mgvb062d); [Saudi Gazette via DairyNews](https://dairynews.today/news/saudi-arabia-achieves-self-sufficiency-in-dairy-and-other-key-food-products.html)
- 2024 by vegetable: eggplant 105%, okra 102%, cucumbers 101%, zucchini 100%, peppers 78% (reported in Arabic press summaries of GASTAT 2024; the search tool merged several articles) — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/economy/na/2226120); [Aleqt](https://www.aleqt.com/الأخبار/ارتفاع-نسب-الاكتفاء-الذاتي-ل-13-منتجا-غذائيا-في-السعودية-4617); [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://aawsat.com/node/5218379)
- Change from 2023: onions +41.2% (51% → 72%, a relative change), tomatoes +9.2%, poultry +1.4% — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/gcc/saudi-food-self-sufficiency-rates-surge-significantly-in-2024-blres82r)
- GASTAT 2023: potatoes 87%, tomatoes 76%, onions 51% — [GASTAT](https://www.stats.gov.sa/w/news/5)
- About 8 billion table eggs were produced in 2024 (MEWA) — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/economy/na/2226120)
- Older or conflicting figures, **year unclear, do not use as 2024**: onions 44% and dates 124% — [Leaders MENA](https://www.leaders-mena.com/self-sufficiency-rates-of-key-agricultural-products-in-saudi-arabia/); potatoes 80% and tomatoes 67% — [Asharq Al-Awsat EN](https://english.aawsat.com/business/4520356-saudi-arabia-achieves-surplus-self-sufficiency-top-3-food-items?page=6)
- One 2026 news report says Saudi Arabia imports about 70% of its fruits and vegetables. It links Hormuz shipping disruption to more attention on Riyadh-area hydroponics. This figure conflicts with the vegetable-level GASTAT rates and probably refers to fruit plus vegetables by value or volume; unverified — [CSMonitor, Aug 2026](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Middle-East/2026/0817/saudi-arabia-hydroponic-farms-food-security)

### Inferences
- **Saturated (avoid as a commodity play):** dates, table eggs, cucumbers, zucchini, eggplant, okra. Their prices crash in season, and big producers set the market.
- **Under-supplied:** peppers (78%) and tomatoes (83%), which are mainly a summer gap, plus onions (72%) and potatoes (93%). Onions and potatoes are large-acreage, low-price-per-kg crops and do not suit 2,000–10,000 m².
- Poultry meat is only 72% self-sufficient, but the gap is filled by industrial integrators and frozen imports. A small farm gains little from it except niche بلدي birds.
- Red meat (sheep and goats) and fruit are structural import categories; see the self-sufficiency press releases above. I found no 2024 percentage for red meat in the snippets.

### Gaps
- No 2024 self-sufficiency rates for red meat (sheep and goats), leafy greens and herbs, strawberries, watermelon or mushrooms in the snippets. GASTAT's full Food Security Statistics 2024 table should have most of these.
- No Riyadh-region breakdown, as GASTAT reports national figures.

## 4. Consumer trends in Riyadh (organic, farm-fresh delivery, premium herbs, local honey, بيض بلدي)

### Takeaway
There is evidence of premium niches, mostly from market-research summaries of weak quality: a ~30% organic premium, about 6× for organic hydroponic parsley compared with a conventional bundle, and Sidr honey at 150–600 SAR/kg. Riyadh is named as the main demand centre for microgreens and hydroponic herbs. Large hydroponic operators are already in Al-Kharj, so they compete directly.

### Cited Findings
- Organic food in Saudi Arabia is about 30% more expensive than conventional (market-research summary) — [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/saudi-arabia-organic-food-retail-market); [Research&Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/report/saudi-arabia-organic-grocery-market)
- MEWA's organic agriculture study (undated) says the organic produce market is still small. It names Riyadh, Dammam/Khobar and Jeddah as the main consumption centres, and mentions farmers' markets and supermarket organic sections in Riyadh — [MEWA PDF](https://www.mewa.gov.sa/ar/Ministry/Agencies/AgencyofAgriculture/OrganicCompain/PublicationsDocs/KSA-Studiefor%20English.pdf)
- Organic hydroponic parsley from Al-Kharj sells for 6.35 SAR per 150 g bundle, compared with about 1 SAR for a conventional bundle — [Alqimam listing](https://alqimam-alwatania.com/%D8%A8%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%86%D8%B3-%D8%B9%D8%B6%D9%88%D9%8A-%D8%B1%D8%A8%D8%B7%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%B7%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8); [Kanbkam](https://supermarket.kanbkam.com/sa/ar/green-parsley-leaves-1-bundle-T16205)
- Saudi microgreens market estimated at USD 18.5 million in 2025, with demand concentrated among young urban consumers in Riyadh, Jeddah and Dammam (low-quality source) — [Vocal Media](https://vocal.media/futurism/saudi-arabia-microgreens-market-urban-farming-health-conscious-consumers-and-growth-outlook)

### Inferences
- The best-supported premium for a small farm is in **branded or organic herbs and leafy greens**, with multiples of several times the price per bundle, and in **direct-to-consumer and restaurant supply** in Riyadh. Both rely on Riyadh's high share of premium demand.
- Organic certification adds cost and time. A "farm-fresh / local / pesticide-free" brand sold through direct delivery may capture part of the premium without certification. This is an inference; no data was found.
- Large Al-Kharj hydroponic players such as Dava already supply lettuce and herbs to retail. A small farm would need to differentiate on freshness, variety (specialty herbs, microgreens) and direct relationships.

### Gaps
- No sourced Riyadh price for بيض بلدي / free-range eggs, so the premium over the 6–15 SAR commodity tray is unknown.
- No restaurant purchase prices for herbs or microgreens, and no price data for mushrooms; local production and import data for mushrooms was also not found.
- No data on the size of farm-fresh delivery services (Instagram, WhatsApp and app-based farm boxes) or their pricing in Riyadh.
- Organic premium figures come from paid market-research summaries with weak sourcing.

## 5. Margins between farm-gate, wholesale and retail

### Takeaway
I found no Saudi-specific data on the split between farm-gate, wholesale and retail prices. Regional evidence suggests intermediaries take a large share of the consumer price, with gaps of 122–300% between farm and consumer (Jordan). Saudi greengrocer margins are put at about 20–40% (informal source). The size of these gaps is the argument for selling direct.

### Cited Findings
- Jordan: the gap between the farmer's selling price and the consumer price is 122%–300% — [7iber](https://www.7iber.com/%D8%B1%D9%81%D8%B9-%D8%A3%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B6%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%B9-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B9/)
- Lebanon: the farmer gets about 10 cents of each dollar spent on local vegetables; wholesalers and intermediaries get 30–40 cents, retailers 50–60 cents — [Alsifr](https://alsifr.org/who-profits-fruit-and-vegetable-market)
- Saudi analysis: produce passes through a long chain of intermediaries, each adding a margin, which partly explains why consumers do not feel the >100% self-sufficiency — [Al-Araby](https://www.alaraby.co.uk/economy/%D8%A7%D9%83%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%BA%D8%B0%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%8A-%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%D9%8A-%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%A7%D9%88%D8%B2-100-%D9%84%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B0%D8%A7-%D9%84%D8%A7-%D9%8A%D8%B4%D8%B9%D8%B1-%D8%A8%D9%87-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%87%D9%84%D9%83)
- Saudi greengrocer gross margin is about 20–40% of the selling price (informal business-advice site) — [Tharei](https://tharei.com/%D8%A3%D8%B1%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%AD-%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84-%D8%AE%D8%B6%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%88%D9%81%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%83%D9%87-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%D9%8A/)
- Riyadh: the same tomato crate sold for 30 SAR at one shop and 40 SAR next door, a 33% spread at the same retail tier (undated) — [Al-Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1043104)

### Inferences
- Implied tomato spread: a glut crate of 15–20 SAR per 10 kg is about 1.5–2 SAR/kg wholesale, against a retail average of about 5–7 SAR/kg. Retail can therefore be 2.5–4× the wholesale price in glut periods. This mixes dates and sources, so it is only indicative.
- Selling direct to households or restaurants in Riyadh could capture much of that spread. Logistics over 50–90 km (cold chain, delivery cost) would eat into it.

### Gaps
- No Saudi farm-gate prices, commission rates at the Riyadh central market (the dallal or auction commission), or MEWA studies on margins were found.
- No cost data for transporting from Al-Kharj, Durma or Muzahmiyah to Riyadh.
