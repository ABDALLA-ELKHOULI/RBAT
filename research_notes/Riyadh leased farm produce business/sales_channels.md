# Sales Channels for a Small Farm near Riyadh (vegetables, herbs, eggs, livestock)

Research note (compiled 2026-11-17). Method: web search snippets plus fetch attempts. **Network limits:** arabnews.com, argaam.com and sabq.org could not be resolved from this session (they are outside the allowed network domains), so claims from those sites come from search-result snippets/summaries only, not from reading the full pages. Many hoped-for specifics (Riyadh dalal commission %, supermarket supplier terms) were not found in any public source; they are listed as Gaps rather than estimated.

## 1. Central wholesale market (Aziziyah / حلقة الخضار): how farmers sell, dalal commission, price gap, payment

### Takeaway
The Aziziyah central fruit-and-vegetable market (Riyadh Development Co.) is the dominant wholesale channel, which the operator says handles about 60% of the Kingdom's fresh-produce trade. Riyadh's wholesale market setup is changing: the North Market was evacuated in Oct 2025, and new operator and development contracts were signed. No public source gives the Riyadh dalal commission rate. Comparable regional figures are about 6–7% of sale value, plus unloading and other costs.

### Cited Findings
- Riyadh Development Co. (شركة الرياض للتعمير) says 60% of fresh-produce transactions in the Kingdom pass through its central fruit and vegetable market in Aziziyah, which it developed in 1998 on its own land. Source: search snippet of the company's sectors page. [riyadh.dev "Our Sectors"](https://new-design.riyadh.dev/en/?p=26879)
- A Sabq report on a municipal-council tour of the Aziziyah market listed poor cleanliness, **expatriate labor controlling the market** ("عمالة تتخفى في ملابس خضراء للسيطرة على سوق خضار عزيزية الرياض") and unlicensed stalls among its main findings. This is a known structural issue for farmers selling there. The article is undated in the snippet and is likely older. [Sabq](https://sabq.org/article/Fa-Dd-K) (snippet only; sabq.org blocked)
- MEWA and Riyadh Development Co. signed an agreement to launch the **"Digital Portal for Vegetables and Fruits" (البوابة الرقمية للخضار والفواكه)**, an e-portal for the Taameer vegetable market. Its stated aims are to empower farmers, keep supply stable, develop public-benefit markets and improve quality and oversight. The snippet gives no details on direct-sale mechanics, fees or registration. [MEWA partnership page](https://www.mewa.gov.sa/ar/Ministry/AboutMinistry/Partnerships/PrivateSector/Pages/riyadhdev.aspx); [MEWA news](https://www.mewa.gov.sa/ar/MediaCenter/News/Pages/News10132020.aspx) (the URL pattern suggests 2020)
- The **National Company for Agricultural Services (الشركة الوطنية للخدمات الزراعية)** set **30 Oct 2025** as the deadline to evacuate the **Riyadh North Market for Fruits & Vegetables**. [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/bf/arriyadh/article/id/1843157/riyadh-s-north-market-evacuation-deadline-set-for-oct-30) (snippet only; argaam blocked)
- A government Furas listing covers a project to "manage, develop, lease, operate and maintain the central vegetable and fruit market in Riyadh city". The same company tenders similar markets in Kharj, Dawadmi, Shaqra and elsewhere. Permitted activities are "sale of agricultural products and loading/unloading services". [Furas (MOMAH)](https://furas.momah.gov.sa/index.php/en/node/390685)
- Aleqt headline: "Multiple-business company signs contract to develop the Saudi capital's vegetable and fruit market" (الأعمال المتعددة توقع عقدا لتطوير سوق العاصمة السعودية للخضار والفواكه). Only the headline was seen; the date is unknown. [Aleqt](https://www.aleqt.com/الشركات/الأعمال-المتعددة-توقع-عقدا-لتطوير-سوق-العاصمة-السعودية-للخضار-والفواكه-3707)
- Before it was evacuated, the Saudi Organic Agriculture Association opened an **organic farmers' outlet ("الزرع العضوي") inside the North Market in Riyadh**, under MEWA patronage, to market organic farmers' produce. [Search summary of MEWA/Al-Watan items](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/ampArticle/1151873) (exact attribution among the result set is uncertain)
- **Nearest regional commission benchmarks (not Riyadh vegetables):**
  - The dalal in the Buraidah Dates City market takes **7%** from the seller. [Al-Mowaten](https://www.almowaten.net/?p=378058) (search snippet)
  - At Amman Central Market (Jordan), the agent's commission is under 6% of sale value, plus offloading, packing and transport costs, and retail prices ran 122–300% above farm-gate. Pakistani commission agents take 5–8%, and one study found farmers received only 57% of the auction price. [IGC study](https://www.theigc.org/publications/understanding-fresh-produce-supply-chain-dynamics-and-price-wedges); [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1922700)

### Inferences
- Dalal commission at Aziziyah is likely in the ~5–8% range seen in comparable Gulf and regional markets, plus porterage (عتالة) and transport costs. **This is an inference, not a sourced Riyadh figure.** The bigger cost is price-taking: the auction clears at the day's glut price, and farm-gate is a fraction of retail.
- Wholesale suits bulk and low-value crops (tomatoes, cucumbers, leafy greens in glut season) and works as a "dump" outlet for surplus. It is a poor primary channel for a 2,000–10,000 m² farm, because volume is too small to matter and margins are lowest.
- Payment at the auction is usually same-day cash or settlement through the dalal (typical practice). This was not confirmed by a Riyadh source.
- The operator and market restructuring (North Market closure, the new operator, the digital portal) means current rules should be checked on-site before planning around this channel.

### Gaps
- No source found for the official or customary dalal commission % at Aziziyah, the market entry or unloading fees, or payment terms. Sabq and Argaam full texts were blocked.
- No Riyadh-specific farm-gate vs wholesale vs retail price-gap study found.
- Whether the MEWA digital portal lets small farmers sell directly, and its fees, is unknown.

## 2. Supermarkets (Tamimi, Danube, Panda, Othaim, Lulu, Carrefour KSA)

### Takeaway
No public local-farmer supplier program, listing-fee schedule or payment-term disclosure was found for any of these retailers. Formal requirements are clear in principle: a CR, VAT registration once over the threshold, and SFDA/food-safety compliance. Saudi B2B payment cycles are long, and small suppliers typically wait about 60–90 days. MEWA runs a campaign ("ريفنا صار أقرب") specifically to get small farmers' products into retail.

### Cited Findings
- MEWA's Deputy Minister launched the **"ريفنا صار أقرب" (Our countryside is closer)** campaign to support small farmers and increase their access to retail outlets and stores. It runs through the **Saudi Reef Academy (أكاديمية ريف)**, which qualifies products to display and distribution standards. [MEWA news](https://www.mewa.gov.sa/ar/MediaCenter/News/Pages/reefna-closer-farmers-support.aspx)
- **VAT:** registration is mandatory when taxable revenue exceeds **375,000 SAR** in 12 months, and voluntary between **187,500 and 375,000 SAR**. [ZATCA SME guideline](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/%28SMEs%29%20Guidelines%20%E2%80%93%20Zatca%201.pdf); [Wafeq](https://www.wafeq.com/en-sa/tax-and-reporting/vat-registration-in-saudi-arabia-zatca-step-by-step-guide-for-businesses)
- A Qoyod report says Saudi Arabia has the **longest working-capital cycle in the region**, and invoice factoring is rising because customers pay late. An analysis of SAMA's draft supply-chain-finance rules uses a 90-day buyer invoice as the typical SME pain point. [Qoyod](https://www.qoyod.com/en/reports/payment-collection-saudi/); [Vision2030.ai analysis](https://vision2030.ai/analysis/sama-supply-chain-finance-draft-rules-sme-working-capital/)
- Carrefour KSA's online fresh section already lists hydroponic and organic local-brand produce (e.g. "Jawhara hydroponic lettuce") with 60-minute Riyadh delivery. Branded hydroponic growers are therefore the incumbents on supermarket shelves. [Carrefour KSA fruits & vegetables](https://www.carrefourksa.com/mafsau/ar/n/c/clp_fruits-vegetables-ksa)

### Inferences
- For a farm of this size, direct supermarket supply is hard. It needs a CR, consistent daily volume and grading, packaging and labels, likely barcodes, food-safety documentation, and the ability to carry receivables of about 60–90 days. A 100k SAR budget leaves little working capital for that.
- The realistic route into retail is indirect: through an aggregator or distributor (see Nabt, Grove and Farmi in section 4) or through Reef Academy support.
- Small independent groceries and butchers (بقالات / تموينات) in Riyadh suburbs may buy cash on delivery. This is not sourced.

### Gaps
- No public supplier-onboarding pages or terms (listing fees, rebates, payment days, barcode/GS1 requirements) found for Tamimi, Danube, Panda, Othaim, Lulu or Carrefour (MAF).
- No evidence found of a dedicated "local farmer program" at any of these chains.

## 3. HORECA (restaurants, cafés, hotels): herbs, microgreens, lettuces

### Takeaway
There is demonstrated Riyadh demand from chefs and hotels for ultra-fresh herbs, microgreens, edible flowers and salad greens, because imported herbs often arrive degraded. However, several funded hydroponic and vertical farms already target this niche (Bather/Green Basket, Arable, YesHealth/Mowreq, Jana Farm). A small farm must compete on niche varieties, reliability and relationship selling.

### Cited Findings
- **Bather Smart Farm / Green Basket** is a 2,500 m² vertical farm in Riyadh, built with iFarm technology. It grows microgreens, edible flowers, salad greens and herbs, and supplies individuals, retailers, restaurants, distributors, and **haute-cuisine restaurants and hotels in Riyadh**. [iFarm](https://ifarm.fi/news/green-basket-to-launch-vertical-farm-with-ifarm-tech-in-riyadh); [Agritecture 2023](https://www.agritecture.com/blog/2023/3/29/bringing-the-freshest-salad-greens-to-saudi-arabia-with-bather-smart-farm)
- Chefs are "particularly interested". The most consistent problem chefs cite is that **imported produce often arrives expired or in poor condition**. [Agritecture/Bather interview](https://www.agritecture.com/blog/2023/3/29/bringing-the-freshest-salad-greens-to-saudi-arabia-with-bather-smart-farm); [Hortidaily](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9802089/when-you-can-grow-locally-you-just-have-a-huge-advantage)
- **Arable** is a Riyadh hydroponic farm that delivers herbs and lettuces to chefs, restaurants and hotels **within 8 hours of harvest**. It has raised about **$2.7M**. [Startup-seeker profile](https://startup-seeker.com/company/arablefoods~com)
- **YesHealth and Mowreq** run a Riyadh vertical farm with nationwide retail supply. **Jana Farm** grows wild rocket, edible flowers, microgreens and herbs (basil, thyme, oregano, rosemary, dill, chives). [Arab News: Saudi growers move to vertical farming](https://arab.news/wh387) (snippet only; arabnews blocked)

### Inferences
- HORECA pays premium prices but wants small daily or every-other-day drops, consistent quality, invoices and often credit terms. A farm 50–90 km out faces a daily delivery cost and time burden; batching 2–3 delivery days a week would be needed.
- Differentiation options are open-field or greenhouse "baladi" herbs that hydroponic players don't emphasize (local mint varieties, jarjeer, regional basil, purslane/رجلة, molokhia), heirloom tomatoes, and edible flowers. Microgreens are the most capital-light high-value crop, but they face direct competition.
- Price lists and payment terms for Riyadh HORECA herb supply were not found publicly.

### Gaps
- No public price data for HORECA herb or microgreen wholesale in Riyadh.
- No data on the typical credit days restaurants and hotels demand from small suppliers.

## 4. Direct-to-consumer and alternative channels (boxes, farmers markets, stalls, agritourism, platforms, startups)

### Takeaway
DTC captures the full retail price and above (eggs at 25–30 SAR/tray direct vs about 18–20 SAR retail average). It needs social-media marketing and delivery logistics. Riyadh has recurring farmers'-market formats (a weekly farmers' day at Murooj Riyadh, now in its 10th year), and MEWA promotes organic and farmers' events. Agritourism is a licensed, growing but tightly gated category: only 15 of 185 rural-farm applicants had opened by Dec 2024. Aggregator and B2B startups (Nabt, Grove, Farmi) are emerging intermediaries. Nana and Sary are general grocery/B2B players with no found farmer-sourcing program.

### Cited Findings
**Farmers markets / events**
- A Sabq headline: "Weekly farmers' day completes its 10th year at 'Murooj Riyadh' with wide attendance" (يوم المزارع الأسبوعي يكمل عامه العاشر في "مروج الرياض"). Only the snippet was seen; the date is unknown. [Sabq](https://sabq.org/article/EkhwjQ1)
- A Bayut blog describes a **weekly Saturday farmers' market at King Abdullah Park, Riyadh, 6 am–8 pm**. The post is undated, so verify it is current. [Bayut blog](https://www.bayut.sa/blog/%D8%A7%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%B4%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9/%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%82-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%B9%D9%8A%D9%86/)
- An Arab News item says a Saudi commission "aims to enhance and develop farmers' markets as platforms that sustain national products". Only the snippet was seen. [Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/2566741/amp)
- **Azka Farmers Market** is monthly, on the second weekend, at Hayy Jameel in **Jeddah** (2026 summer edition 4–6 June). It is free entry, and its "Azka Selection" stall sells produce for farmers who cannot have their own booth. Azka is Riyadh-based and runs the **Azka Basket** delivery service (twice-weekly fresh produce deliveries). One farmer said he had previously sold at markets in Riyadh, Makkah and Taif. [Hayy Jameel listing](https://hayyjameel.org/whats-on/azka-farmers-market); [Arab News (summer 2026)](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2646117/saudi-arabia); [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2596804)
- MEWA publishes an organic-food-day events calendar (يوم الغذاء العضوي), which offers event-based selling slots for organic producers. [MEWA organic calendar PDF](https://mewa.gov.sa/organic/content/روزنامة%20فعاليات%20يوم%20الغذاء%20العضوي%20مفتوح.pdf)
- The Riyadh Region has **47 certified organic farms plus 23 in conversion**. MEWA inspects organic outlets: 21 inspection visits found 6 violations. [Al-Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/ampArticle/1151853) (search summary)

**Direct sales / social / classifieds (eggs as an example)**
- A Riyadh classifieds ad (June 2026) sells "baladi/organic" farm eggs at **30 SAR/tray**, with free Riyadh delivery for 2+ trays. Another ad (Diriyah, undated) asks **25 SAR/tray**. These are single sellers' asking prices. [OpenSooq](https://sa.opensooq.com/ar/search/280907088); [Mstaml](https://www.mstaml.com/en/product/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B6-%D8%AF%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%AC-%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A)
- GASTAT-based averages: the national egg tray price peaked at **19.78 SAR in Feb 2026**, then was 17.60 in July and 17.91 in Aug 2026. [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/ar/article/articledetail/id/1937273) (snippet only)
- Okaz reported local eggs in malls at **18–20 SAR/tray** (date unclear). [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/ampArticle/1561655)

**Agritourism / farm rental**
- MEWA said only **15 rural farms (مزارع ريفية) had opened to visitors out of 185 that applied for licences** (Aleqt, 5 Dec 2024). Visitor-facing farm hospitality needs a licence. [Aleqt 2024-12-05](https://www.aleqt.com/2024/12/05/article_2753814.html)
- The **Al-Ammariyah strawberry destination** (NW Riyadh) combines farming with leisure: rural cafés and rest areas overlooking orchards. Pick-your-own and farm-café models already exist near Riyadh. [Sabq](https://sabq.org/article/rytSxRy) (snippet)
- Rest-house units (استراحات) on agricultural land in Dirab (Riyadh) were listed at **50,000 SAR/yr per unit**. The listing licence expired in July 2026. [Bayut listing](https://www.bayut.sa/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%B1/%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%B5%D9%8A%D9%84-87697509.html)
- Sabq reports that sustainable agritourism is strengthening Saudi rural destinations under Vision 2030. [Sabq](https://sabq.org/article/oQQWkIi) (snippet)

**Startups / platforms**
- **Nana**: an online grocery delivery platform operating since 2016 in 14 cities. It raised an $18M Series B and signed a SAR 22M deal to launch 50 branches. **No farmer-sourcing program was found.** [Sharikat Mubasher](https://sharikatmubasher.com/news/article/21487034/nana-signs-sar-22-mn-deal-to-launch-50-new-branches?lang=en); [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/en/2020/03/wamda-invests-online-grocery-platform-nanas-18-million-series-b-round)
- **Sary**: one of the largest B2B wholesale marketplaces, supplying groceries to retailers. Not a farmer channel per the sources found. **Farmi**: a B2B platform connecting Saudi farmers and SMEs to source and sell local farm products. [Sharikat Mubasher: RetailTech B2B](https://www.sharikatmubasher.com/media-hub/experts-thoughts/16468?lang=en)
- **Nabt** (Riyadh, founded 2022) raised **US$34M** to build physical and digital fresh-produce infrastructure connecting local farms with commercial buyers. [Inc. Arabia](https://en.incarabia.com/nabt-raises-us34-million-to-scale-fresh-produce-infrastructure-across-saudi-arabia-807098.html)
- **Grove** partners directly with farmers through an "Agri-Marketing" service covering sales planning, cold-chain logistics and guaranteed market access, and plans expansion to more Saudi cities. [Sharikat Mubasher](https://sharikatmubasher.com/media-hub/experts-thoughts/20593)
- Rewaa is a retail POS/inventory SaaS, not a produce channel. No source was checked for this; it is noted from general knowledge only, so treat it as unverified.

### Inferences
- **The best fit for a 2,000–10,000 m² farm is a DTC core**: WhatsApp/Instagram/Snapchat orders, weekly "سلة خضار" boxes, eggs, and pre-orders. Neighbourhood drop points in north and west Riyadh would reduce the cost of the 50–90 km trip. Add farmers-market and event stalls for customer acquisition, and HORECA for herbs as a premium B2B line. Use the Aziziyah wholesale market and aggregators (Nabt, Grove, Farmi) only for surplus.
- Eggs illustrate the DTC premium: direct "baladi" eggs at about 25–30 SAR/tray vs about 18–20 SAR retail averages, roughly 40–60% higher, if the producer has a credible baladi/free-range story.
- Agritourism (weekend farm rental, pick-your-own, school visits) could be high-margin. It requires a rural-farm licence (MEWA / tourism) and facilities, and the licensing funnel appears slow (15 of 185). On a leased farm, the lease must permit it.
- A Salla or Zid storefront (low-cost Saudi e-commerce builders) could handle subscription checkout. No farm-specific fee data was gathered.

### Gaps
- No Riyadh farm-box subscription service with published prices was found. Instagram and Snapchat accounts could not be searched directly.
- Booth fees and vendor requirements for Riyadh farmers markets (Murooj, King Abdullah Park, Riyadh Season events) were not found.
- No current info on MEWA-organised farmers markets in Riyadh in 2025–2026. The Arab News commission item could not be opened.

## 5. Livestock selling: Riyadh markets, Eid al-Adha, platforms, slaughter rules

### Takeaway
Livestock sells through Riyadh's livestock markets (Aziziyah and Al-Nadhim are cited) and through a large ecosystem of classified and auction apps. Eid al-Adha is the key price event: 2025 sheep prices in Riyadh ranged about 1,700–2,500 SAR per head. Average Najdi sheep prices hit a record 1,865 SAR in 2025, up from 1,391 SAR in 2021.

### Cited Findings
- Aleqt surveyed the **Aziziyah and Al-Nadhim livestock markets in Riyadh**. Sheep were about **1,700–2,500 SAR** before Eid, despite thousands of imported Naimi sheep from Jordan and Syria. Prices are about 2,000 SAR at the start of Dhul-Hijjah and typically rise toward Arafah day. The page's date display is inconsistent; the content appears to be from 2025. [Aleqt](https://www.aleqt.com/الأخبار/أغنام-مستوردة-من-الأردن-وسورية-تفشل-في-كبح-أسعار-الأضاحي-في-السعودية-10036)
- Average Eid sacrifice prices in KSA set records in 2025, exceeding the 2015 spike:
  - **Najdi**: 1,391 SAR (2021) to **1,865 SAR (2025)**
  - **Harri**: 1,344 to **1,724 SAR**
  - **Sawakni**: 1,082 to **1,286 SAR**
  
  [Aleqt analysis](https://www.aleqt.com/تقارير-وتحليلات/أسعار-3-فئات-أضاح-في-السعودية-تكسر-طفرة-2015-10170)
- Other 2025 markets:
  - **Hafar Al-Batin** (wholesale): imported sheep 1,500–1,900 SAR; baladi Naimi 1,800–2,200 SAR
  - **Qassim**: 1,500–2,400 SAR
  - **Arar**: Najdi up to 2,100 SAR
  
  [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/economy/saudi/2199037); [Slaati 2025-05-31](https://slaati.com/2025/05/31/p2692659.html); [Sabq Arar](https://sabq.org/saudia/bqjt4q7xg4)
- **Online livestock platforms** (app-store descriptions):
  - **Al-Majma'** (المَجْمَع): ads and live auctions with in-app chat
  - **Haraj Abu Jumaa** (حراج أبو جمعة): sheep-focused
  - **Haraj Sahm** (حراج سهم): e-payment and livestock transport by couriers
  - **Haraj Halal**: animal classifieds
  - **Haraj** (general): has a large livestock section
  
  [Haraj Abu Jumaa](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1491418804); [Haraj Aghnam](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1529934928); [Haraj Mazadat](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1597635793); [Al-Mazad Al-Hur](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6447368166)

### Inferences
- For a small farm, the most profitable livestock play is likely **fattening lambs and goats timed to sell 2–4 weeks before Eid al-Adha**. Sell direct to families (Haraj/WhatsApp, or on-farm pickup), with the market as fallback. Prices peak near Arafah.
- Selling live animals is straightforward. Selling meat requires slaughter at a licensed municipal slaughterhouse (مسلخ). Home and roadside slaughter for sale is prohibited under municipal rules. The fines were not sourced in this research.
- Feed costs and imported-sheep supply (Jordan, Syria, Sudan) cap margins outside Eid.

### Gaps
- No source found for Riyadh livestock-market entry fees, dalal commission on sheep sales, or Haraj's current commission policy. A commonly cited "1% Haraj commission" could not be confirmed.
- Slaughterhouse rules and fines (MOMAH / SFDA) were not retrieved.
- The current location and status of Riyadh's main livestock market (سوق الأنعام) after any relocations was not confirmed.

## 6. Competition: large producers, imports, differentiation

### Takeaway
Saudi vegetable supply is largely domestic and increasingly greenhouse-based:
- **Vegetables overall:** about 78% self-sufficiency (MEWA, 2024)
- **Cucumbers:** 101% (GASTAT, 2024)
- **Tomatoes:** 83% (GASTAT, 2024)
- **Eggs:** above 100%

Commodity crops (cucumber, tomato, standard eggs) are therefore saturated by large-scale producers, while hydroponic startups target premium greens. A small farm should differentiate on freshness, baladi/organic credibility, niche herbs and varieties, and personal relationships.

### Cited Findings
- **Vegetables overall (MEWA, 2024):** production exceeded **3.5 million tonnes**, giving **78% self-sufficiency**, with significant greenhouse expansion. [Zawya](https://zawya.com/en/world/middle-east/saudi-arabia-achieves-76-self-sufficiency-in-tomato-production-pkdaopc6); [Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/650125)
- **GASTAT 2024 self-sufficiency by crop:**
  - Cucumbers: **101%**
  - Okra: **102%**
  - Tomatoes: **83%**
  - Onions: **72%**
  
  [Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/650125)
- **Tomatoes (2023):** output exceeded 691,875 t (76% self-sufficiency). Greenhouses produced about 299,581 t and open fields about 392,294 t. [Hortidaily](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9713952/saudi-arabia-reaches-76-tomato-self-sufficiency)
- KSA exceeds **100% self-sufficiency in dairy, dates and eggs**. [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/gcc/saudi-arabia-surpasses-100-self-sufficiency-in-key-food-products-led-by-dairy-dates-and-eggs-mgvb062d)
- The egg and poultry market is seeing **"increased supply widening competition"** (Argaam headline, 2026). [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/ar/article/articledetail/id/1937273)
- **Premium greens incumbents:** Bather/Green Basket, Arable, YesHealth/Mowreq and Jana Farm (see section 3), plus branded hydroponic lettuce on Carrefour KSA shelves. [Carrefour KSA](https://www.carrefourksa.com/mafsau/ar/n/c/clp_fruits-vegetables-ksa)
- **Livestock:** imported sheep from Jordan and Syria flood markets before Eid but did not stop the 2025 price rises. [Aleqt](https://www.aleqt.com/الأخبار/أغنام-مستوردة-من-الأردن-وسورية-تفشل-في-كبح-أسعار-الأضاحي-في-السعودية-10036)
- **Organic niche (Riyadh region):** 47 certified organic farms and 23 in conversion, which shows a small but existing certified competitor set. [Al-Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/ampArticle/1151853)

### Inferences
- **Avoid head-to-head commodity competition** (standard tomatoes, cucumbers, white eggs) with Almarai, Tanmiah, Al-Watania, Astra and large greenhouse firms. National self-sufficiency of 100%+ means low and volatile wholesale prices.
- **Differentiation levers:** same-day-harvest freshness; baladi / free-range eggs; organic certification (achievable, with a small certified peer group); seasonal and baladi varieties; niche Arab and Asian herbs; edible flowers; a farm-visit trust story; Eid lambs.
- Large-company names (Almarai, Astra, Al-Watania, Tanmiah) were given in the brief. Their specific market shares were **not researched or sourced** here.

### Gaps
- No sourced market-share data for Almarai, Astra Farms, Al-Watania or Tanmiah in fresh vegetables or eggs.
- No import-volume data for Riyadh herbs and greens.

## 7. Saudi small-farm examples selling direct in Riyadh, and pricing

### Takeaway
Documented examples are mostly mid-size operators (Azka Basket, Bather/Green Basket, Arable) and individual classified sellers (baladi eggs at 25–30 SAR/tray). No well-documented case study of a 1-hectare Riyadh family farm with published prices was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Azka**: Riyadh-based. It runs the Azka Basket website with twice-weekly fresh produce delivery and curates farmers markets featuring small Saudi farms, including a shared "Azka Selection" stall for farmers without their own booth. [Hayy Jameel](https://hayyjameel.org/whats-on/azka-farmers-market); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2646117/saudi-arabia)
- **Bather Smart Farm**: a family-founded 2,500 m² vertical farm in Riyadh selling direct to individuals and to restaurants and hotels. [Agritecture](https://www.agritecture.com/blog/2023/3/29/bringing-the-freshest-salad-greens-to-saudi-arabia-with-bather-smart-farm)

[OpenSooq](https://sa.opensooq.com/ar/search/280907088); [Mstaml](https://www.mstaml.com/en/product/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B6-%D8%AF%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%AC-%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A)
