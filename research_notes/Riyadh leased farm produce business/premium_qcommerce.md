# Premium / clean produce supply to Riyadh quick-commerce and online grocery (2024–2026)

Method note: research used web-search snippets. Direct fetches of bridgemena.com and arabnews.pk failed because the session's network proxy does not allow those hosts, so those items rest on search snippets. Arabic supplier-program searches ("توريد خضار نعناع نينجا") returned nothing relevant. Market-research sites (actowiz, makreo, sacra, bigcartel) are low-quality aggregators and are flagged as such.

## 1. Who operates q-commerce / dark stores in Riyadh, and how big is the market

### Takeaway
Ninja is now the dominant pure-play: it became a unicorn in July 2025 and runs about 100 dark stores. Nana, the original dark-store pioneer, entered court-supervised financial restructuring in April 2026. That is a direct warning about a supplier's payment risk. HungerStation, Jahez, Keeta, Amazon and Noon also compete in grocery. Market-size estimates are small and inconsistent, at roughly USD 0.4–0.45bn.

### Cited Findings
- Ninja raised about USD 250–254m (reported as SAR 1bn) in July 2025, led by Riyad Capital, at a USD 1.5bn valuation. It is described as a pre-IPO round, with an IPO eyed for 2027 — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1825981); [MEA Tech Watch](https://meatechwatch.com/2025/07/14/saudi-startup-ninja-raises-254-million-pre-ipo-round-valued-at-1-5-billion/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/retail/saudi-arabias-fast-growing-startup-ninja-secures-sar-1-billion-funding-1.500188902)
- Ninja operates about 100 dark stores across 28 cities. One aggregator claims it reached 40+ cities by late 2025, which is unverified — [Sacra (aggregator)](https://sacra.com/c/ninja); [DealStreetAsia](https://media.dealstreetasia.com/stories/ninja-enters-unicorn-club-448181)
- Ninja job ads mention SFDA, SABER and product-registration work, which suggests it imports and private-labels some products — [Workable/ananinja](https://apply.workable.com/ananinja/jobs/view/42B003A4F6.md)
- Nana raised SAR 500m (USD 133m) in a Series C in Feb 2023, led by Kingdom Holding. Jahez is also a shareholder — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/en/2023/02/nana-raises-133-million-series-c-round-led-kingdom-holding)
- On 17 April 2026 the Riyadh Commercial Court opened financial reorganization proceedings for Central Markets Co. (Nana's operator). During the first phase, creditor claims are suspended. Reports at the time said the app was still operating — [Jawlah](https://jawlah.co/en/53814); [Mubasher](https://sharikatmubasher.com/news/article/21545473/nana-enters-financial-restructuring-after-raising-over-200mn?lang=en); [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/business/5264854-after-hundreds-millions-investments-saudi-grocery-app-nana-faces-survival-test)
- In Nov 2025, Arab News reported that Nana and Ninja, plus Amazon and Keeta, were driving Saudi e-grocery growth, and that HungerStation and Jahez had moved into grocery — [Arab News (snippet; fetch blocked)](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2624019)
- Rabbit (from Egypt) entered Riyadh in 2025. It reportedly built dark stores covering half of Riyadh within six weeks — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2603775/amp); [MENAbytes](https://www.menabytes.com/rabbit-expands-saudi/)
- One aggregator calls Noon Minutes a direct 15-minute competitor — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/ninja)
- Market size: estimates are about USD 0.40bn (2025) and USD 448m (2024). These come from low-quality report sites and conflict with each other — [deonde/aggregators via search](https://deonde.co/blog/?p=11239); [PS Market Research](https://www.psmarketresearch.com/market-analysis/saudi-arabia-online-grocery-market-report)

### Inferences
- In practice the buyer universe is Ninja, HungerStation Mart, Jahez/Keeta grocery, Noon Minutes, Rabbit, Carrefour/Tamimi/Danube online, and possibly Nana under its trustee. Ninja is the most important buyer, but relying on Ninja alone creates concentration risk.
- Nana's restructuring shows that unsecured trade creditors can be frozen. A small farm should keep credit exposure to any one app very short.

### Gaps
- No verified dark-store counts were found for HungerStation Mart, Keeta, Jahez, Noon Minutes, Careem Quik, Barakat or Sary in Riyadh.
- Nana's dark-store count is a 2022 target of 150+ or an aggregator claim of "30 in Riyadh"; neither is reliable.
- No reliable 2026 figure was found for the size of the Saudi q-commerce market.

## 2. How the apps source fresh produce: onboarding, terms, margins

### Takeaway
I found no public supplier program, onboarding pack, commission rate or payment term for Ninja, Nana, HungerStation or others. The baseline documents that Saudi buyers commonly ask for are a commercial registration (CR), a VAT certificate and an IBAN, plus SFDA compliance for food.

### Cited Findings
- SALIC's supplier registration (a comparable Saudi corporate buyer) asks for a CR, a VAT certificate and an IBAN — [SALIC](https://www.salic.com/supplier)
- SFDA publishes a guide to registering and licensing fresh fruit and vegetables. I did not get its content — [SFDA guide](https://sfda.gov.sa/en/guide/guide-fresh-fruit-and-vegetable-registration-and-licensing)
- Searches found nothing on consignment vs outright purchase, payment days, listing fees or margins for any Riyadh q-commerce operator — (search results, see above)

### Inferences
- Expect to need a CR, VAT registration (mandatory above the SAR 375k turnover threshold), ZATCA-compliant e-invoicing, barcoded and labelled packs, and SFDA-compliant labelling. These are standard KSA retail requirements; the specifics for each app were not verified.
- Under Saudi bankruptcy law, Nana's restructuring freezes supplier claims. This is the clearest documented evidence that payment risk is real.

### Gaps
- Each app's vendor requirements, commissions and payment terms. These must come from direct contact with the category or procurement teams, and none are documented publicly.

## 3. Premium positioning: price points and existing local brands

### Takeaway
Local "clean" or hydroponic brands already sit on Carrefour KSA shelves: Dava, Al Johara Farm and Saac. Hydroponic lettuce sells for about SAR 6–7 a head and hydroponic cucumbers for about SAR 10 per 800g. Several Riyadh hydroponic or vertical-farm brands exist (Red Sea Farms, Bather, Arable, Green Basket), so a new entrant needs a sharper point of difference.

### Cited Findings
- Carrefour KSA lists "Dava Cucumber Hydroponic Bag 800g" at SAR 9.95, "Al Johara Farm Lollo Bionda Lettuce" at SAR 6.95, and "Saac Lollo Bionda Lettuce 1 pc" at SAR 5.99. These are search snippets and prices change — [Carrefour KSA category](https://www.carrefourksa.com/mafsau/en/c/FKSA1209030)
- Red Sea Farms is sold in 80+ supermarkets (Tamimi, AlRaya, Carrefour). It targets a mid-range price between cheap local produce and imports, and has a 6-hectare retrofit site near Riyadh — [HortiDaily](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9452320/red-sea-farms-produce-now-in-80-ksa-supermarkets-in-back-to-school-launch); [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/articles/red-sea-farms-ploughs-new-ground-as-investments-soar/)
- Bather Smart Farm (Riyadh) runs a 2,500 m² vertical farm growing microgreens, edible flowers, salad greens and herbs (2023) — [Agritecture](https://www.agritecture.com/blog/2023/3/29/bringing-the-freshest-salad-greens-to-saudi-arabia-with-bather-smart-farm); [iFarm](https://ifarm.fi/bather_farm_2_saudi_arabia)
- Arable is a Riyadh agritech founded in 2024 that grows lettuce, kale and herbs, backed by Plug and Play ME — [MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/saudi-agritech-arable-secures-investment-from-plug-and-play-middle-east-to-scale-hydroponic-food-production/)
- Green Basket planned an iFarm vertical farm in Riyadh (2022) — [iFarm](https://ifarm.fi/news/green-basket-to-launch-vertical-farm-with-ifarm-tech-in-riyadh)
- Jana Farm (Mowreq) is a hydroponic farm in Jeddah that sells through supermarkets and online platforms (2023) — via [Arab News search snippet](https://arab.news/wh387)

### Inferences
- A conventional-to-premium price gap exists, but hydroponic lettuce at SAR 6–7 a head is already common. The premium is modest, and established players compete on it.
- Differentiation options include washed, ready-to-eat herb and salad packs, published pesticide-residue test results, and same-day harvest dates.

### Gaps
- No verified prices were found for microgreens, cherry tomatoes, baby cucumbers or free-range eggs on Ninja, Nana or HungerStation.
- I could not confirm "Sadu Farms" or Mowreq's Riyadh retail presence.

## 4. Certifications (Saudi GAP, organic) and whether apps require them

### Takeaway
MEWA launched Saudi GAP in December 2018 and began applying it in phases from 1 January 2019, by farm area. It covers food safety, traceability, environment and worker welfare. MEWA organic certification is annual and goes through licensed certification bodies, with a multi-year conversion period. The state has historically subsidised certification fees. I found no evidence that any app requires either certificate.

### Cited Findings
- Saudi GAP launched in Dec 2018, with phased application to producing units from 1/1/2019 by area. Its scope is food safety and traceability, environment, and worker health — [MEWA](https://www.mewa.gov.sa/ar/MediaCenter/Events/Pages/43265.aspx); [Al-Yaum](https://www.alyaum.com/ampArticle/6065370); [SFDA news](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/en/node/1858)
- Organic: licensed certification bodies inspect and sample, and the certificate is valid for one year. A 2019 MEWA contract had the Saudi Organic Farming Society prepare small farms for certification, and the government paid the certification fees. A SAR 1.6m support program was announced in 2019 — [MEWA 2019](https://mewa.gov.sa:443/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/News-29-10-2019.aspx); [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/603424); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/1479761)
- A secondary source says the organic conversion period "typically lasts a few years" — [TechSci](https://www.techsciresearch.com/report/saudi-arabia-organic-farming-market/8074.html)

### Inferences
- Saudi GAP is the cheaper, faster credibility marker. Organic conversion is unlikely to pay back within the first year on a SAR 100k budget.

### Gaps
- Current fees and timelines for Saudi GAP and organic certification.
- Whether Ninja, HungerStation or others require GAP or residue reports. I found nothing public on this.

## 5. Volume, packing house, cold chain; risks

### Takeaway
I found no sourced figures for minimum daily volumes, packing-house standards or reefer-van costs. The risks the evidence supports are buyer concentration and insolvency (the Nana case), plus competition from funded hydroponic players.

### Cited Findings
- Nana's restructuring suspended creditor claims (see section 1) — [Jawlah](https://jawlah.co/en/53814)
- Red Sea Farms cut prices by more than 30% in a 2022 retail campaign, an example of price pressure in the segment — [FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/article/9452876/red-sea-farms-launches-back-to-school-campaign-in-saudi-arabia)

### Inferences
- Mitigations: diversify across 3–4 buyers and include B2C or restaurant channels; negotiate short payment terms; avoid consignment where possible; and consider supplying through an established distributor first.

### Gaps
- Minimum volumes, rejection rates, cost of a reefer van (buying or renting), and the cost of setting up a packing house in Riyadh. No sources were found; these need supplier quotes.
