## 1. Per-enterprise numbers: capex, running cost, yield, cycle, water, revenue

### Takeaway
Cooled plastic greenhouses are much cheaper used (about 9.5–11.5k SAR per 9×40 m house on Haraj in Al-Kharj) than consultancy studies suggest. Feed inputs for livestock are well documented: barley costs about 65–83 SAR per 50 kg bag and layer feed about 92–104 SAR per 50 kg (Nov 2026). Sale-side prices are the weakest data. I found no reliable Saudi wholesale prices per kg for tomato, cucumber or leafy greens, no price for young lambs (طلي), and no per-hive honey yield.

**B. Open-field winter vegetables and leafy greens/herbs**
- The best sowing window for coriander in KSA is **October–November**. Arugula, coriander and parsley are listed as Saudi winter crops, with the cool season starting around Sept–Oct — [altalebagri.com](https://altalebagri.com/ar/blog/%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%B2%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D8%B2%D8%A8%D8%B1%D8%A9/a-548072779); [saudiseeds.com](https://saudiseeds.com/blog/winter-crops-saudi-arabia/a-129102542); [saudiseeds planting dates](https://saudiseeds.com/blog/vegetable-seed-planting-dates-saudi-arabia/a-23524655)
- **Coriander:** first cut **~35 days after sowing**, then **4–5 cuts per season** — [altalebagri.com](https://altalebagri.com/ar/blog/%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%B2%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D8%B2%D8%A8%D8%B1%D8%A9/a-548072779) (snippet)
- **Arugula:** first cut **~6 weeks** after sowing, then every 4 weeks, giving **3–4 cuts** in winter. Seed rate is ~4 kg/feddan in rows or 8 kg broadcast (Egyptian guidance) — [agriceg.com](https://agriceg.com/watercress-cultivation-method/)
- **Parsley:** ~**5 cuts** of **4–4.5 t/feddan per cut**, i.e. **20–22.5 t/feddan** per season (Egyptian source; 1 feddan ≈ 4,200 m²). A lower estimate is **14–20 t/feddan**. Seed rate is **2.5–3.5 kg/dunum** — [v-alue.com](https://v-alue.com/%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A9-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%89-%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D8%B2%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%86%D8%B3/) (snippet; Egyptian conditions)
- I found no Saudi price for a bunch (حزمة) of greens at Al-Aziziyah or Riyadh wholesale markets. The only price found was Egyptian.

**E. Sheep/goat fattening (تسمين خرفان)**
- **Barley (50 kg bag):** GASTAT average **67.9 SAR (Feb 2026)** and **69.1 SAR (May 2026)** for imported barley — [GASTAT Feb 2026 PDF](https://www.stats.gov.sa/documents/d/guest/average-prices-of-goods-and-services-february-2026-ar-en-3-pdf); [GASTAT May 2026 PDF](https://www.stats.gov.sa/documents/20117/2435267/Average_Prices_of_Goods_and_Services_May_2026-AR-EN.pdf_fixed_15261219/afb8ebe5-3191-7b7a-ea1c-fd8bf55e3f7e?t=1781498501944) (snippet values). The Sanabil Al-Jouf indicator on 17 Nov 2026 shows **64.64–82.80 SAR** per 50 kg depending on city and brand — [Sanabil Al-Jouf](https://sanabilaljouf.com.sa/ar/price-indicator). Importers on the "Istirad" platform quoted **~59.5 SAR/bag** (trader claim) — [Al-Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1075450). During past shortages the price spiked to **120 SAR/bag** in southern and western regions (undated, older) — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/ar/article/articledetail/id/840085)
- **Alfalfa (برسيم):** GASTAT average **30.0 SAR/bale (Feb 2026)** and **28.8 SAR/bale (May 2026)**, bale weight not stated. Alfalfa cubes are **~50–51 SAR per 40 kg** (Sanabil, Nov 2026) — same GASTAT PDFs and [Sanabil Al-Jouf](https://sanabilaljouf.com.sa/ar/price-indicator)
- **Adult sheep prices (GASTAT national averages, April 2025):** Naimi **1,895 SAR**, Najdi **1,864**, Harri **1,740**, Sawakni **1,276**, local billy goat (jadha') **1,185**, Barbari **747** — [@SaudiNews50 citing GASTAT](https://x.com/SaudiNews50/status/1928422462968025312?lang=en). Around Eid al-Adha the ranges were Harri **1,750–2,600**, Naimi **2,600–2,800** and Najdi **~2,400 SAR** — [Al Riyadh newspaper](https://www.alriyadh.com/2189693). Seasonal swings are large.
- **Fattening cycle: 90–120 days** on a barley-heavy ration with free-choice straw. An example 1-tonne mix is **400 kg barley, 200 kg crushed maize, 200 kg wheat bran** — [small-projects.org](https://small-projects.org/%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D8%AA%D8%B3%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%AE%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%81/) (snippet)
- A Saudi example (2025) assumes a **finished weight of 50 kg**, a **live price of 25–30 SAR/kg**, and a **profit of 1,375 SAR/head** — [small-projects.org](https://small-projects.org/%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D8%AA%D8%B3%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%AE%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%81/) (snippet). **Treat this as implausible:** 50 kg × 25–30 SAR is only 1,250–1,500 SAR of revenue, so a 1,375 SAR profit would require near-zero lamb and feed cost.
- **Daily gain:** lambs on barley programmes gain **120–200 g/day**, versus **>240 g/day** in intensive fattening on concentrate pellets. Awassi lambs gained **278 vs 146 g/day** on commercial concentrate vs barley (Palestine) — [search summary of journals.iau.ir / Najah Univ.](https://staff.najah.edu/media/sites/default/files/Comparison_and_evaluation_of_Awassi_lambs_fattening_systems_in_Palestine.pdf)
- A King Saud University trial on Naimi lambs found that **up to 50% of the barley can be replaced by crushed date pits plus an enzyme cocktail** without hurting growth and at similar economic efficiency — relevant to a farm that already has palms — [AGRIS/FAO record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/67f3947d6872bc21d4cb6d7b) (identified via search; values not shown)
- Mortality: a 2026 fattening guide (Syria) recommends budgeting **3–5% of the flock as a mortality reserve** and locking in a 90-day barley price — [osoolix.com](https://www.osoolix.com/ar/blog/livestock/sheep-calf-fattening-feasibility-syria-2026)

**F. Beekeeping/honey; date palms**
- A swarm of local bees (طرد نحل بلدي) costs **up to 500 SAR**, and boxes, masks, smokers and foundation wax are also expensive (Nahal association, Riyadh) — [Al-Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1158145)
- MEWA (May 2025): **25,644 licensed beekeepers**, about **5,832 t from ~1 million hives** — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/local/na/2233657). May 2026: **>7,786 t/year from >2 million hives** — [Sabq](https://sabq.org/article/odEqCt7). Imported honey holds a large share of the market — [Al-Eqtisadiah](https://www.aleqt.com/الأسواق/العسل-المستورد-يستحوذ-على-725-من-السوق-السعودية-12226)
- **Dates (Khalas, Al-Kharj):** **8–15 SAR/kg** at the Al-Kharj date market at the start of the season (July 2024) — [Sabq](https://sabq.org/article/9Kyd2k1). Retail Khalas Al-Kharj is **~14 SAR/kg**, or **115 SAR per 8×1 kg carton** — [shuyukhcoffee.com](https://shuyukhcoffee.com/ar/%D9%83%D8%B1%D8%AA%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%AA%D9%85%D8%B1-%D8%AE%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B5-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B1%D8%AC-8*1000%D8%AC); [kaifalshiuokh.com](https://www.kaifalshiuokh.com/%D8%AA%D9%85%D8%B1-%D8%AE%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B5-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B1%D8%AC-%D9%81%D8%A7%D8%AE%D8%B1)

**G. Water and salinity**
- **About 69.4% of Al-Kharj wells are slightly to moderately saline; the rest are severely saline.** They remain usable for irrigation only with restrictions. In a Saudi greenhouse tomato trial, medium- and high-salinity water reduced photosynthesis, stomatal conductance and chlorophyll; for medium-salinity water the authors recommend **60% deficit irrigation** to improve water productivity — [Agronomy 2023, 13(12):3016 (MDPI)](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/13/12/3016)
- Tomato water footprint in existing Saudi greenhouses is **~350 L/kg** — [SALIC PDF](https://www.salic.com/storage/media/717/Hydroponics-vs--Conventional-Farming.pdf)
- Most Saudi greenhouses are still soil-grown, which **builds up salinity and soil-borne disease** (Estidamah) — [estidamah.gov.sa research](https://www.estidamah.gov.sa/EN/RESEARCHES)
- Average summer temperature is **45°C**, and scarce, saline irrigation water limits productivity (Wageningen UR) — [HortiDaily/WUR](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9130779/saudi-arabia-growing-fruits-and-vegetables-at-an-average-summer-temperature-of-45degc/)

### Inferences
- **Greenhouse arithmetic (my calculation, not sourced):** a 9×40 m house is 360 m² (9×30 m is 270 m²).
  - Cucumber at 10–15 kg/m² (Estidamah Riyadh trial) gives roughly **3.6–5.4 t per house per crop**.
  - Tomato at 15–25 t/1,000 m² gives roughly **5.4–9 t per house**.
  - Each **1 SAR/kg** of farm-gate price is therefore worth about **3,600–5,400 SAR per cucumber crop per house**. Price is the missing variable (see Gaps).
- **Greenhouse cooling cost (my calculation):** pad (15–30 SAR/m²) plus fans (10–25 SAR/m²) on 270–360 m² comes to roughly **6,750–19,800 SAR** for cooling alone if bought new, before structure, cover and irrigation. Buying used cooled houses at ~9.5–11.5k (Haraj, Al-Kharj) looks like the main way to fit 2–3 houses inside a 100k budget. Dismantling, transport and re-erection are extra and unquantified.
- **Greenhouse water (my calculation):** 30 kg/m²/yr × 350 L/kg ≈ **10.5 m³/m²/yr**, i.e. about **3,800 m³/yr for one 360 m² tomato house**, plus pad-cooling evaporation in summer. On a weak or saline well, summer pad cooling plus irrigation is the binding constraint. Cucumber and tomato are only moderately salt-tolerant (general agronomy, not sourced here), so 1–2 houses is a realistic ceiling.
- **Feed cost per kg (my calculation):** barley ≈ **1.29–1.66 SAR/kg** (Nov 2026 Sanabil); 1.36–1.38 SAR/kg (GASTAT Feb/May 2026). Layer feed ≈ **1.84–2.08 SAR/kg**.
- **Sheep margin:** I found no sourced lamb purchase price. Adult averages are 1,740–1,895 SAR (Apr 2025) and Eid peaks reach 2,400–2,800. The fattening margin is mostly a **timing trade**: buy lighter lambs off-season, sell before Eid al-Adha or Ramadan.
  - The 1,375 SAR/head profit figure should be discarded.
  - With 120–200 g/day on barley over 90–120 days, a lamb adds roughly **11–24 kg** (my calculation from the sourced gains).
- **Eggs:** the baladi egg price (1–1.5 SAR/egg; 23–57 SAR/tray retail) is 2–4× the commercial tray (14–23 SAR, with promotions far lower). The baladi premium is the whole business case, and it depends on direct-to-consumer marketing in Riyadh.
- **Leafy greens:** coriander and arugula pay out within **5–6 weeks of sowing** and then every ~4 weeks, for 3–5 cuts between Oct and Apr. This is the fastest cash flow of any option, with the lowest capex. Value depends on bunch prices I could not source.
- **Dates:** if the leased farm has Khalas palms, 8–15 SAR/kg wholesale is real but seasonal (once a year, Jul–Sep). Date pits can partly replace barley in lamb rations (KSU), which favours combining palms with a small fattening batch.
- **Honey:** the national average works out to roughly **~4–6 kg per hive per year** (my calculation: 5,832 t / ~1M hives, and 7,786 t / >2M hives). That is low, and importers compete hard on price. Honey looks like a poor fit for a 100k, cash-flow-focused plan unless the bees are moved to forage seasonally (سدر/سمر).

### Gaps

The search only returned Egyptian market data. The GASTAT monthly price PDFs (stats.gov.sa) likely contain retail averages but could not be opened (network block).

## 2. Which 2–3 enterprises give the best return and fastest cash flow for ~100k SAR?

### Takeaway
Based on the evidence, the strongest options for ~100k SAR (of which 20–35k is rent) are:
1. **Winter open-field leafy greens and herbs**: lowest capex, first cash in about 5–6 weeks.
2. **One or two used cooled greenhouses for cucumber**: a proven Riyadh-region yield of 10–15 kg/m² per crop, with houses available used for ~10k each.
3. **A small, time-to-market sheep fattening batch** (90–120 days, sold before Eid or Ramadan) **or a baladi laying flock sold direct**.

None of these can be confirmed as profitable without the missing sale prices.

### Cited Findings
- Coriander first cut at ~35 days, then 4–5 cuts; arugula first cut at ~6 weeks, then every 4 weeks — [altalebagri.com](https://altalebagri.com/ar/blog/%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%B2%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D8%B2%D8%A8%D8%B1%D8%A9/a-548072779); [agriceg.com](https://agriceg.com/watercress-cultivation-method/)
- Used cooled 9×40 greenhouses at 9,500–11,500 SAR in Al-Kharj — [Haraj](https://haraj.com.sa/search/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%88%D8%AA%20%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%87%20%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B9/&gsessionid=TzVrFkpwrKnD2f8i5kuFmDphGpOHB9y9S4hJCXEHiBd7sNQviSkqkQ/)

- Mushroom margin claim: cost 5–10 vs price 20–50 SAR/kg — [Warif](https://warifksa.com/%D9%87%D9%84-%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D8%B2%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D8%B7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%B1%D8%A8%D8%AD%D8%9F-%D9%86%D8%B8%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%B4%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%84%D8%A9/)

### Inferences (illustrative budgets, my construction; not sourced except where an input is cited above)
- **Rent alone takes 20–35% of the 100k**, leaving about 65–80k for capex plus working capital. That rules out new climate-controlled greenhouses (85k+ per unit per consultancies) and serious hydroponics.
- **Illustrative split A ("greens + greenhouse"):**
  - Rent 25–35k
  - 2 used cooled 9×40 houses ~20–23k, plus relocation, drip and electrical (unquantified, so reserve ~10k)
  - Open-field herbs and greens on the remaining land: seed, fertiliser, drip lines (unquantified, small)
  - Working capital and reserve ~15–20k
  - Cash flow from greens starts within ~6 weeks of an Oct sowing; cucumber starts in ~6–8 weeks (cycle length not sourced here).
- **Illustrative split B ("livestock + greens"):**
  - Rent
  - Shade pens and troughs (unquantified)
  - A batch of lambs timed to finish before Eid al-Adha or Ramadan
  - Barley at ~65–83 SAR/50 kg; alfalfa at ~29–30 SAR/bale
  - Herbs on the land in winter
  - This option uses far less water than a greenhouse. Profit is very sensitive to the buy/sell spread and to barley-price spikes (as high as 120 SAR/bag historically).
- **Baladi eggs** suit a farmer who can sell direct (subscription trays in Riyadh at 25–50 SAR). Licensing is now simplified via the rural-poultry licence on Naama. Summer heat stress on birds is a risk (see Q4).
- **Lower priority:**
  - Beekeeping: low national yields and import competition.
  - Hydroponics/NFT: high capex, no Saudi cost data.
  - Dates: only worthwhile if palms already exist; one harvest a year.
  - Mushrooms: a plausible indoor add-on, but needs a cooled room in summer, and the margin claims come from supplier marketing.

### Gaps
- Without Saudi sale prices for greens and greenhouse vegetables, and without lamb buy prices, the ranking is qualitative. Key next step: collect 2–3 weeks of Al-Aziziyah wholesale quotes and lamb market prices in Al-Kharj and Riyadh.

## 4. Common failure reasons for small farms in central Saudi Arabia

### Takeaway
The documented failure drivers are:
- **saline groundwater** (most Al-Kharj wells are at least slightly saline);
- **extreme summer heat** (45°C average summer);
- **salinity and soil-borne disease build-up** in soil-grown greenhouses;
- **feed-price volatility** for livestock;
- **price competition** from large producers (eggs, tomato, cucumber, imported honey).

### Cited Findings
- 69.4% of Al-Kharj wells are slightly to moderately saline; the rest are severely saline. Salinity reduces tomato photosynthesis and chlorophyll — [Agronomy 2023 (MDPI)](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/13/12/3016)
- Average summer temperature is 45°C, which stresses crops; scarce, saline water limits productivity — [HortiDaily/WUR](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9130779/saudi-arabia-growing-fruits-and-vegetables-at-an-average-summer-temperature-of-45degc/)
- Soil-grown greenhouses accumulate salinity and soil-borne disease — [Estidamah](https://www.estidamah.gov.sa/EN/RESEARCHES)
- Groundwater abstraction (>18 bn m³/yr) far exceeds recharge (<1 bn m³/yr) — [Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/2646967/saudi-arabia)
- Barley shortages have pushed bags as high as 120 SAR. Distributors are accused of deliberately "drying up" (تعطيش) the market — [Argaam 840085](https://www.argaam.com/ar/article/articledetail/id/840085); [Argaam 762061](https://www.argaam.com/ar/article/articledetail/id/762061). Barley prices swing with global markets and geopolitics — [Al-Eqtisadiah](https://www.aleqt.com/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AE%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1/%D8%AA%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A8%D8%B2%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%B9%D9%8A%D8%B1-%D8%A5%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-20-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%B6%D8%B9%D9%81-%D9%85%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B9-1895)
- Commercial egg trays fluctuate (14→23 SAR; promotions as low as 5.99 SAR, unverified); KSA is 100% self-sufficient in eggs — [X: Sout Al-Jazeera](https://x.com/soutaljazeera/status/1997627644452503927); [X post](https://x.com/ii3snd/status/2122259786800492706)
- Imported honey dominates the market, and cheap "Sidr" listings online call for caution — [Al-Eqtisadiah](https://www.aleqt.com/الأسواق/العسل-المستورد-يستحوذ-على-725-من-السوق-السعودية-12226)
- Tomato and cucumber are 83% of greenhouse output, a crowded segment — [Al-Eqtisadiah 2014](https://www.aleqt.com/2014/04/30/article_845157.html)

### Inferences
- **Test well water (EC/TDS) before signing the lease.** Water salinity largely decides which crops are possible. Leafy greens like parsley and coriander and cucumbers under deficit irrigation may still fail on severely saline water (no thresholds sourced).
- **Plan the calendar around summer.** Open-field production is effectively Oct–Apr. Cooled greenhouses in Jun–Aug need large volumes of pad water and electricity. Many small farms likely lose money trying to crop through summer (inference from the heat and water findings).
- **Marketing risk dominates for undifferentiated products.** Commercial eggs, tomato and cucumber compete against large producers; baladi eggs, fresh herbs and Eid-timed lambs have more pricing power if sold direct.
- Livestock operations should hedge feed (bulk purchase at cycle start) and keep a 3–5% mortality reserve.

### Gaps
- No Saudi survey or study quantifying small-farm failure rates or causes was found. Pest-specific issues (e.g., whitefly or tomato leafminer in greenhouses, red palm weevil in dates) were not researched within the tool budget.
- No source on heat-stress mortality for poultry or sheep in central KSA summers.
