# Low-water and salt-tolerant forage crops and cultivation methods in Saudi Arabia

Context: KSA banned green fodder cultivation (effective 5 Nov 2018). MEWA said green fodder used about 17 billion m3/yr of water and expected to save about 9 billion m3 [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/568056). Output was projected to fall 65%, with about 1.1 Mt of hay to be imported [agroberichtenbuitenland](https://www.agroberichtenbuitenland.nl/actueel/nieuws/2018/12/03/ksa-fodder-ban); [TradeArabia](https://www.tradearabia.com/news/IND_296265.html). Almarai bought farmland in California and Arizona to grow hay abroad [NBC/Reuters](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/saudi-land-purchases-fuel-debate-over-u-s-water-rights-n546591). Note: the ban's scope and exemptions for small farms were not verified here.

## Which crops give the most feed per m3 under Saudi heat and salinity?

### Takeaway
The best KSA and Gulf evidence favours perennial C4 grasses (buffel grass, blue panic) and CAM spineless cactus over alfalfa. Buffel grass yields about 14 t DM/ha on 400-500 mm, against alfalfa's about 17 t/ha on 2,500-3,000 mm, which is roughly 4-5x more DM per m3. Halophytes (Atriplex) suit brackish water and marginal land, but their quality is lower.

### Cited Findings
- **Buffel grass (Cenchrus ciliaris), KSA (Ghorbel, Alghamdi et al., Agronomy 15(2):371, Jan 2025):** This was the first systematic evaluation of buffel grass in Saudi Arabia, using accessions from northern KSA (Aja, Jameen, Zaitoun, Gaed, Industrial zone). Yields were 9.3-18.4 t/ha (mean about 14.1) on an estimated 400-500 mm/yr of water. The 5-year alfalfa average was about 17 t/ha on 2,500-3,000 mm/yr. The Jameen accession was the most productive (18.4 t/ha) and the most resistant to overgrazing. Aja was the most salt tolerant. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/15/2/371); [AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/67dac0b9677d8be0233bae1c)
- **Buffel grass WUE, UAE (closest analogue):** DM was 14.6-15.1 t/ha, with WUE of 0.7-0.8 kg DM/m3, the highest among the grasses tested, including Rhodes grass. — [IGC 2023](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/igc/XXV_IGC_2023/Ecology/60)
- **KSU evaluated 24 local buffel grass seed sources near Riyadh (2003).** Details were not retrieved. — [KSU repository](https://direct.ksu.edu.sa/bitstreams/a2cea811-4618-4633-bec6-5b1a72bcbb8f/download)
- **Blue panic (Panicum antidotale), Saudi study (Bokhari et al., 1988):** It tolerated salinity of 15,000 ppm and drought, and used almost 50% less water than alfalfa. Yield was 150-180 t green matter/ha/yr with 15-18% crude protein. — [AGJSR](https://agjsr.agu.edu.bh/publications/paper/1097)
- **Blue panic is more salt-resistant than buffel grass and Dichanthium on seed and forage yield.** — [Feedipedia](https://feedipedia.review.fao.org/node/413)
- **KAU project on blue panic:** It planned saline irrigation treatments at 1,000, 5,000 and 10,000 mg/L plus sulfur. No results were found. — [KAU](https://ceoalm.kau.edu.sa/Files/305/Researches/57455_27668.docx)
- **Spineless cactus (Opuntia ficus-indica var. inermis), ICARDA Arabian Peninsula program:** 38 accessions were introduced to Oman in 2015 and used as a nursery for KSA, Qatar, UAE and Yemen. The KSA trials tested frost tolerance in the northern region. An irrigation comparison of 2,500 vs 5,000 m3/ha/yr showed no significant difference in annual yield, which suggests about 250 mm/yr is enough. Qatar fruit yield averaged 11 kg/plant. — [CGIAR MEL](https://repo.mel.cgiar.org/items/35a07403-d2fe-4753-8028-47dbbb14900f) (full text not retrieved; KSA yield numbers missing)
- **Atriplex, KFU:** A. halimus and Suaeda vermiculata gave higher fodder yields than Artemisia herba-alba under all regimes. Drip irrigation at 2,000 m3/ha/yr gave the best yield and quality. — [KFU Scientific Journal](https://services.kfu.edu.sa/ScientificJournal/en/Home/ContentsDetails/128)
- **Atriplex nutritive value:** Six Atriplex species grown in KSA had 16.7-25.2% CP (DM basis) (Khalil et al.). A 1989 study fed A. halimus to Najdi sheep in northern KSA (Bhattacharya, J. Range Mgmt). A 1990 KFU thesis studied salinity and water volume effects on Atriplex. — [Univ. Arizona JRM](https://journals.uair.arizona.edu/index.php/jrm/article/download/7953/7565); [SDL](https://drepo.sdl.edu.sa/items/24bf0f80-9ff0-4b45-868a-259cbcb8ef8d)
- **Atriplex elsewhere:** A. nummularia irrigated with brine gave 6.5 t DM/ha at 14.5% CP (Brazil). In Australia, sheep productivity was unaffected by saltbush salinity up to 0.8%. — [Embrapa](https://www.alice.cnptia.embrapa.br/alice/bitstream/doc/151924/1/OPB124.pdf); [CSIRO](https://publish.csiro.au/EA/EA9750760)
- **Pearl millet:** ICBA (Dubai) screened about 1,000 lines, and about 20 grew well at one-third seawater salinity. ICBA says pearl millet is the most promising forage for dairy. — [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/uae/search-for-crops-to-make-uae-s-desert-bloom-1.583173)
- **Pearl millet and sorghum, other trials:** Sultan Qaboos University (Oman) evaluated 30 salt-tolerant pearl millet genotypes under saline irrigation [ASA 2008](https://scisoc.confex.com/crops/2008am/webprogram/Paper42172.html). In Egypt (Sinai), sorghum and pearl millet were irrigated with 4,000 ppm water, and mulch raised yields [EKB](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_45348.html).
- **Quinoa, KSA (Estidamah):** 21 varieties were screened and 7 were adapted to heat, salinity and limited water. Under drip on moderately saline soil, the best grain yields were Golden Afternoon at about 4.3 t/ha and Mint Vanilla at about 4.0 t/ha. — [Estidamah](https://estidamah.gov.sa/en/media-center/news/Pages/تعزيزاً-للأمن-الغذائي-استدامة-تنجح-في-ادخال-7-اصناف-من-الكينوا-تتوائم-مع-ظروف-المملكة-الجافة--.aspx)
- **Moringa:** KAU ran a project on M. peregrina and M. oleifera forage in the western region (Medina-Wadjah). Livestock grazed the trees heavily, and no yields were reported. — [KAU](https://cegmr.kau.edu.sa/Files/305/Researches/57453_27666.docx). A non-KSA trial found that 50,000 plants/ha with 28-day cuts gave the best yield and protein. — [AgEcon](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/353745)

### Inferences
- Approximate feed per m3 of water: buffel grass about 2.8-3.5 kg DM/m3 by the KSA paper's own numbers (14 t / 4,000-5,000 m3), or a conservative 0.7-0.8 kg/m3 from the UAE trial; alfalfa about 0.6-0.7 kg/m3 (17 t / 25,000-30,000 m3). The two buffel figures differ a lot because the water-supply estimates differ, so the conservative value is safer for planning.
- Cactus at about 2,500 m3/ha is plausibly the most water-thrifty option, but its DM content is low (about 10%). It needs a fibre and protein complement and frost management in Al-Jouf, Hail and the north.
- Ranking for salinity tolerance: Atriplex/halophytes > blue panic > pearl millet > buffel grass > sorghum > alfalfa. This is inferred from the sources above.

### Gaps
- No KSA field data were found for Sesbania, Acacia or Prosopis browse yields, or for sorghum/pearl millet forage under saline water. The search did not reach them.
- KSA cactus yields (t/ha) were missing because the CGIAR full text could not be fetched.
- No per-hectare establishment costs for KSA were found for any of these alternative crops.

## What KSA university trials (KSU, KFU, Qassim Univ) exist?

### Takeaway
KSU's Precision Agriculture Research Chair (PARC) has the strongest irrigation and forage engineering work (Rhodes grass deficit irrigation, subsurface drip). KFU has halophyte and Atriplex work in Al-Ahsa. KAU (Hada Al-Sham) has subsurface drip trials on alfalfa and blue panic. No Qassim University forage trials were located.

### Cited Findings
- **KSU, Rhodes grass under center pivot (Pak. J. Agric. Sci., 2016):** A 50 ha field compared 100/80/60/40% ETc across five N rates. 80% ETc gave the highest hay yields in cuts 1 and 2, and 60% ETc in cut 3. The best cumulative yields were 24.7 and 27.8 t/ha in the two management zones at 80% ETc with high N. The authors recommend 80% ETc with variable-rate N fertigation. — [KSU PARC PDF](https://parc.ksu.edu.sa/sites/parc.ksu.edu.sa/files/imce_images/response_of_rhodes_grass_to_variable_rate_application_of_irrigation_water_and_fertilizer_nitrogen_1.pdf); [ECU](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworkspost2013/2276)
- **KSU, conventional vs modified subsurface drip (Abass, Al-Ghobari, Tola, Al-Gaadi, 2016):** This study looked at soil-water distribution. — [KSU PARC](https://parc.ksu.edu.sa/sites/parc.ksu.edu.sa/files/imce_images/impact_of_conventional_and_modified_subsurface_drip_irrigation_system_on_water_distribution_through_soil_profile._1.pdf)
- **KAU, Ismail, El-Nakhlawy and Basahi (2015):** Alfalfa and blue panic were grown under sprinkler, surface drip and subsurface drip. Subsurface drip gave the highest yield at 100% water requirement and the best WUE, and sprinkler was the worst. — [AUN listing](https://b.aun.edu.eg/agriculture/maximizing-productivity-and-water-use-efficiency-alfalfa-under-precise-sub-surface-drip-irrigation)
- **KAU, Ismail and Almarshadi (2013, Irrigation and Drainage), deficit subsurface drip on alfalfa:** Irrigating at 85% field capacity saved 13% of water for a 12% yield loss. Irrigating at 70% field capacity saved 27% of water for a 21.7% yield loss. — [AUN](https://b.aun.edu.eg/agriculture/effect-fully-and-limited-irrigation-forage-production-and-irrigation-water-use-alfalfa-medicago)
- **Al-Hassa, subsurface drip on date palm:** WUE was 1.32 vs 0.96 kg/m3 for surface drip, a 27% gain. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2022.1038163/pdf)
- **KFU:** Atriplex, Suaeda and Artemisia were compared under three irrigation regimes, and there is a 1990 Atriplex salinity thesis. — [KFU](https://services.kfu.edu.sa/ScientificJournal/en/Home/ContentsDetails/128)
- **NCVC rangeland rehabilitation:** The target is 350,000 ha by 2030 under the Saudi Green Initiative. Over two years, more than 51,000 ha were restored (58 sites plus 15 valleys) using 109 t of seed from 40 species. The 2026 target is 60,000 ha using more than 110 t of seed from 70 species. — [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/saudi-arabia/saudi-arabia-to-rehabilitate-350000-hectares-of-pasture-3000343). A further 80 t of native seed is planned for 33 sites aligned with cloud seeding — [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/culture-society/saudi-arabia-to-rehabilitate-33-sites-under-cloud-seeding-initiative). NCVC holds a Guinness record for the largest seasonal seed store (95.6 t, Al-Jouf, Oct 2025) — [Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/778528-largest-seasonal-seed-storage-collection)
- **Solar pumping:** KFUPM estimated 2-3 US cents/m3 from a 50 m well (2012) [KFUPM](https://pure.kfupm.edu.sa/en/publications/economical-feasibility-of-utilizing-photovoltaics-for-water-pumpi/). Over 30 years, solar's life-cycle cost was about half that of diesel pumping (2018) [KFUPM](https://pure.kfupm.edu.sa/en/publications/techno-economic-study-of-a-solar-photovoltaic-and-diesel-powered-/). A 684 kW First Solar plant at Al Watania (Al-Jouf) replaces about 628,000 L/yr of diesel [Technical Review ME](https://technicalreviewmiddleeast.com/power-a-water/power-generation/new-solar-plant-to-power-irrigation-in-saudi).

### Inferences
- Deficit irrigation at 80-85% of requirement is the KSA-validated sweet spot. It gives roughly proportional or better water savings for a small yield loss, and Rhodes grass hay actually peaked at 80% ETc.

### Gaps
- No Qassim University forage or irrigation trials were found. They likely exist (Qassim is a major alfalfa area) but were not reached.
- No KSA trial data were found on the nutritive value of buffel grass or cactus.
- The KSA trial and cost evidence above includes no Sesbania, Acacia or Prosopis results.

## What's a practical crop mix for a Saudi farm?

### Takeaway
Use a perennial C4 grass base (buffel grass or blue panic) on deficit drip or subsurface drip, plus spineless cactus as a water-thrifty energy and water source. Use summer pearl millet or sorghum as cut-and-carry forage, Atriplex on saline or brackish blocks, and a limited protein source such as subsurface-drip alfalfa or imported hay. Run pumps on solar.

### Cited Findings
- The supporting yields, water use and salinity data are cited in the sections above. Region-specific evidence is limited to: northern KSA buffel grass accessions [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/15/2/371), frost testing of cactus in the north [CGIAR](https://repo.mel.cgiar.org/items/35a07403-d2fe-4753-8028-47dbbb14900f), Atriplex work at KFU in Al-Ahsa [KFU](https://services.kfu.edu.sa/ScientificJournal/en/Home/ContentsDetails/128), and solar pumping in Al-Jouf [TRME](https://technicalreviewmiddleeast.com/power-a-water/power-generation/new-solar-plant-to-power-irrigation-in-saudi).

### Inferences (not directly sourced; agronomic judgement)
- **Jazan** (hot, humid, frost-free, monsoonal rain): cactus, moringa, Sesbania, sorghum and pearl millet, and buffel grass all suit it.
- **Al-Jouf, Hail, Tabuk** (cold winters with frost): buffel grass (northern accessions such as Jameen), Atriplex and summer millet or sorghum. For cactus, choose frost-tolerant accessions and expect damage. These regions have strong solar resources.
- **Qassim and Riyadh** (fossil groundwater, center pivots): deficit-irrigated Rhodes grass and alfalfa at 80% ETc, with a move to subsurface drip; blue panic and buffel grass on marginal blocks.
- **Eastern Province and Al-Ahsa** (saline soils and water, sabkha): Atriplex, Suaeda, blue panic and salt-tolerant pearl millet.
- **Illustrative 100 ha mix:** 40% buffel grass or blue panic, 20% summer pearl millet or sorghum, 15% cactus, 15% Atriplex on saline blocks, and 10% alfalfa on subsurface drip for protein.

### Gaps
- No costed whole-farm model specific to KSA was found.
- No KSA data on feeding trials or animal performance were found for buffel grass, cactus or millet diets.
