## What are the biggest feed cost/supply pain points for Saudi farmers now?

### Takeaway
Saudi livestock feed depends heavily on imports. Barley imports roughly doubled to about 4.2-4.4 MMT in MY2025/26 and are forecast at about 4.5 MMT in 2026/27. Corn imports are forecast at about 4.66 MMT, and alfalfa hay imports run at about 0.95 MMT a year, about 60% of it from the US. This leaves breeders exposed to world prices, freight and FX. Most feed import subsidies have been replaced by per-head payments to small herders.

### Cited Findings
**Demand base (GASTAT 2024)**
- 2024 herd: more than 22 million sheep (+0.25% y/y), about 7.3 million goats (-1%), more than 2.24 million camels (+1%) — [GASTAT via search, Livestock Statistics](https://stats.gov.sa/en/node/171118)
- 2024 output: broiler production 1.3 million t (+12.9%); table eggs more than 8.4 billion (+6.4%); raw milk about 2.7 billion L (-3.5%) from about 233k dairy cows in specialised projects — [GASTAT](https://stats.gov.sa/en/w/news/119)
- The GASTAT 2023 release gives inconsistent sheep/goat numbers (29.4M sheep / 22M goats vs. 7.4M goats), so treat it with caution — [GASTAT 2023](https://stats.gov.sa/en/w/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%87%D9%8A%D8%A6%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%85%D8%A9-%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A5%D8%AD%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%B4%D8%B1-%D9%86%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%AC-%D9%86%D8%B4%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A5%D8%AD%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%A1%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84-1)

**Imports**
- USDA GAIN (Apr 2026, SA2026-0003): barley imports for MY2026/27 forecast at 4.5 MMT (+2%). Corn is forecast at 4.66 MMT and wheat at 3.5 MMT. Drivers are rebuilding strategic reserves, opportunistic buying when barley is cheaper than corn in rations, and new feed mill capacity — [USDA FAS GAIN Apr 2026](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/gain/2026/04/saudi-arabia-grain-and-feed-annual)
- Barley went from 1.9 MMT (2024/25) to about 4.2 MMT (2025/26). The Dec 2025 semi-annual report had projected 4 MMT, and one trade outlet cites 4.6 MMT for 2026/27, so the sources conflict — [World-Grain](https://www.world-grain.com/articles/22195-saudi-arabia-to-import-more-barley-corn); [USDA semi-annual](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/saudi-arabia-grain-and-feed-semi-annual-1)
- Modern Mills reportedly plans a 422,000 t/yr feed plant, expected to start in Q4 2026 (private market study, lower reliability) — [MarkNtel](https://www.marknteladvisors.com/research-library/gcc-barley-market-study.html)
- Alfalfa: 2023 imports were about 950k t worth about USD 459.6M, with the US supplying 59.4% — [Tridge](https://www.tridge.com/intelligences/alfalfa/SA/import). The Saudi alfalfa hay market was about USD 420M in 2025, and global hay prices fell from USD 288/t (Apr 2023) to USD 165/t (Nov 2024) — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/saudi-arabia-alfalfa-market) (market-research grade)
- After the ban, dairy farms were expected to import up to 1.1 million t of fodder, mostly alfalfa — [TradeArabia](https://www.tradearabia.com/news/IND_296265.html)
- FAO GIEWS (2026) forecasts above-average cereal import requirements for 2026/27 — [FAO GIEWS](https://www.fao.org/giews/countrybrief/country.jsp?code=SA)

**Subsidy shift**
- Barley import subsidy history: cut from USD 200 to 80/t in 2009 — [ThePoultrySite](https://www.thepoultrysite.com/news/2009/02/saudi-arabia-reduces-import-subsidies-on-feed). A later overhaul eliminated import subsidies for most feed ingredients, sharply cut the barley subsidy and moved to per-head payments to small producers — [USDA Barley Trade Quarterly](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/saudi-arabia-barley-trade-quarterly-9)
- Per-head small-herder subsidy (about 2022 data): USD 320M/yr budget, paying monthly USD 2.13 per sheep or goat, 10.67 per camel and 16 per cattle head, capped at 300 head per category — [millingmea](https://millingmea.com/?p=2027472)
- ARASCO priced its "Wafi" compound feed at least SAR 8 per 50 kg bag below barley (about 2022) — [millingmea](https://millingmea.com/?p=2027472)
- Local wheat procurement price for 2025/26 is SAR 1,750/t (USD 467) — [millingmea](https://millingmea.com/?p=2027472)
- SALIC is preparing to take over wheat purchasing as grain import policy is restructured — [Commodity Board](https://commodity-board.com/saudi-arabia-reshapes-grain-import-and-food-security-policy-as-salic-prepares-to-take-over-wheat-purchasing)

### Inferences
- The main pain points are price volatility of imported barley, corn and alfalfa (USD-denominated), freight and geopolitical risk, and the loss of broad import subsidies. Small sheep and goat herders get only per-head support, and the 300-head cap leaves mid-size operators out.
- Barley at about 4.5 MMT plus corn at about 4.7 MMT means more than 9 MMT of imported feed grain a year. Any local substitute (hydroponic fodder, seasonal fodder, by-products) would compete against that volume.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025-2026 SAR/ton prices for barley, alfalfa or Rhodes grass were found. Local dealer or GASTAT CPI data is needed.
- No current official barley subsidy or sale price from GFSA was found (search returned Jordanian data, which was excluded).
- No soybean meal volumes were captured.

## What incentives, loans (ADF) or programs support new feed production methods?

### Takeaway
The ADF is the main lender, with a record SAR 7.17B approved in 2024, heavy poultry exposure and specific feed-import financing. Reef supports rural livestock smallholders. SALIC secures upstream supply through overseas assets, most notably majority control of Olam Agri, completed in April 2026. No verified ADF product specific to hydroponic fodder was found.

### Cited Findings
- ADF approved a record SAR 7.17B and disbursed SAR 5.54B in 2024. Cumulative lending since founding reached SAR 72B by the end of 2024 — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/economy/na/2201017)
- ADF poultry lending: more than 1,575 loans totalling SAR 10.4B by Q1 2025, of which broilers took 1,096 loans (SAR 5.24B) and layers 334 loans (SAR 1.98B) — [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/business/saudi-poultry-sector-attracts-687m-in-deals-at-riyadh-expo-3003233)
- 2025 ADF board approvals: more than SAR 2.7B at one meeting and SAR 473M at another — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1821684); [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1841839)
- In 2023 the ADF signed SAR 926M of contracts in feed, animal production and dairy to finance imports of maize, soy and barley — [Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/2334716/)
- The ADF allocated SAR 100M to finance fodder imports (undated) — [EFG Hermes One](https://www.efghermesone.com/News/Index/agricultural-development-fund-allocates-sar100mn-for-fodder-imports)
- A SAR 1.5B ADF financing window supports water-efficient modern feed technologies (market report claim, unverified) — [Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/saudi-arabia-alfalfa-market)
- SIDF and ADF have a joint portfolio for feed mills. SIDF lent SR22M to Nal Foods for a Tabuk feed mill (2018) — [Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/1252346/)
- Poultry: 29 agreements worth SAR 5B were signed at the Middle East Poultry Expo in April 2025 — [Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/ksa/2025/04/17/sar-5-bn-agreements-hatched-at-middle-east-poultry-exhibition/)
- Reef Saudi (launched January 2019 with MEWA and FAO) covers 8 sectors including livestock. It reports more than 87k-90k beneficiaries, a market value above SAR 5.6B and 1,100% growth in rainfed crop participation (13,300 beneficiaries). These figures are self-reported — [Riyadh Daily](https://www.alriyadhdaily.com/article/3f80811f84de45189d0321d6300136dd); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2627681/)
- SALIC completed majority control of Olam Agri (feed, grains, oilseeds) in April 2026, with a portfolio target of 80.01% — [Milling MEA](https://millingmea.com/olam-agri-posts-record-2025-results-as-salic-ownership-marks-new-chapter/); [SALIC](https://www.salic.com/our-portfolio). Earlier steps: Minerva stake (2015) and a search for Sudan fodder land (2015, statement of intent only) — [farmlandgrab](https://farmlandgrab.org/post/25630)

### Inferences
- Funding flows mainly to poultry, dairy and feed-import finance. Novel low-water fodder (hydroponic, sprouted barley, halophytes) fits the policy on water but has no confirmed dedicated loan line, so applicants would likely go through general ADF or SIDF windows.

## What new rules govern fodder cultivation (regions, water, plot limits)?

### Takeaway
The 2015 cabinet decision phased out green fodder, with the ban in force since November 2018. Alfalfa and Rhodes grass are banned as perennial high-water crops. A December 2023 cabinet decision allows companies and large farmers to grow wheat and seasonal fodder under MEWA controls. MEWA now issues licences, including winter fodder seed licences in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- The ban took effect November 2018. Green fodder used about 17 bcm of water a year, and the ban was expected to save about 9 bcm — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/568056); [TradeArabia](https://www.tradearabia.com/news/IND_296265.html)
- The implementing mechanism bans alfalfa and Rhodes grass (and lists sorghum and corn as high-water crops), plus any other species MEWA adds. It sets three farm tiers: up to 50 ha, 50-100 ha and over 100 ha. Farms up to 50 ha could take compensation of SAR 4,000/ha (capped at SAR 200k over 5 years) or grow wheat on up to 50 ha. Farms over 100 ha may grow forage only with modern irrigation, on crops and areas MEWA specifies. Fodder grown abroad for export to the Kingdom is encouraged — [Qanoniah](https://qanoniah.com/en/File/Jy4DGw9da7vj1vg78Qv65RrKX-Mechanism-for-Implementing-Regulations-on-Halting-the-Cultivation-of-Green-Fodder)
- Perennial fodder is being phased out within 3 years in favour of seasonal fodder, capped at 50 ha — [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/local/na/2232883)
- Fine of SAR 4,000/ha with aerial surveillance. Violations include unlicensed fodder farms, unregistered farms and licensed farms exceeding their licensed area — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/612910)
- December 2023 cabinet decision: companies and large farmers may grow wheat and seasonal fodder under MEWA controls — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1692596)
- 2025: Al-Jouf Agricultural (licensed from 29 May 2025) and TADCO received two-year MEWA licences to produce winter fodder seed — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1818699); [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1817657)
- SFDA regulates feed safety and registration — [SFDA](https://SFDA.GOV.SA/sites/default/files/2021-08/SFDAfeed465E.pdf)

### Inferences
- The policy favours low-water, seasonal, licensed and modern-irrigated forage, plus imports and overseas production. Technologies that cut water per ton of fodder fit the official direction.

- The date and current status of the Qanoniah mechanism are unconfirmed.
- The status of the per-head subsidy in 2026 is unconfirmed.
