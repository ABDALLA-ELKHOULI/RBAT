# Water-Saving Methods for Berseem (Trifolium alexandrinum) and Alfalfa in Arid MENA / Egypt

Note: Egyptian Knowledge Bank (journals.ekb.eg) full texts could not be fetched (DNS failure), so many figures below come from abstracts/search snippets. Verify against the originals before quoting precise numbers.

## Baseline water use of berseem in Egypt vs. savings from each method

### Takeaway
Surface-irrigated berseem in the Nile Delta uses roughly 38-60 cm consumptive use (about 1,600-2,500 m3/feddan), with around 2,200-2,600 m3/fed applied in clay-soil trials. Practical surface-irrigation tweaks (cutoff irrigation, dry sowing, fewer irrigations between cuts, ETc-based scheduling) save about 4-25% of water with small yield penalties. Drip/subsurface drip and smart scheduling for alfalfa save about 30-36%.

### Cited Findings
**Baseline**
- A 1981 UNDP/JICA master plan puts seasonal berseem consumptive use in Lower Egypt at about 56 cm, with 9-11 irrigations in Upper/Middle Egypt — [JICA report](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/11705621_17.pdf)
- Sakha (El-Bably 2002): consumptive use was 59.62, 48.98 and 37.98 cm for three, two and one irrigations between cuts. More irrigations raised yield but lowered WUE — [JSSAE article 51928](https://jssae.journals.ekb.eg/article_51928.html)
- Two irrigations per cut gave higher applied water than one in both the 2008/09 and 2009/10 Sakha seasons. A control flooded to 5 cm depth gave the highest applied water — [JSSAE 51928 PDF](https://jssae.journals.ekb.eg/article_51928_b8171bd1b30e2dd189cd30b9f74aa619.pdf)

**Cutoff (strip-length) irrigation, North Nile Delta clay, 2010-2012**
- Seasonal applied water ran from 2,615 m3/fed (control) down to 2,215 m3/fed for the shortest cutoff, a saving of 4.1-15.3%. The best water productivity was about 16.58 kg (fresh) per m3, at 90% strip length — [Response of Egyptian Clover to Cutoff Irrigation](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_3155.html); [PDF](https://asejaiqjsae.journals.ekb.eg/article_3155_2b3150036a4506bc9a03c6040dda7cff.pdf)

**ETc-based / deficit scheduling**
- In a wheat/berseem rotation study, irrigating at 1.2 ETc and 0.70 ETc raised berseem water productivity by 12.40% and 25.16% over farmer practice — [JSSAE 55100](https://jssae.journals.ekb.eg/article_55100_521cbf84c96ec4c8bc7896c2ae98c986.pdf)
- Bed-furrow irrigation gave higher fresh and dry berseem yields than farmer practice at Gemmeiza. Higher P rates also raised water productivity — [search snippet, EKB](https://fjard.journals.ekb.eg/article_194691_1f9356ad8572c559731e00799679e374.pdf)

**Dry vs. wet (herati) sowing**
- Dry cultivation saved irrigation water with about a 3-6% dry-yield reduction. Scaled to roughly 2.5 million feddans, the authors estimate 700-840 million m3/yr saved nationally — [MoA Egypt PDF](https://moa.gov.eg/media/ezzhbcr5/1-2.pdf) (attribution from search snippet; confirm the exact source)

**Alfalfa: drip, subsurface drip (SDI), smart irrigation**
- Saudi Arabia: SDI and surface drip saved 35.7% and 29.2% of water vs. sprinkler — [Assiut Univ. summary](https://b.aun.edu.eg/agriculture/maximizing-productivity-and-water-use-efficiency-alfalfa-under-precise-sub-surface-drip-irrigation)
- Saudi Arabia (Ismail & Almarshadi 2013): irrigating at 85% and 70% of field capacity cut alfalfa yield by 12% and 21.7% but raised IWUE — [same source](https://b.aun.edu.eg/agriculture/maximizing-productivity-and-water-use-efficiency-alfalfa-under-precise-sub-surface-drip-irrigation)
- Al-Farafra Oasis, New Valley (2022/23-2023/24): smart irrigation with leaky pipes plus the highest soil-amendment rate gave the highest alfalfa WUE, about 3.04-3.09 kg/m3. Manual sprinkler irrigation without amendment ranked lowest — [Al-Azhar J. Agric. Eng. 436686](https://azeng.journals.ekb.eg/article_436686.html)
- Kansas (non-MENA reference): alfalfa yield was unaffected across 70-100% ETc, averaging 20.8 Mg/ha — cited in the [search results](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/529271/1/N529271PP.pdf) (source not verified)

### Inferences
- Moving berseem from 1-2 m3/fed flood practice to cutoff plus dry sowing plus ETc scheduling plausibly saves 15-25% with less than 6% yield loss. That is the cheapest option, since it needs only management.
- Drip/SDI suits alfalfa (perennial, so the capital cost is amortized) better than annual berseem, which is usually broadcast in the Delta.

### Gaps
- No Egyptian field trial was found with berseem under drip or SDI, and no Egypt-specific alfalfa SDI water-productivity data turned up.
- No full-text figures in m3/fed for alfalfa baseline water use in the New Lands.

## Yield penalties and costs

### Takeaway
Yield penalties are small for surface-management fixes (3-6% for dry sowing, near zero for cutoff) and moderate for 70-85% deficit irrigation in alfalfa (12-22%). Salinity is the largest penalty: berseem is relatively salt-sensitive. Cost data for Egypt were essentially not found.

### Cited Findings
- Dry sowing: about 3-6% dry-yield penalty — [MoA PDF](https://moa.gov.eg/media/ezzhbcr5/1-2.pdf)
- Deficit irrigation at 85% and 70% of field capacity: 12% and 21.7% alfalfa yield loss — [AUN](https://b.aun.edu.eg/agriculture/maximizing-productivity-and-water-use-efficiency-alfalfa-under-precise-sub-surface-drip-irrigation)
- **Saline/brackish water:** At Hisar, India, saline drainage water (EC 3.6-7.4 dS/m) cut Egyptian clover yield by 68% vs. canal water. Losses for the other crops were rye grass 36%, oat 42% and Persian clover 54%, and yield declined linearly with salt applied. Berseem is therefore a poor candidate for saline water; barley, oat or ryegrass are better — [Experimental Agriculture (Cambridge)](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/experimental-agriculture/article/yield-responses-of-winter-rabi-forage-crops-to-irrigation-with-saline-drainage-water/C42921A07660E693B892359C0DC1B378)
- Berseem salt sensitivity varies by growth stage, tested at 0.62-12 dS/m (Pakistan) — [Pak J Bot](https://pakbs.org/pjbot/paper_details.php?id=7824)
- ISSR markers separate salt-tolerant, moderate and sensitive Egyptian clover genotypes, which indicates breeding potential — [EKB JACB 38681](https://jacb.journals.ekb.eg/article_38681_1b22a2b93a8cb04128dcc5940a1c26af.pdf)
- **Treated wastewater:** In a Greek field study (2009), treated urban wastewater was suitable for berseem and corn, with no nutrient toxicity. Wastewater plus fertilizer gave the highest yields, and tap water without fertilizer the lowest — [AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65de30770f3e94b9e5cb9c75). Saudi alfalfa productivity was not adversely affected by TWW (2020) — [search result, Saudi index](https://sindex.sdl.edu.sa/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Changes-in-Yield-and-Forage-Quality/9950291708331). In arid-region barley, TWW raised forage yield 39.4% vs. groundwater — [PMC8871893](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8871893/)
- **Hydrogel:** An Egyptian 2024 Scientific Reports study found that 0.3% hydrogel with 85% of the irrigation requirement gave the highest WUE without significant yield loss. This was not alfalfa or berseem — [Sci Rep 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41598-024-57786-5)
- **Humic acid (biostimulant):** For alfalfa on calcareous soil under water stress in Egypt (Seiam & Allam 2020), WUE rose with higher humic acid rates — [EAJBS 141242](https://eajbsh.journals.ekb.eg/article_141242.html)
- **Mycorrhiza:** AM symbiosis mitigated water-stress effects on berseem in semi-arid Sicily (PLOS One 2014) — [PMC3940947](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3940947). An Iranian thesis on mycorrhiza plus superabsorbent in berseem under water stress reported improved performance — [IUT thesis](https://thesis.iut.ac.ir/effects-mycorrhiza-and-super-absorbent-growth-characteristics-berseem-clover-under-different)
- **PGPR:** Egyptian drought-tolerant Bacillus isolates were tested on wheat, not berseem (2024) — [Sustainability 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jsusta/v16y2024i11p4605-d1404439.html). Egyptian hydrogel plus Glomus work exists for pepper at 70% ET — [Damanhour J. 2024](https://jaesj.journals.ekb.eg/article_410194_bbec270c34e537c6987dacb11a49b476.pdf)

### Inferences
- Treated wastewater is a strong fit for fodder legumes, provided it is low in salinity. Brackish water above about 3-4 dS/m should go to barley, ryegrass or alfalfa rather than berseem.
- Hydrogel and mycorrhiza evidence for berseem specifically is thin and mostly from outside Egypt.

### Gaps
- No Egyptian cost data (EGP/feddan) were found for drip/SDI, hydrogel or biostimulants on fodder.
- No berseem salinity threshold (ECe at which yield starts to decline) was retrieved from FAO tables.
- No mulching studies on berseem or alfalfa in MENA were found. Mulching is unusual for broadcast forage.

## Recent (2020-2026) trials and new varieties

### Takeaway
Recent Egyptian work centres on alfalfa in the New Lands (Farafra smart irrigation, North Sinai drip varieties) and humic acid. No ARC/FCRI release explicitly marketed as drought-tolerant berseem was found. Helaly remains the top-yielding standard cultivar.

### Cited Findings
- Helaly gave the highest yields at Sakha (mean of sowing dates: 54.83 t/fed fresh, 8.17 t/fed dry) and showed somewhat less stability than Gemmeiza 1 — [EKB JPP 85575](https://jpp.journals.ekb.eg/article_85575.html); [EKB 46975](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_46975.html)
- Other ARC cultivars in trials include Sakha 4, Gemmeiza 1, Serw 1 and Giza 6. Sakha 4 had a dodder tolerance index of 70-73% vs. 26-33% for Serw 1 — [EJARC 211135](https://ejarc.journals.ekb.eg/article_211135.html)
- North Sinai (Bir El-Abd, 2020-2021): alfalfa varieties were evaluated under drip irrigation — [AUN](https://aun.edu.eg/agriculture/node/38074)
- Al-Farafra smart-irrigation alfalfa trial (2022-2024), described in section 1 — [azeng 436686](https://azeng.journals.ekb.eg/article_436686.html)
- **Planting date:** The recommended berseem sowing window is early to mid-October. Sakha trials sowed on 16-22 October with dry broadcasting — [JSSAE 51928](https://jssae.journals.ekb.eg/article_51928.html)

### Inferences
- Sowing in early October uses the cooler, lower-ET period. Ending with fewer late cuts (stopping before the April-May high-ET period) is a likely water lever, but it was not quantified in the sources found.

### Gaps
- No 2020-2026 ARC drought- or salt-tolerant berseem variety release was confirmed. FCRI forage-crops announcements were not reachable.
- No ICARDA or FAO berseem-specific water-saving data were retrieved within the search budget.
