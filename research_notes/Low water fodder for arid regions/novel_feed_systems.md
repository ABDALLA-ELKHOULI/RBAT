# Soilless and Novel Low-Water Livestock Feed Systems (Arid MENA focus)

Note on method: several primary sources (MDPI, Feedipedia, Iowa State, Agritecture) could not be fetched directly (DNS block in sandbox), so figures below come from search-result extracts of those pages. Before citing a figure in the final report, check it against the source.

## Is hydroponic fodder actually economical once DM is accounted for?

### Takeaway
Usually not. Hydroponic barley sprouts save 90-98% of water per kg of fresh fodder, but sprouting loses 7-47% of the seed's dry matter. The output is only about 12-25% DM, and the seed plus energy and capex push the cost per kg DM to several times the cost of hay or grain. A Saudi peer-reviewed study found it loss-making for small farms. It makes sense mainly where water is the binding constraint, imported hay is very expensive, or a subsidy or service model absorbs the capex.

### Cited Findings
- Sprouting causes a 7-47% loss in DM from the original seed, because starch is respired for energy. — [NJAP review](https://njap.org.ng/index.php/njap/article/view/6075); [Agritecture](https://www.agritecture.com/blog/hydroponic-fodder)
- Saudi Arabia, Sustainability 2023 (hydroponic vs field barley green fodder): WUE was much higher for hydroponics. However, lower DM content plus higher fixed and variable costs led to economic loss, and the authors judged it not suitable for small-scale farming. — [Sustainability 15(1):822](https://dx.doi.org/10.3390/su15010822); [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jsusta/v15y2023i1p822-d1023094.html)
- Water use: about 1.5-2 L per kg of green fodder hydroponically, vs. about 73 L for barley and 85 L for alfalfa in the field (per kg fresh, as cited by Feedipedia). Vendor claims go as low as 1-1.5 L/kg. — [Feedipedia Broadening Horizons #48 (Bakshi, Wadhwa, Makkar)](https://www.feedipedia.org/sites/default/files/public/BH_048_hydroponic_fodder.pdf)
- A 2026 systematic review of 92 studies reports 90-98% water savings vs. conventional fodder. (I only saw a summary, not its economic findings.) — [Springer 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44279-026-00652-x)
- Hydroponic barley DM is about 12-15% in one source and 18-25% in a UNDP (Rwanda) report. — [UNDP hydroponics cost-benefit 2025](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2025-10/hydroponics_cost_benefit_analysis_final_report_3_1.pdf)
- Iowa State extension: about US$0.40/lb DM (about $880/t DM) for hydroponic fodder vs. about $0.11/lb DM for good hay. Reviews are "very inconsistent" on DM or animal-performance benefits. — [Iowa State Animal Industry Report](https://www.iastatedigitalpress.com/air/article/id/6470/)
- Utah State (2025): total cost per kg of hydroponic barley fodder was about 13.4 times that of conventionally grown barley. — [USU grad report](https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1080&context=gradreports2023)
- Feedipedia critical assessment: on a per-kg-DM basis, hydroponic fodder is less competitive than traditional fodder. — [Feedipedia](https://www.feedipedia.org/content/hydroponic-fodder-production-critical-assessment)
- A vendor (Hydrogrow) claims DM cost is 30-40% below imported alfalfa. This is unsupported marketing. — [Hydrogrow](https://hydrogrow.net/innovative-hydroponic-barley-fodder-revolutionizing-efficient-livestock-farming/)
- Egyptian engineering study (sprout production): seed density of 0.5 cm with fogger irrigation for 30 s every 2 h gave the best output. — [Misr J. Ag. Eng.](https://mjae.journals.ekb.eg/article_98600.html)

### Inferences
- The core accounting problem is that 1 kg of seed gives 5-7 kg of fresh sprouts but less than 1 kg of DM. The farmer is effectively buying grain and paying extra to lose part of it, in exchange for palatability and fresh green feed. The water saved per kg fresh weight is real, but per kg DM the advantage is smaller, and the embedded water of the purchased seed is usually ignored.
- In Egypt, berseem is a cheap irrigated winter forage, so hydroponics is unlikely to compete on DM cost. It is more plausible in Gulf states that import Rhodes grass or alfalfa hay at high prices.

### Gaps
- No Egypt-specific cost per kg DM for hydroponics vs. berseem was found. Local EGP prices for seed, electricity and berseem are needed.
- The full numbers of the Saudi 2023 study (SAR/kg, capex) could not be fetched.

## Which novel feeds are proven at farm scale in arid regions?

### Takeaway
Crop-residue upgrading is the most proven and lowest-water option at farm scale in MENA. This covers urea or molasses-treated rice-straw silage in Egypt, urea-treated palm leaves, and date-waste multi-nutrient blocks in the Maghreb and Gulf. Azolla, duckweed and spirulina have good protein profiles but are mostly at trial stage in the region. BSF larvae are at early commercial scale (UAE, Egypt). Fog harvesting is proven for household water, not fodder.

### Cited Findings
**Crop residues / by-products**
- Egypt (Alexandria Univ.): ensiling rice straw with urea greatly increases feeding value and lowers the cost per tonne of TDN. It is recommended for small farms. — [FAO, A.M. Nour](https://www.fao.org/4/x5487e/x5487e0b.htm)
- Buffalo calves fed chopped rice-straw silage with urea and molasses at 40% of the ration (plus concentrate) reached a marketing weight of 518 kg vs. 384 kg. The authors note that 92% of Egyptian farmers are smallholders holding 88% of livestock, and they argue for training. Straw burning is a key driver. — [AgEcon Search](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/244970?ln=en)
- Adoption caveat (Vietnam): treated straw improved intake and growth, but farmers dropped it after the demonstration because of inconvenience and small herd size. — [LRRD 16(7)](https://lrrd.org/lrrd16/7/trac16052.htm)
- UAE (AAAID): palm leaves treated with 4-6% urea were an adequate fibre source for lambs, as an alternative to expensive Rhodes grass. — [AAAID](https://www.aaaid.org/?p=3654)
- Algeria: multi-nutrient blocks (70% date rejects, 12.5% pedicels, 7.5% urea) gave about 0.92 UFL/kg DM and about 87 g PDIN/kg. Goats accepted them better than sheep, and in a dairy-goat trial milk per gram consumed was about double that of barley. — [Univ. Ouargla](https://dspace.univ-ouargla.dz/jspui/handle/123456789/4195)
- Feed blocks keep for 2-3 years when properly made. — [CGIAR/ICARDA flyer](https://mel.cgiar.org/reporting/download/hash/jxyAthJ9)
- Date pedicels are used like straw: about 34% crude fibre. — [Feedipedia](https://feedipedia.review.fao.org/node/7358)

**Azolla / duckweed**
- Duckweed yields 10-30 t DM/ha/yr with up to 43% CP in managed systems (Leng et al. 1995). Pigs use it with slightly less efficiency than soybean meal, and there is little ruminant work. In ducks, replacing more than 20% of soy protein cut growth. — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/AC151E/AC151E08.htm); [LRRD 9(1)](https://lrrd.org/lrrd9/1/anh91.htm)
- Azolla CP is about 25-35% DM. An Egyptian NRC paper evaluates Azolla pinnata for ruminants. Egypt lacks feasibility studies, for example in South Sinai. Limits are short shelf life and invasiveness. — [NRC Egypt (Bull. NRC)](https://bnrc.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s42269-022-00752-w); [PMC review 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12559161); [Egyptian study](https://asejaiqjsae.journals.ekb.eg/article_330091.html)
- Anecdotal: a farmer in Idlib, Syria feeds dried azolla to poultry. — [Gulf News/AFP](https://gulfnews.com/photos/news/in-pictures-struggling-syrian-farmer-turns-to-tropical-fern-for-fodder-1.1608546252235)

**Spirulina / SCP**
- Egyptian calf trial: Spirulina as an additive gave a similar total feed cost but a significantly lower feed cost per kg of gain. Its role is as an additive, not a bulk feed. — [J. Anim. Poult. Prod. (Mansoura)](https://jappmu.journals.ekb.eg/article_40308.html)
- Global spirulina output is about 56,000 t, mostly in China and Asia-Pacific. — [Adv. Vet. Res.](https://www.advetresearch.com/index.php/AVR/article/view/1531)

**Fog / solar**
- Dar Si Hmad, Morocco: 600 m² of nets, up to about 65 L/m²/day at peak, solar-powered distribution, supplying households. Water scarcity had forced livestock sales, but no fodder use was reported. — [UNFCCC](https://unfccc.int/node/10018)

### Inferences
- At typical fog yields, fodder irrigation is impractical. Fog harvesting fits livestock drinking water at best.
- For arid MENA, the strongest "zero added water" lever is upgrading residues that already exist: rice straw in Egypt, and date waste and palm fronds in the Gulf and Maghreb.

### Gaps
- No water-use-per-kg-DM data for azolla or duckweed in arid climates were found. Pond evaporation in desert heat is likely large but was not quantified.
- No spirulina feed price per kg for MENA was found.
- No direct ICARDA date-palm block programme was confirmed.

## Recent startups/commercial systems 2020-2026?

### Takeaway
The Gulf has several hydroponic fodder operators (Al Aliyo in Sharjah, World of Farming in Abu Dhabi, GrazeIT in Dubai) and UAE BSF waste-to-feed (Circa Biotech). Egypt has BSF ventures (Proteina Organics, EGYMAG). Most evidence is press releases, with no audited economics.

### Cited Findings
- Al Aliyo Hydrofarms (Hamriyah Free Zone, Sharjah) runs a US$2.4M facility producing about 10 t/day of hydroponic barley, corn, wheat and alfalfa fodder. — [GCC Business News](https://www.gccbusinessnews.com/uae-based-al-aliyo-opens-2-4mn-hydroponic-fodder-farms-in-hamriyah-free-zone/); [Gulf News advertorial](https://gulfnews.com/uae/al-aliyo-hydrofarms-promotes-sustainable-farming-1.1669970033649)
- World of Farming (Abu Dhabi, a Hatch & Boost Ventures venture) builds on-farm hydroponic fodder units. Producers it targets import 80-90% of their feed. — [Vertical Farm Daily](https://www.verticalfarmdaily.com/article/9450467/desert-nation-turns-to-hydroponics-to-make-feed-for-its-livestock/)
- GrazeIT (Dubai) uses a design-finance-build-operate service model for hydroponic fodder. — [Vertical Farm Daily](https://verticalfarmdaily.com/article/9444860/hydroponic-fodder-is-more-nutritious-than-conventional-fodder)
- Circa Biotech (Masdar City) turns food waste into feed and fertilizer using BSF. It signed an MoU with the UAE climate ministry in 2022. A pilot with ADAFSA and Spinneys cut landfilled food waste by 54% and then expanded to all Spinneys stores in Abu Dhabi. — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/b35ypbl-circa-biotech-upcycles-organic-waste-into-animal)
- Egypt: Proteina Organics aims to build Egypt's first large-scale BSF larvae facility, with EU backing. EGYMAG Biotechnology was seeking US$6M. — [Aquafeed](https://aquafeed.com/regions/africa/egyptian-company-leads-the-way-in-insect-based-protein-production-with-eu-backing); [WE4F](https://we4f.org/?p=12920)
- The Middle East & Africa BSF market is projected at about US$47.9M by 2033 (20.2% CAGR). This is a commercial market report, so treat it with caution. — [Meticulous Research](https://www.meticulousresearch.com/pressrelease/883/middle-east-and-africa-black-soldier-fly-market-2033)

### Inferences
- Gulf operators lean on a service or offtake model, which shifts capex off farmers. This is consistent with hydroponics being viable only at scale or with outside financing.
- BSF fits monogastrics and aquaculture (protein meal) better than ruminant forage, and depends on securing a reliable organic waste stream.

### Gaps
- No Saudi BSF startup was found.
- No public cost per kg data were found for any of these companies.
- The current operating status of the 2022-era ventures is unverified.
- No link between Almarai and hydroponic fodder was found.
