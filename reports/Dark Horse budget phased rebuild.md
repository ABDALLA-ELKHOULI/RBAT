# Budget-build the Dark Horse, but bid like the structure costs full price

## ملخص

بناء السيارة بميزانية محدودة ممكن، لكنه لا يجعلها صفقة رخيصة. قطع الـ GT والإيكوبوست تركب على معظم الجزء الخلفي: الربع الخلفي، وغطاء الشنطة، والأضواء الخلفية، والجسر الخلفي (السب فريم)، ودفرنس Torsen إذا كانت النسبة مطابقة، وفرامل البيرفورمنس باك، والمساعدات MagneRide. أما الصدام الخلفي السفلي والدفيوزر والجناح ومبرّد الدفرنس فهي خاصة بالدارك هورس. والقطع الصينية تنفع للكماليات فقط، ولا يوجد منها صاج هيكلي للجيل S650. وفي الرياض محل قطع تجارية (alm2000.com) يبيع صدام خلفي بشكل الدارك هورس بـ 1,100 ريال، واسطب شكل الوكالة بـ 1,100 ريال للجهة (2,200 للزوج)، والشراء من محل محلي يجنّبك حجز الشحنة في الجمارك بسبب شهادة سابر. الجودة والتركيب على الدارك هورس غير مؤكدين، فجرّبها قبل الدفع. التكلفة الكلية للإصلاح على ست مراحل، مع قطع مستعملة وشغل يدوي من المالك، تقدير بين **70,000 و185,000 ريال**، والرقم الواقعي للتخطيط حوالي **95,000 إلى 110,000 ريال**. الشغل اليدوي يوفّر 4,000 إلى 10,000 ريال فقط، لأن أغلب المبلغ يذهب على إصلاح الشاصي، وهذا شغل محترفين. **أقصى مزايدة تبقى حوالي 35,000 ريال**، والهدف المريح 30,000 ريال أو أقل، قبل ضريبة 15%. السبب أن قرار الاستمرار أو التوقف لا يتخذ إلا بعد الشراء. السيارة المتبرعة يجب أن تكون GT كوبيه مضروبة من الأمام، وتُشترى من السعودية أو الإمارات، أو تُفكّك في أمريكا وتُشحن قطعاً. استيراد سيارة حوادث كاملة إلى المملكة ممنوع. فورد لا تعتمد تركيب "قصّة" خلفية (clip)، والمرور يعتبر تعديل الهيكل بدون الإجراءات الرسمية مخالفة. لذلك يلزم أخذ **إذن إصلاح من أبشر** قبل البدء، وموقف مكتوب من المرور.

---

A budget rebuild of this car is possible. It saves real money on the bolt-on half of the job and almost nothing on the half that matters. Mustang GT parts carry over to most of the rear of the Dark Horse. Used US assemblies are cheap: a rear subframe costs about **$500**, a GT Torsen differential **$807–1,000**, and a tail lamp **$125–500**. Chinese sellers cover the cosmetics. The structure is a different story. The rails, trunk floor, quarters and C-pillar have no copy parts. They need a frame bench and a professional welder, and Ford's October 2025 position statements say it **does not approve "clip" repairs and has not validated salvaged structural parts**. Across six phases the budget build comes to an estimated **SAR 70,000–185,000 ($18,700–49,300)**, with a realistic planning figure of **SAR 95,000–110,000**. That is barely below the "used parts, piece by piece" path in the earlier analysis. Doing the easy work yourself saves only about SAR 4,000–10,000. The go/no-go gate after Phase 1 is the most valuable money-saving tool in the plan, because it costs about SAR 3,000–8,000 and stops you before the roughly SAR 25,000–75,000 structural phase. You only reach that gate after buying the car, though. So the **maximum hammer bid stays at about SAR 35,000, with SAR 30,000 or less as the target** (before 15% VAT and any mazadat fees). Any whole-car donor has to come from inside the GCC or be dismantled in the US, because Saudi Arabia bans imports of accident-damaged vehicles.

> **How to read the numbers.** Figures with a link come from the research notes, mostly US Ford-dealer web prices, US used-part listings and Copart results via aggregators. Figures marked **(est.)** are estimates that fill gaps where no source was found. No Saudi labor rates, no Al Jazirah Ford counter prices and no priced S650 parts in Saudi or UAE scrapyards (تشليح) could be found online, so every SAR figure for those is an estimate. SAR = USD × 3.75. "Landed" means part plus freight, then **5% duty (one tariff source cites 8.5% for HS 8708.99; verify) and 15% VAT**. Shipments under SAR 1,000 including shipping are duty-exempt but still pay VAT ([ZATCA](https://www.zatca.gov.sa/en/RulesRegulations/Taxes/Pages/customs-individual/e-stores-customers.aspx), [DutyDecoder](https://dutydecoder.com/calculate/us-to-saudi-arabia-automotive-tariffs/)).

## GT parts fit most of the rear; the Dark Horse-only pieces are few but awkward

No source shows a Dark Horse-specific rear body structure. The new Ford quarter panel PR3Z-6327841-A is listed simply as "Coupe", with no trim split, at **$1,574.95 sale / $2,243.52 MSRP** ([Ford OEM Parts Direct](https://www.fordoempartsdirect.com/oem-parts/ford-quarter-panel-assembly-pr3z6327841a)). Aftermarket decklids are sold as one part for EcoBoost, GT and Dark Horse ([FFPerformance](https://ffperformance.co/product/anderson-composites-2024-ford-mustang-double-sided-carbon-fiber-type-oe-rear-decklid)). LMR describes the visible rear differences as "very subtle": spoiler, badge, valance and exhaust tips ([LMR](https://lmr.com/products/dark-horse-vs-gt-whats-the-difference)). The trim differences are bolt-on and system items: the **lower bumper cover and valance**, the **wing**, the **5-inch exhaust tips**, and the **factory rear-axle cooler** ([Hagerty](https://www.hagerty.com/media/?p=252981)). The best donor is therefore a **GT coupe with the Performance Pack, MagneRide, active exhaust and the same transmission as the Dark Horse**. That gives the same Torsen ratio, the same Brembo rear brakes and the closest harness match. A convertible donor is useless for the structure.

## The cheapest sensible source depends on whether the part can hurt you

The sourcing rule is simple. Buy **cosmetics from China**, **bolt-on mechanicals used from the US or the Gulf**, and **restraints, brake hydraulics and structural fasteners new from Ford**. Chinese sellers make S650 diffusers, lips, spoilers, carbon hoods and trim, often marked "not suitable for... Dark Horse version" ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/285897004889)). No Chinese S650 structural sheet metal or MagneRide damper was found. Lighting, glass, brakes and seat belts fall under SASO's auto-parts regulation ([TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-ae/services/product-certification/saso-saber-product-certification-in-saudi-arabia)). Shipments without a SABER shipment certificate can be ordered re-exported ([HQTS](https://www.hqts.com/?p=240092)). That is the hidden cost of a cheap AliExpress tail lamp. In the Gulf, Haraj lists many whole 2024 Mustangs, but the only Mustang part-outs found were 2005–2014 cars ([Haraj](https://haraj.com.sa/pic/موستنج)). The UAE's salvage auctions, such as Marhaba in Sharjah and Dubai ([Marhaba](https://marhabaauctions.com/salvage-damaged-cars-sharjah-dubai)), are the likeliest regional donor pool. Riyadh itself has a copy-parts channel. The user's screenshots of **alm2000.com** (Snapchat xALM2000, TikTok 3alm_2) show 2024+ Mustang parts at local SAR prices: a Dark Horse-style rear bumper with diffuser and quad-tip cutouts for **SAR 1,100**, OEM-style tail lamps (اسطب شكل الوكالة) at **SAR 1,100 per side**, smoked and clear tail-lamp sets at SAR 1,850 and SAR 1,750, a Dark Horse-style front lip (لحية) for a 2024–2026 GT at SAR 650, and a wheel set (طقم جنوط) at SAR 3,450 ([alm2000.com](https://alm2000.com)). The site also sells side skirts, spoilers and a GT-to-Dark Horse conversion kit (hood, front bumper, grille vents, wing) without visible prices, and it shows a discount-code banner ("A2000"). Buying lamps from a local seller avoids the SABER import hold entirely. A pair at SAR 2,200 costs less than half of one OEM lamp at Ford MSRP (about SAR 4,860), and it competes with a used US pair once freight is added. Quality, Dark Horse fitment and connector compatibility are unverified, so inspect and test-fit the lamps and bumper before paying, and confirm a return policy. A Haraj "wanted" ad (مطلوب قطع موستنج 2024 2025 دارك هورس: ربع خلفي، شنطة، صدام خلفي، دفرنس، اسطبات) costs nothing to post.

Freight decides what is cheap from the US. Aramex bills volumetric weight at L×W×H/5000 ([Aramex](https://www.aramex.com/faqs?page=2)), and one Shop & Ship user reported about SAR 90/kg ([Expat.com](https://www.expat.com/en/forum/middle-east/saudi-arabia/806210-shop-ship-aramex.html)). A crated quarter panel would bill at about 152 kg, which works out to roughly **SAR 13,700 by air (est.)**, so panels must go by sea consolidation. Small, dense parts such as a diff, fob, lamps or modules travel economically by forwarder. As a worked example, a $727 used US quarter ([recycler listing](https://demo10.autopartsearch.com/parts/FORD/MUSTANG/888R/2024/QUARTER_PANEL_ASSY/1001995017)) with about $600 of sea freight lands at about **$1,600 (SAR 6,000) (est.)**. That is roughly what a new Ford panel costs before freight. Buying used structure from the US saves little.

One wording point on the listing is worth checking with the seller. In Gulf usage, **اسطبات** usually means the tail and stop lamps, while shocks are **مساعدات**. "كسر اصطبات خلفية" may therefore mean the lamps rather than the dampers. The photos show both the lamps and the suspension damaged, so the budget covers both either way.

## Six phases, with the money concentrated in Phase 2

The order is fixed by physics and by Ford's procedures. First power the car up and diagnose it. Then measure and repair the structure on a bench, hang the mechanicals, finish the electrics, paint and wrap, and do calibration and alignment last. The structure must be true before the subframe hard points can be trusted, and pulling the body moves those points. That is why alignment comes last. Ford allows sectioning only at documented locations. Its manual permits outer-quarter sectioning through the sail panel ([I-CAR](https://rts.i-car.com/crn-526.html)) and states that "no sectioning repairs can be carried out on inner reinforcement panels" ([I-CAR](https://rts.i-car.com/prm-9581.html)). Rear-rail guidance conflicts across I-CAR entries: one 2023 Mustang listing says "Do Not Section" ([I-CAR](https://rts.i-car.com/prm-9122.html)), so plan for rail and floor replacement at factory seams. In the table below, "DIY" means owner-doable jobs: teardown and labeling, interior removal and cleaning, bumper cover, lamps, trim, exhaust, wheels, battery and power supply, and FORScan scans. FORScan's Extended licence costs about $20 a year or $50 for life, and the OBDLink EX adapter costs $59.95 ([OBDAdvisor](https://obdadvisor.com/forscan-review/), [OBDLink](https://www.obdlink.com/products/obdlink-ex/)). Subframe and damper bolt-up is DIY only with a torque sheet and a mentor. "Pro" means bench measuring, pulling, spot welding and bonding, SRS, all-keys-lost programming, glass bonding, ADAS calibration, alignment and the matte wrap.

| **6 Finish & legalize** | Glass bonding, lamps (local OEM-style pair SAR 2,200), bumper (local copy SAR 1,100 or OEM lower cover), trim; BLIS/camera calibration (FDRS) ([Ford 85186](https://rts.i-car.com/images/pdf/oem-info/ford/statements/85186.pdf)); alignment; Muroor / SASO / Fahas | DIY trim and lamps; pro glass, calibration, alignment | **6,000–17,000** | 1,600–4,530 |
| **Total** | | | **≈ 70,000–185,000** (low +20% contingency ≈ 84,000) | ≈ 18,700–49,300 |

Every SAR figure in the table is an estimate. No Saudi industrial-area price list for frame pulling, quarter replacement or wraps was found. The only local anchors are a Haraj full PPF offer at **SAR 6,969 plus SAR 800 for matte** ([Haraj](https://haraj.com.sa/en/11188551775/Full_Protection_PPF_Offer_for_Matte_and_Glossy/)), the Riyadh copy-part prices from alm2000.com (bumper SAR 1,100, lamp pair SAR 2,200), and the used-part prices above. The local lamp and bumper prices put Phase 6's cosmetic parts at roughly SAR 3,300, which supports the low end of that phase. The estimate puts outsourced labor across all phases at SAR 34,000–78,000, against SAR 30,000–68,000 with owner DIY. **The DIY saving is real but small**, because the welding and the calibration cannot be moved off the pro's bill. Cash flow follows the same logic. Phase 1 spends about 3–8% of the budget to settle the unknowns that could double it. Phase 2 then commits roughly 40% of the total in one step. Phases 5 and 6 can be stretched out: a primer-and-paint rear first, with the matte wrap later, is a legitimate way to pace the spending.

### The go/no-go gate after Phase 1

The gate needs written evidence, not optimism. Fail any **no-go** line and stop there, then part the car out. The cost so far is the hammer price plus about SAR 3,000–8,000.

| Check | Go | No-go |
|---|---|---|
| Modules (BCM, PCM, ABS, RCM, IPC) | All answer a FORScan or pro scan | Modules missing or dead (a cut harness can mean modules were stripped) |
| Key | Locksmith or dealer confirms all-keys-lost is possible on this S650, at ≤ SAR 4,000 | Nobody can program it. FORScan's PATS routine needs an existing working key ([FORScan forum](https://forum.forscan.org/viewtopic.php?p=11174)), and the dealer route needs FDRS/NASTF ([diag.net](https://diag.net/discussion/snnl3h2a6rggi9eyz2ka4ivgp2)) |
| Odometer | Mileage read from the cluster or modules | Unreadable anywhere. That cuts resale further |
| Airbags / restraints | None deployed, no RCM crash data | Deployments add a large new-OEM-only bill |
| Flood | No silt under carpets, no corrosion on seat rails or connectors | Water signs. Module corrosion surfaces months later |
| Bench measurement | Damage ends behind the B-pillar and rockers, and subframe mounting points are repairable | Distortion into boron B-pillar or inner reinforcements, which Ford says can't be sectioned ([I-CAR](https://rts.i-car.com/prm-9122.html)), or a roof or floor tear ahead of the rear seat |
| Front | Hood, support, radiator and condenser only | Apron or rail damage at the front |
| Paperwork | Car still registered; Absher permit issued; Muroor confirms the repair route in writing | Car de-registered as wrecked (تالفة), which cannot be re-registered ([Ajel](https://ajel.sa/local/8gg3zf)) |
| Quotes | Two or more written Phase 2 quotes and a parts list keep the total ≤ ~SAR 110,000 | Quotes push the total past ~SAR 120,000 |

## The donor GT must be front-hit and bought or broken in the right country

A front-hit GT coupe is the ideal donor, because its whole rear is intact. Copart sold prices for front-damaged 2024 GTs cluster at **$11,900–15,900 (SAR 44,600–59,600)** ([CarsBat](https://carsbat.com/en/detail/1fa6p8cf6r5401465-cud3s2/), [CarsBat](https://carsbat.com/en/detail/1fa6p8cf6r5435471-hhq8sr/)). A GT Premium with front and side damage went for $19,500 ([dreambid](https://dreambid.pl/en/lots/copart-90664565/2024-Ford-Mustang-1FA6P8CF9R5436095)). Those prices come before fees, dismantling and freight. Saudi Arabia, however, bans imports of cars damaged in accidents or floods ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/397938), [Bureau Veritas notice](https://cdn1-verigates-ppd.bureauveritas.com/sites/verigates/files/2019-02/Saudi%20Arabia%20-%20Notice%20to%20Exporters%20No%202012-1%20%28Used%20products%29.pdf)), and current guides list structurally damaged cars as prohibited ([Syarah](https://syarah.com/carsguide/en/conditions-importing-cars/)). A whole US Copart donor therefore almost certainly cannot enter the Kingdom as a car. The same logic applies to driving or trucking a damaged donor in from the UAE (est.). That leaves three workable routes. The first is a Saudi-registered accident GT bought locally on mazadat or Haraj, which has no import question at all. The second is a UAE salvage car dismantled in Sharjah, with the parts brought in by land. Whether US-origin used parts get GCC duty-free treatment is unverified. The third is a US Copart car dismantled by a US recycler, with the rear components crated into a sea container as parts. Whether customs treats a cut structural section as a "part" or as damaged-vehicle content is **unverified**. Get a broker's written ruling before buying, and prefer shipping individual panels over a joined clip.

The arithmetic decides whether a donor beats buying parts one by one. A donor supplies the Phase 2 panels (if you accept recycled structure), all of the Phase 3 mechanicals, and the lamps, glass, decklid and exhaust. At the individual-parts prices above, that is worth roughly **SAR 35,000–70,000 (est.)**. A US donor costs SAR 49,000–64,000 at hammer plus fees of about $800–1,500 (est.), before dismantling and freight. On its own it only breaks even. It wins only if the donor's surplus front half and Coyote/10R80 drivetrain can be resold, and no used Gen4 Coyote prices were found to prove they can. A local or UAE donor at **SAR 40,000 or less (est.)**, with a resaleable front, is the version that saves money. A pristine rear-half EcoBoost coupe is a fallback for body panels, glass and Premium lamps only.

## Red lines that no budget should cross

Ford's collision position statements, reissued on 1 October 2025, say Ford **"does not approve or validate so-called 'clip' repairs"** and that recycled or salvaged structural parts "have not been validated by Ford" ([CollisionWeek](https://collisionweek.com/2025/10/23/ford-details-updated-collision-repair-position-statements-technical-data-latest-target-magazine/), [I-CAR Ford statements](https://rts.i-car.com/oem-information/ford/ford-statements.html)). The safe budget compromise is to use **donor or used parts for everything bolted on, and new Ford structural panels fitted at factory seams** wherever the quotes allow. Two things should never happen in any version of this build: a joined clip cut across the rockers or B-pillar, and welding within 50 mm of a hinge or striker ([Ford On Target](https://rts.i-car.com/images/pdf/oem-info/ford/on-target/OT20242.pdf)). Airbags, belts and pretensioners, the restraint module, crash sensors and brake hydraulics are **new OEM only**. Inspect the fuel tank and lines behind the crushed rear floor before power-up (est.). MagneRide should not be replaced with passive shocks plus a simulator, because owners report intermittent faults and speed limiting ([Mustang6G](https://www.mustang6g.com/forums/threads/magneride-code-removal.149155/)). Lighting and glass must be SASO-conformant, which in practice means buying them used-OEM or from a local certified seller.

On the Saudi side, secondary sources report that Muroor treats any body or structure modification made without the official procedure as a violation. They also describe a separate SASO modified-vehicle inspection, reportedly SAR 300–500 (unverified), which periodic Fahas does not replace ([rukn-services](https://rukn-services.com/فحص-السيارات-المعدلة/)). No official text addresses installing a rear section from another VIN. Treat that as a written question for Muroor before buying a donor. If a donor panel carries its own VIN stamp or label, declare it openly and never conceal it. The single most valuable legal fact is that this car is still **registered with a valid فحص دوري**. Keep it that way, confirm mazadat transfers the registration (استمارة), and never let it be dropped as تالف. Once a car is de-registered as wrecked it cannot be re-registered ([Ajel](https://ajel.sa/local/8gg3zf)). Keep every invoice, the before-and-after measurement printouts and photos for Muroor, SASO, Fahas, insurers and the eventual buyer.

## A revised bid: still about SAR 35,000, ideally 30,000

The earlier valuation still holds. Clean low-kilometer 2024 Dark Horses are listed at **SAR 285,000–290,000** in Saudi Arabia ([SaudiSale](https://cars.saudisale.com/en/listings/0A83wv)). With a structural-repair history and an unknown odometer, the estimated resale is SAR 160,000–190,000 (midpoint 175,000). The same bid formula as before applies: resale, minus repair × 1.2, minus a 10% risk margin, all divided by 1.15 for VAT.

| Rebuild outcome | Repair cost (SAR, est.) | Max hammer bid (SAR) |
|---|---|---|
| Best case (gate passes cleanly, local donor, DIY) | 70,000 | ≈ 64,000 |
| Planning case | 95,000 | ≈ 38,000 |
| Planning case, upper | 110,000 | ≈ 22,000 |
| Stress case | 130,000+ | ≈ 0 |

The best-case bid looks tempting, but the gate that would prove you are in the best case only opens after you own the car. **Bid to the planning case: target ≤ SAR 30,000 and keep SAR 35,000 as the ceiling (≈ SAR 40,250 with VAT).** That is the earlier figure, unchanged. The budget methods mostly protect you from overruns after purchase. They do not create room for a higher bid. The downside below SAR 35,000 is the part-out exit. Its value (earlier estimate SAR 55,000–110,000 gross, about 60–70% realized) is **unsourced**, so treat it as a soft floor rather than a guarantee.

## Conclusion

The budget toolkit lowers the ceiling of the rebuild far more than its floor. GT interchange, used US assemblies, Chinese cosmetics and owner labor together remove most of the frightening OEM line items, such as the $1,300 tail lamps and the $1,500 mufflers. They cannot touch the structural repair, which decides both safety and legality. The useful reframing is that this car is a **SAR 100,000 structural project with a cheap shopping list attached**, not a cheap car. The owner's best lever is not his wrench but his sequencing. Spend about SAR 5,000 on Phase 1, demand written answers from the scan, the bench and Muroor, and keep the discipline to part the car out if any of them is a no.

A front-hit GT donor makes sense only where the import ban doesn't bite and the donor's own front half can be resold. That points to a Saudi or UAE car at about SAR 40,000 or less, not a US auction win. If the hammer price stays at or below SAR 30,000 and the Phase 1 gate passes, a careful owner can finish a legal, safe Dark Horse for well under half the price of a clean one. If either condition fails, the car's best future is as parts for someone else's rebuild.
