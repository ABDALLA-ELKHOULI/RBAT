# Most Apple deals still lose money in Riyadh

The earlier finding mostly holds up against the new Apple-specific data. **No Apple product on the US Black Friday 2026 deal lists is a clear buy for resale in Saudi Arabia.** Only four lines come near break-even or above it, and each depends on a number nobody has checked yet. **iPad Air 11" M4** makes about **+SAR 315 (10%)**, but only if the unconfirmed **$559** price comes back. **AirPods 4 with ANC at $119** breaks even on its own and makes **+SAR 59 per unit (9%)** when four ship in one box. The entry **Mac mini at $779** breaks even if it resells for about SAR 3,870. The **AirTag 4-pack** needs a Saudi resale price of about **SAR 486**. Everything else loses money once shipped. MacBook Air and Pro lose **SAR 400–1,500 per unit**, AirPods Pro 3 at today's **$199** loses **SAR 242**, Watch Ultra 4 loses **SAR 422**, and iPhones lose **SAR 775–1,100**. Two things changed since the last report. The **AirPods 4 line has the widest US-to-Saudi price gap of any Apple product** (Apple SA asks SAR 599/829, against $89/$119 in the US), and the **iPhone 17 Pro the US notes list is a discontinued, previous-generation phone** that Saudi retailers are already clearing. Three costs explain almost every loss: **15% import VAT on goods plus freight, 5% duty once a parcel passes SAR 1,000, and freight of SAR 105–340**. On top of that, Saudi White Friday and Jarir's bank-card offers push local prices down in the same week you buy. Every price below comes from search snippets, not pages anyone opened. Several product names (MacBook Neo, M6 Mac mini) are unverified. Treat this report as a shortlist to verify, not a buy list.

## VAT, freight and duty add 21–45% before the first riyal of resale

The model uses the rules the notes could source. **15% VAT applies to every import regardless of value** ([DHL regulatory update](https://mydhl.express.dhl/at/en/help-and-support/customs-clearance-advice/customs-regulatory-updates/africa-middle-east.html)). It is charged on the goods plus shipping, insurance and any duty ([Fuuffy](https://www.fuuffy.com/en/tax-and-duty/SAUDI%20ARABIA)). **Customs duty is waived only when the shipment, freight included, is SAR 1,000 or less** ([ZATCA](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/RulesRegulations/Taxes/Pages/customs_individual/e-stores-customers.aspx); [Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/611390/SAUDI-ARABIA/Customs-No-duty-for-shipments-valued-below-SR1000)). Above that threshold the model charges 5%, which is the general rate. HS-specific rates for phones, laptops and watches are unconfirmed, and one calculator lists smartphones at 0% ([CalcMyTariff](https://www.calcmytariff.com/country/saudi-arabia/consumer-electronics)). Clearance is **SAR 15 per declaration at or below SAR 1,000** ([Saudi Export Pro](https://saudiexportpro.com/رسوم-الجمارك-على-الشحنات-الشخصية/)). Above that, Aramex charges 0.15% plus USD 10 ([Stackry](https://www.stackry.com/duties-and-taxes/saudi-arabia)) and carriers add SAR 20–100 in disbursement fees ([Fuuffy](https://www.fuuffy.com/en/tax-and-duty/SAUDI%20ARABIA)). The model uses SAR 55 for those parcels. A seller below the **SAR 375,000** VAT-registration threshold cannot reclaim import VAT ([Skylight](https://skylightchat.com/en/resources/saudi-vat-ecommerce-guide)), so the 15% is a real cost.

Freight follows MyUS's October 2026 sample for a 1 lb parcel to Saudi Arabia: **$19.51 budget, $21.87 Aramex, $42.25 DHL** ([MyUS](https://www.myus.com/stores/how-to-shop-apple-and-ship/saudi-arabia/)). Shipito adds a processing fee of about $3.25 ([DutyGlobal](https://dutyglobal.com/forwarders/shipito)). The model charges **$25 for the first lb, $9 for each additional lb, plus $3**. That works out to SAR 105 for 1 lb (AirPods, AirTag, Pencil, Series 11/SE), SAR 139 for 2 lb (iPhone, iPad, Ultra), SAR 172 for AirPods Max, SAR 206 for Mac mini, SAR 240 for the 13" Air, SAR 274 for the 15" Air and SAR 308–341 for MacBook Pro. These rates and shipping weights are extrapolations, not quotes. The model assumes a forwarder in Delaware, Oregon or New Hampshire, so there is no US sales tax ([Forwardme](https://www.forwardme.com/shipping/us-to-saudi-arabia)). It also deducts Haraj's **1% fee** ([Haraj FAQ](https://haraj.com.sa/en/faq)). Resale prices sit below the cheapest Saudi street or list price found. Buyers discount US units because they get no Saudi warranty, and Apple's warranty lets it **restrict service to the country of original sale** ([Apple warranty](https://www.apple.com/legal/warranty/products/ios-warranty-row-english.html)). Where no Saudi price exists for the exact model, the estimate uses the roughly **4.2–4.7× SAR-per-USD list ratio** seen on sourced Apple SA prices. Examples are AirPods Pro 3 at SAR 1,049 vs $249, AirPods 4 at 599 vs $129, and Ultra 4 at 3,599 vs $799 ([Apple SA AirPods 4](https://www.apple.com/sa-en/shop/buy-airpods/airpods-4); [Apple SA AirPods Pro 3](https://www.apple.com/sa-en/shop/buy-airpods/airpods-pro-3)). These estimates are flagged "est." in the table.

## The iPhone 17 Pro in US deal lists is last year's phone

The two sets of notes disagree on which iPhone is current. **They are describing the same market at different points in its cycle.** Forbes reports Best Buy selling new unlocked iPhone 17 Pro and Pro Max at **$1,099.99 and $1,199.99 with no discount** on 5 November 2026. It adds that every discounted iPhone 17 at Best Buy is refurbished ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jaymcgregor/2026/11/05/apple-iphone-17-price-cut-discount-amazon-black-friday-iphone-17-pro/)). Wikipedia says Apple **discontinued the 17 Pro and Pro Max on 9 September 2026** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone_17_Pro)). Saudi sources say iPhone 18 Pro pre-orders opened on 12 September and sales began on 18 September, at **SAR 5,699** for the 18 Pro and from **SAR 9,499** for the foldable **iPhone Duo** ([Saudi Life Guide](https://saudilifeguide.com/iphone-18-pro-price-in-saudi-arabia/); [Apple SA iPhone 18 Pro](https://www.apple.com/sa-en/shop/buy-iphone/iphone-18-pro); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/tech/iphone-18-pro-duo-price-comparison-uae-us-saudi-arabia-india)). Both can be true. Apple stops making the old Pro, and US retailers keep selling the stock they already hold at full price. Two independent signals confirm the 18 Pro exists in the US: carriers are advertising a "free iPhone 18 Pro" ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/live/news/early-black-friday-holiday-sales-2026-im-shopping-now)), and Gulf News ranks the US as the cheapest market for the 18 Pro and Duo ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/retail/us-uae-saudi-uk-or-india-where-iphone-duo-and-iphone-18-pro-are-cheapest-to-buy-1.500669754)). For resale, this makes the 17 Pro worse than the earlier report assumed. In Saudi Arabia it is **clearance stock**: sealed 17 Pro Max 256GB units ask **SAR 5,200–5,500 on Haraj**, often with STC warranty ([Haraj](https://haraj.com.sa/en/11171659218/%D8%A7%D9%8A%D9%81%D9%88%D9%86_17_%D8%A8%D8%B1%D9%88_%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%83%D8%B3_256_GB_%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF_%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B9/)), and an earlier Pricena snapshot had Amazon.sa at SAR 4,875 ([Pricena](https://sa.pricena.com/en/product/apple-iphone-17-pro-max-price-in-saudi-arabia)). A US unit landed at SAR 5,656 cannot compete with that.

The current 18 Pro comes closest of any iPhone, but the notes contain no US price for it. On the assumptions in the table, it breaks even only if the US price is **$1,066 or less**, and Apple has historically offered at most about **$75 in gift card** on an iPhone, never a price cut ([Plug.tech](https://www.plug.tech/blogs/news/how-much-do-iphones-drop-on-black-friday)). The Duo needs a US price below about **$1,809** to break even at a SAR 8,500 resale, and its US price is also not in the notes. Refurbished iPhones fail outright. Walmart's restored iPhone 17 256GB at **$734** lands at SAR 3,546 ([Walmart](https://www.walmart.com/c/kp/apple-iphone-black-friday-deals)), while Jarir sells a **new** iPhone 17 256GB with local warranty for **SAR 3,279** ([Jarir](https://www.jarir.com/apple-iphone-17-smartphones-666784.html)).

Two hardware caveats determine what buyers will pay. The first is SIM type. US iPhones are eSIM-only. A MacRumors forum list says Saudi iPhone 17 models are eSIM-only as well ([MacRumors forums](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/iphone-17-and-iphone-17-pro-models-are-esim-only-in-these-countries.2464993)), and a Gulf spec page says the same of the Gulf 17 Pro Max ([mob4g](https://mob4g.com/specs/iphone-17-pro-max/)). The US notes' belief that Saudi units keep a physical SIM tray is unsourced, so the sourced view wins here. eSIM is probably not a penalty for the 17 series, but it is unconfirmed for the 18 series and the Duo. The second is warranty. **iPhone warranty is country-limited in practice.** Apple's terms allow it to restrict service to the country of sale, and Apple Community replies say a US iPhone has no warranty in Saudi Arabia ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255258597)). Saudi buyers also lose their Saudi consumer-law rights against a local seller ([Apple SA sales policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/sales-support/sales-policies/retail_sa.html)). The common belief that Mac, iPad, AirPods and Watch carry international warranty was not confirmed this session. Every phone must be carrier-unlocked.

## The luggage route is cheaper but legally closed to resale goods

Carrying items in a suitcase removes freight and clearance fees, and for small, light goods that is most of the problem. The table's "Bag" column shows what happens when goods are carried **and declared** at 5% duty plus 15% VAT on the purchase price. On that basis, AirPods 4 ANC rises to **+SAR 115 (17%)**, the iPad Air at $650 to +SAR 126, the Mac mini to +SAR 284, and AirPods Pro 3 at $149 to +SAR 78. Macs, watches and iPhones still lose. The legal catch is that the personal exemption does not cover these goods. Article 103 of the GCC Common Customs Law exempts passengers' personal effects and gifts only when they are **"of a personal nature and not in commercial quantities"** ([ZATCA airport guide](https://eservices.zatca.gov.sa/sites/sc/en/CustomsGuideNew/CustomsGuideAirPorts/Pages/Page02.aspx)). The commonly quoted **SAR 3,000** baggage cap comes from secondary sources and is unconfirmed for 2026 ([House of Saud](https://houseofsaud.com/travel/saudi-customs-rules/); [World Travel Guide](https://www.worldtravelguide.net/guides/middle-east/saudi-arabia/money-duty-free/)). The SAR 3,000 limit ZATCA itself publishes applies to arrival duty-free shops, not to baggage ([ZATCA news](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/news_1238.aspx)). Declarations go through ZATCA's e-Declaration, and reported thresholds for cash and goods conflict at SAR 60,000 and 40,000 ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-passengers-required-to-declare-money-goods-worth-more-than-sr60000-1.90831519); [Wego](https://blog.wego.com/saudi-arabia-travellers-must-declare-cash-and-gold/)). **Goods bought to resell must be declared and taxed.** Several sealed identical units in one bag are likely to be judged commercial, which can trigger SABER conformity requirements for electronics ([HQTS](https://www.hqts.com/saber-certificate-guide/)). The Bag column also assumes passengers get no SAR 1,000 duty waiver, which is a conservative assumption, and leaves out excess-baggage charges. One iPad or one pair of AirPods carried for your own use is a different matter. It falls under the personal allowance, but it is not a business.

## Gift cards, White Friday and Jarir's bank offers cap the upside

**Apple's own Black Friday promo would make every row worse.** In 2025 Apple announced its event on 20 November and ran it from 28 November to 1 December. It gave gift cards rather than price cuts: **$100** with Mac mini and iPad Air, **$150** with iMac, **$175** with the 13" MacBook Air, **$200** with the 15" Air, and up to **$250** on some products ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/11/20/apple-announces-2025-black-friday-event/); [appleworld.today](https://appleworld.today/2025/11/apples-black-friday-specials-run-november-28-through-december-1/)). As of 7 November there was no 2026 announcement. Third-party lists of $200/$250 cards appear to be carried over from earlier years ([Groupon](https://www.groupon.com/coupons/blog/apple-black-friday-deals)). A gift card is US store credit. Saudi duty and VAT are charged on the full invoice price, so the card does not reduce them. Take a 15" Air 1TB at its $1,799 list price with a $200 card. It lands at about SAR 8,530. Even counting the card at its full SAR 750 face value, that is **SAR 7,780, against SAR 7,400 for Amazon's $1,549 cash price**. Apple also reportedly refuses forwarder addresses ([Apple Community](https://discussions-cn-prz.apple.com/thread/254991858)), and Best Buy blocks them ([Planet Express](https://planetexpress.com/how-to/bestbuy/)). Amazon is the practical buying channel. Its lowest prices are short-lived: the June AirPods Pro 3 low lasted about a day ([9to5Toys](https://9to5toys.com/2026/06/17/airpods-pro-3-dropped-best-price-to-date/)).

On the Saudi side, competition peaks in the same week you would buy. ## Conclusion

The Apple arbitrage that works is narrow. It is not about the biggest US discount. It is about products where **Apple's own Saudi list price is unusually high relative to the US** and the item is **heavy enough in value to absorb freight but light enough to ship cheaply**. The AirPods 4 line and the iPad Air fit that description. The headline Black Friday deals, the $250-off MacBook Airs and the $100-off Ultra 4, do not. That reverses the usual shopper's logic: the percentage off in the US matters less than the SAR-per-dollar ratio Apple SA charges and the SAR 1,000 duty line.

The margins that remain are thin enough that a single local event can erase them: one Jarir bank-card weekend, one Amazon.sa White Friday markdown, or one forwarder quote above the model. The right move is to treat Black Friday 2026 as a **paid experiment rather than a trade**. That means a handful of pre-sold iPads and AirPods 4 ANC, bought only after the Saudi street prices on the checklist are confirmed. The downside is capped at a few hundred riyals, and the result is real buyer-discount data that no research note has yet captured.
