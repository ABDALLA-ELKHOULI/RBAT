# Grow more fodder per drop in arid Egypt

The most useful ways to cut water use for fodder in Egypt and MENA are mostly simple, not new technology. For berseem (برسيم), better field management saves **15–25% of water for under 6% yield loss**. Swapping part of the forage base to crops that make more dry matter per cubic metre does more: spineless cactus, pearl millet and sorghum, plus salt-tolerant forages for brackish land. The biggest "no added water" option is upgrading residues farms already have, such as urea-treated rice straw and date-palm waste. Hydroponic sprouted barley gets the most attention as "fodder without water". It does save **90–98% of water per kg of fresh feed**, but it loses dry matter during sprouting and usually costs several times more than hay per kg of dry matter. One Saudi peer-reviewed study found it loss-making for small farms. This matters because forage takes a large share of irrigation water in the region: about 12% of Saudi water in 2020, and enough that Saudi Arabia has restricted alfalfa and Rhodes grass since 2018. Two cautions apply to the whole report. Many Egyptian water-productivity figures are on a fresh-weight basis, and almost no Egypt-specific cost data (EGP per feddan) were found, so the costs below come from Gulf and US studies.

## Berseem is protein-efficient, not water-efficient

Surface-irrigated berseem in the Nile Delta uses about **38–60 cm of water over the season (roughly 1,600–2,500 m³/feddan)**. Clay-soil trials applied about 2,200–2,600 m³/feddan ([JICA](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/11705621_17.pdf); [JSSAE](https://jssae.journals.ekb.eg/article_51928.html)). At Sakha, water productivity was **16.6 kg fresh fodder per m³ applied and 24.8 kg per m³ consumed** ([JSSAE PDF](https://jssae.journals.ekb.eg/article_51928_b8171bd1b30e2dd189cd30b9f74aa619.pdf)). Berseem is roughly 12–15% dry matter, so this works out to about 2–3.5 kg DM/m³. That is similar to pearl millet and well below cactus. Berseem's real advantage is protein quality.

Four management changes are proven in Egypt and cost little:

- **Cutoff irrigation:** stopping water before it reaches the end of the strip cut applied water from 2,615 to 2,215 m³/feddan, a **4–15% saving**. Yields held, with the best productivity at 90% strip length ([EKB](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_3155.html)).
- **Scheduling by crop water demand (ETc):** this raised berseem water productivity **12–25% over farmer practice** ([JSSAE](https://jssae.journals.ekb.eg/article_55100_521cbf84c96ec4c8bc7896c2ae98c986.pdf)).
- **Fewer irrigations between cuts:** water use falls from 59.6 cm to 38.0 cm when farmers move from three irrigations per cut to one. Yield falls somewhat, but water use efficiency improves ([JSSAE](https://jssae.journals.ekb.eg/article_51928.html)).
- **Dry sowing instead of wet (herati) sowing:** this costs only **3–6% of dry yield**. A Ministry of Agriculture document estimates the national saving at **700–840 million m³ per year** across about 2.5 million feddans ([MoA](https://moa.gov.eg/media/ezzhbcr5/1-2.pdf)). That attribution came from a search snippet, so confirm it against the original.

Bed-furrow planting also beat farmer practice at Gemmeiza ([EKB](https://fjard.journals.ekb.eg/article_194691_1f9356ad8572c559731e00799679e374.pdf)). Sowing in early to mid-October uses the cooler, low-evaporation months.

Drip irrigation works better for perennial alfalfa than for broadcast berseem.

- **Saudi Arabia:** subsurface drip saved **35.7%** and surface drip 29.2% compared with sprinklers ([Assiut Univ.](https://b.aun.edu.eg/agriculture/maximizing-productivity-and-water-use-efficiency-alfalfa-under-precise-sub-surface-drip-irrigation)).
- **Farafra Oasis:** smart irrigation through leaky pipes, plus soil amendment, gave about **3.0–3.1 kg/m³** ([Al-Azhar J.](https://azeng.journals.ekb.eg/article_436686.html)).

No Egyptian trial of berseem under drip was found.

Hydrogels, humic acid and mycorrhizae all show water-use gains. The evidence for berseem specifically is thin:

- The hydrogel result (0.3% hydrogel with 85% of normal irrigation) came from a crop other than berseem ([Sci Rep](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41598-024-57786-5)).
- The mycorrhiza benefit for berseem comes from Sicily ([PLOS One](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3940947)).

Treat these as add-ons to test, not as core strategy.

Berseem also handles salt poorly. Saline drainage water at 3.6–7.4 dS/m **cut Egyptian clover yield by 68%**, against 36% for ryegrass ([Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/experimental-agriculture/article/yield-responses-of-winter-rabi-forage-crops-to-irrigation-with-saline-drainage-water/C42921A07660E693B892359C0DC1B378)). Low-salinity treated wastewater, by contrast, suited berseem well ([AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65de30770f3e94b9e5cb9c75)). Among cultivars, Helaly yields highest, at about 54.8 t fresh and 8.2 t dry per feddan ([EKB](https://jpp.journals.ekb.eg/article_85575.html)). No drought-tolerant berseem release from 2020–2026 was confirmed.

## Cactus and C4 grasses produce more dry matter per cubic metre

| Crop | Water productivity (as reported) | Water source / note |
|---|---|---|
| Spineless cactus (Opuntia) | **3.3–6.2 kg DM/m³**; 17.7 t DM/ha on 285 mm transpired | Rainfed ([CGIAR](https://mel.cgiar.org/reporting/downloadmelspace/hash/e93bd596c556981ce90316a0662ccb76/v/2f75c6cf9793152c6b6ee91b8595b5b5)) |
| Pearl millet, sandy soil, Ismailia | 6.2–7.0 kg/m³ (probably fresh); 38% water saved at 100% ETo vs farmer practice | Sprinkler ([EKB](https://ejdr.journals.ekb.eg/article_466356.html)) |
| Sorghum–berseem rotation | 1.80 kg/m³ for the whole system | Irrigated ([CSIRO](https://publish.csiro.au/CP/CP22139)) |
| Berseem | about 2–3.5 kg DM/m³ (converted from fresh) | Surface irrigation |
| Rhodes grass / alfalfa | 21,000–23,000 / 36,000–45,000 m³/ha per season | Restricted in Saudi Arabia ([EKB](https://bfa.journals.ekb.eg/article_231893_660333e04db23f319e3d4c52cbe1302e.pdf)) |

The figures come from studies with different methods and units, so treat the ranking as approximate.

Cactus delivers the most feed per unit of water by a wide margin. It also supplies drinking water to the animal: sheep eating about 300 g DM/day of cactus cut their drinking water from **2.4 L to 0.1 L per day** ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/Y2808E/y2808e0b.htm)). Its limits are low fibre and low protein. Feed it alongside straw and a protein source; it can replace up to 60% of hay ([Encyclopedia.pub](https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/history/show/60324)).

Pearl millet is the best summer complement to winter berseem. Unlike sorghum, it carries **no prussic acid risk**. Sorghum needs grazing discipline: don't turn hungry animals onto it, and wait 7–14 days after a drought-breaking rain ([SDSU](https://extension.sdstate.edu/prussic-acid-precautions)).

## Saline land supports halophytes, but only as part of a ration

**Brackish water (about 5–20 dS/m).** Several forages hold up under brackish water:

- **Blue panic and Sesbania** yielded more than cereals under moderate to high salinity in Morocco ([ICARDA](https://ftp.icarda.org/node/18655)). Sesbania yield was barely affected up to 5 dS/m in the UAE ([CGIAR](https://repo.mel.cgiar.org/items/6c204308-2592-44f5-914d-59c30a5d0090/full)).
- **Salt-tolerant pearl millet:** ICBA selected 10 forage lines under 7.5–8.25 dS/m ([ICBA](https://iiqp.biosaline.org/sites/default/files/project_brief_plant_genetic_resources_for_marginal_environments_v1-eng-web.pdf)).
- **Quinoa** has been grown at up to 20 dS/m ([ICBA](https://quinoaconference.biosaline.org/sites/default/files/Quinoa%20-%20Conference%20presentation-nk%20rao.pdf)).
- **Dual-purpose barley** at Ras Sudr does best when sown in late October ([AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65df05adb766d82b18ffc641)).

**Seawater.** Only true halophytes survive seawater irrigation:

- **Atriplex (saltbush)** yielded 1.5–2.9 kg DM/m²/yr, but contains 25–40% ash. Sheep ate only about 400 g DM/day even with added corn meal ([BGU](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/developing-the-seawater-agriculture-concept-2/)).
- **Salicornia** contains about 50% ash ([AREEO](https://wra.areeo.ac.ir/article_124782.html?lang=en)).

These plants supplement a ration. They cannot replace berseem.

**Rainfed rangeland.** Forage kochia gives **1–1.8 t/ha on 100–200 mm of rain** ([AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65de17306eef00c2ce9fb215)).

## Hydroponic sprouts save water but lose dry matter

Hydroponic fodder uses **1.5–2 L of water per kg of fresh fodder**. Field barley uses about 73 L/kg and alfalfa about 85 L/kg ([Feedipedia](https://www.feedipedia.org/sites/default/files/public/BH_048_hydroponic_fodder.pdf)). The economics fall apart on dry matter:

- **Dry matter loss:** sprouting **burns 7–47% of the seed's dry matter** ([NJAP](https://njap.org.ng/index.php/njap/article/view/6075)). The output is only 12–25% DM ([UNDP](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2025-10/hydroponics_cost_benefit_analysis_final_report_3_1.pdf)). The farmer is in effect buying grain and paying to lose part of it, and the water embedded in that purchased seed is rarely counted.
- **Cost per kg DM:** Iowa State estimates **about $0.40/lb DM, against $0.11 for good hay** ([Iowa State](https://www.iastatedigitalpress.com/air/article/id/6470/)). Utah State found costs **13.4 times** those of field barley ([USU](https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1080&context=gradreports2023)).
- **Small farms:** a Saudi study found it uneconomic for small farms ([Sustainability](https://dx.doi.org/10.3390/su15010822)).

Gulf operators such as Al Aliyo, a $2.4M facility producing about 10 t/day ([GCC Business News](https://www.gccbusinessnews.com/uae-based-al-aliyo-opens-2-4mn-hydroponic-fodder-farms-in-hamriyah-free-zone/)), and GrazeIT, which runs a service model ([Vertical Farm Daily](https://verticalfarmdaily.com/article/9444860/hydroponic-fodder-is-more-nutritious-than-conventional-fodder)), avoid the problem by taking the capital cost off the farmer. None has published audited economics.

## Residue upgrading is the cheapest "no-water" option

The most proven zero-added-water approach in the region is upgrading residues farms already produce:

- **Rice straw in Egypt:** ensiling it with urea improves its feed value and lowers the cost per unit of digestible nutrients ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/x5487e/x5487e0b.htm)). Buffalo calves fed urea-molasses straw silage at 40% of the ration reached **518 kg against 384 kg** ([AgEcon](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/244970?ln=en)). Adoption is the weak point: farmers elsewhere dropped treated straw because it was inconvenient ([LRRD](https://lrrd.org/lrrd16/7/trac16052.htm)), so training and extension matter.
- **Palm leaves in the UAE:** leaves treated with 4–6% urea replaced Rhodes grass for lambs ([AAAID](https://www.aaaid.org/?p=3654)).
- **Date-waste blocks in Algeria:** these made good goat feed ([Ouargla](https://dspace.univ-ouargla.dz/jspui/handle/123456789/4195)) and keep for 2–3 years ([ICARDA](https://mel.cgiar.org/reporting/download/hash/jxyAthJ9)).

Several other options are still at trial or niche stage:

- **Azolla and duckweed** are high in protein (25–43% CP), but the region has only trials so far. Their pond evaporation in desert heat has not been measured ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/AC151E/AC151E08.htm); [NRC Egypt](https://bnrc.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s42269-022-00752-w)).
- **Spirulina** works as a feed additive, not as bulk feed ([Mansoura](https://jappmu.journals.ekb.eg/article_40308.html)).
- **Black soldier fly larvae** (Circa Biotech in the UAE, Proteina in Egypt) mainly feed poultry and fish ([WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/b35ypbl-circa-biotech-upcycles-organic-waste-into-animal)).
- **Fog harvesting** supplies household water, not irrigation ([UNFCCC](https://unfccc.int/node/10018)).

## Conclusion

The question "how do we grow berseem without water?" is framed too narrowly. Berseem's water cost can be trimmed by about a fifth through management alone. Bigger gains come from treating berseem as the high-protein part of a mixed system:

- winter berseem on the best fresh water, or on treated wastewater;
- summer pearl millet in place of more irrigated perennials;
- cactus hedges on marginal or rainfed land, supplying both energy and drinking water;
- brackish-tolerant Sesbania or blue panic on saline plots;
- treated rice straw or date waste as the fibre base.

A practical sequence for an Egyptian farm:

1. Adopt dry sowing, cutoff irrigation and ETc scheduling now.
2. Pilot pearl millet and cactus on a few feddans.
3. Ensile straw instead of burning it.
4. Consider hydroponics only if imported hay is very expensive and someone else carries the capital cost.

The main unknown is local cost. A same-site comparison of dry matter per m³ and EGP per kg DM for berseem, millet, cactus and hydroponic sprouts would settle the choice, and no such study was found.
