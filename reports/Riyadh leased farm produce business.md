# Grow herbs, sell clean, avoid commodities

**Bottom line:** With about 100,000 SAR, the most defensible business near Riyadh is a **leased 4,000–10,000 m² working farm in Al-Kharj or Durma, about 18,000–35,000 SAR a year in rent**. Grow **winter leafy greens and herbs** (jarjeer, coriander, parsley, dill, mint, lettuces, baladi varieties) from October to April. Add a **small baladi (free-range) laying flock**, and sell under a **clean, semi-premium brand**. Sell first direct to households, restaurants and farmers' markets, and add quick-commerce apps (Ninja, HungerStation, Jahez, Keeta) once you have a commercial register (CR), labels and steady volume. Use the Aziziyah central market (سوق الخضار المركزي بالعزيزية) only to clear surplus. Commodity tomatoes, cucumbers and white eggs are a trap: Saudi Arabia is at or above **100% self-sufficiency in cucumbers, zucchini, eggplant, okra and table eggs** ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1867020); [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/economy/na/2226120)). Wholesale tomato crates swing between 15 and 65 SAR within weeks ([Alyaum](https://www.alyaum.com/articles/6622564/); [Al-Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1043104)). The price gap in herbs is wide: a conventional parsley bundle sells for about **1 SAR**, and a branded organic hydroponic 150 g pack sells for **6.35 SAR** ([Kanbkam](https://supermarket.kanbkam.com/sa/ar/green-parsley-leaves-1-bundle-T16205); [Alqimam](https://alqimam-alwatania.com/%D8%A8%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%86%D8%B3-%D8%B9%D8%B6%D9%88%D9%8A-%D8%B1%D8%A8%D8%B7%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%B7%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8)). That gap is the opportunity. Government setup costs are small, roughly 1,000–5,000 SAR, because the Naama agricultural register is free and new firms pay no Chamber fee for three years. Labour and rent are the big costs. Expect **year one to lose money** (an illustrative −20k to −60k SAR) while the brand and buyer base are built. Year two is where the plan has to prove itself. **Caveat:** most Saudi news, government, classifieds and app sites were blocked during this research, so nearly every figure below comes from search-engine snippets, not full pages. Treat each number as a lead to verify in person, not a quote.

## Leafy herbs beat tomatoes, cucumbers and white eggs

Ranking enterprises on a 100k budget comes down to three filters: low capex, fast cash, and pricing power against large producers. **Coriander gives a first cut about 35 days after sowing, then 4–5 cuts a season. Jarjeer (arugula) is first cut at about 6 weeks, then every 4 weeks.** Sowing is best in October–November ([altalebagri](https://altalebagri.com/ar/blog/%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%81%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%B2%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D8%B2%D8%A8%D8%B1%D8%A9/a-548072779); [agriceg](https://agriceg.com/watercress-cultivation-method/)). Parsley can produce 20–22.5 t per feddan (about 4,200 m²) over five cuts under Egyptian conditions ([v-alue](https://v-alue.com/%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A9-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%89-%D9%85%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9-%D8%B2%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%86%D8%B3/)). Chefs in Riyadh say imported herbs often arrive degraded ([Agritecture](https://www.agritecture.com/blog/2023/3/29/bringing-the-freshest-salad-greens-to-saudi-arabia-with-bather-smart-farm)). Riyadh is named as the main demand centre for premium herbs and microgreens ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/saudi-arabia-indoor-farming-market)).

Baladi eggs rank second because the premium is large and the product is easy to explain. Direct sellers in Riyadh ask **25–30 SAR per tray of 30** ([OpenSooq](https://sa.opensooq.com/ar/search/280907088); [Mstaml](https://www.mstaml.com/en/product/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B6-%D8%AF%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%AC-%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A)), and online "100% natural" trays reach 48–57 SAR ([Alqimam](https://alqimam-alwatania.com/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B6-%D8%A8%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A-%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%82-30-%D8%AD%D8%A8%D9%87-%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B9%D9%8A-100-%D8%AE%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A-%D9%85%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%8A-%D9%87%D9%8A%D8%B1%D9%88%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%88%D9%8A%D8%A9)). The national average for a commercial tray is about **17.6–19.8 SAR** ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/ar/article/articledetail/id/1937273)). Feed is the risk: layer feed costs **92–104 SAR per 50 kg** (Nov 2026) and makes up 65–70% of costs ([Sanabil Al-Jouf](https://sanabilaljouf.com.sa/ar/price-indicator); [Dejena](https://dejena.com/baladi-chicken/)). A rural-poultry licence has been available on Naama since July 2025 ([businesscloud](https://businesscloud.sa/%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%AE%D9%8A%D8%B5-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%AC%D9%86-%D8%A3%D9%87%D9%85-%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B7-%D9%88%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%B7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D8%B5%D9%88/)).

| Rank | Enterprise | Why | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Winter leafy greens and herbs, washed and branded (jarjeer, coriander, parsley, dill, mint, lettuces, purslane, baladi basil) | Lowest capex, cash in 5–6 weeks, 3–6× retail price gap between commodity and premium packs | Well salinity; hydroponic incumbents (Dava, Red Sea Farms, Bather, Arable) |
| 2 | Baladi egg flock (~150 hens) | Direct price about 1.5× the commercial tray; repeat subscription buyers | Feed cost; summer heat; needs direct selling |
| 3 | Small lamb batch timed for Eid al-Adha (optional, funded from cash flow) | Najdi averaged a record **1,865 SAR** in 2025; prices rise toward Arafah ([Aleqt](https://www.aleqt.com/تقارير-وتحليلات/أسعار-3-فئات-أضاح-في-السعودية-تكسر-طفرة-2015-10170)) | No sourced lamb buy price; barley has spiked to 120 SAR a bag ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/ar/article/articledetail/id/840085)) |
| 4 | One or two used cooled greenhouses (cucumber, peppers, cherry tomato), year 2 | Used 9×40 m houses in Al-Kharj cost **9,500–11,500 SAR** ([Haraj](https://haraj.com.sa/search/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%88%D8%AA%20%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%87%20%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B9/&gsessionid=TzVrFkpwrKnD2f8i5kuFmDphGpOHB9y9S4hJCXEHiBd7sNQviSkqkQ/)); cucumber yields 10–15 kg/m² in a Riyadh-region trial ([Estidamah](https://estidamah.gov.sa/ar/researches/experiments/Pages/292.aspx)) | Crowded category; summer cooling water and power |
| Avoid | Field tomato and cucumber for wholesale, broilers, white eggs, honey, hydroponics, mushrooms | Saturated markets, high capex or weak data | — |

Water decides all of this. About **69.4% of Al-Kharj wells are slightly to moderately saline, and the rest severely so** ([MDPI Agronomy](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/13/12/3016)). Test the well's EC/TDS before signing anything. Average summer temperatures near 45°C ([HortiDaily](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9130779/saudi-arabia-growing-fruits-and-vegetables-at-an-average-summer-temperature-of-45degc/)) make open-field cropping an October–April business.

## Rent and labour consume two-thirds of the 100k

Earlier lease research found working farms advertised at **18,000 SAR a year for 4,288 m² in Al-Jumum** and **25,000 SAR for 50,000 m² in Al-Shanan (Al-Kharj)**. Durma farms ran 35,000–90,000 SAR ([Dealapp Al-Kharj](https://dealapp.sa/ar/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%82/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%B1%D8%AC/%D9%85%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%B9_%D9%88_%D8%A3%D8%AD%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B4/%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%8A%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%B1)). Lease only a farm that already has a licensed well and an electricity meter. Connecting utilities can cost up to **50,000 SAR** ([rgh.com.sa](https://www.rgh.com.sa/%D8%AA%D9%83%D9%84%D9%81%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%85%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%83%D9%8A%D9%81/)), and new wells near Riyadh are now heavily restricted.

Labour is the second-largest cost. Recruiting a worker through Musaned requires an agricultural register and is capped at six workers per individual ([Rukn](https://rukn-services.com/استقدام-مزارع/)). Using domestic-worker proxies, the one-off cost is about **10,000–15,000 SAR**: a 2,000 SAR visa plus a 7,000–10,000 SAR office fee ([Capsulaa](https://www.capsulaa.sa/saudi-arabia-services/شروط-استقدام-عاملة-منزلية-من-بنجلاديش/)), plus about 650 SAR a year for the iqama ([almaal](https://almaal.org/agricultural-worker-residency-renewal-fees)). Since December 2025, employers must cover housing, food, transport and medical care, give 30 days' paid leave, and pay all recruitment fees ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-updates-labour-laws-for-agricultural-workers-1.500379272)). An establishment pays a levy of 700–800 SAR a month per expat unless it qualifies for the small-firm exemption (owner working full-time, up to 2 expats) ([holool2030](https://holool2030.com/?p=6957); [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/ampArticle/2100362)). **No reliable farm-worker wage was found.** The budget assumes about 2,000 SAR a month all-in for a worker hired locally or transferred, for eight months, which avoids the recruitment cost in year one.

Electricity is cheap but changing. The agricultural tariff is **18/22 halalas per kWh through December 2026, then 19/23 halalas plus a new fixed charge of 140–200 SAR a month from 1 January 2027** ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-to-raise-electricity-water-and-sewage-tariffs-from-january-2027-1.500723516); [Ajel](https://ajel.sa/economy/akkj28rot)). A 5.5 kW pump running 7 hours a day works out to roughly 300–500 SAR a month in 2027 (my calculation). Diesel has cost **1.79 SAR/L since January 2026** ([Asharq Al-Awsat](https://aawsat.com/node/5096937)). A new Hilux single cab costs about 88,000–100,000 SAR ([Al-Muraba](https://www.almuraba.net/japanese-cars/toyota/hilux/hilux-2026/)), so rent or use an existing vehicle.

## Company setup costs a few thousand riyals, not tens of thousands

For one Saudi owner, the choice is a **sole establishment (مؤسسة فردية)**, which is cheapest but carries unlimited liability, or a **one-person LLC**, which gives limited liability with more paperwork and can reportedly be set up online in about a day ([Etqan](https://etqanlawfirm-sa.com/%d8%aa%d8%a3%d8%b3%d9%8a%d8%b3-%d8%b4%d8%b1%d9%83%d8%a9-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%b4%d8%ae%d8%b5-%d8%a7%d9%84%d9%88%d8%a7%d8%ad%d8%af-%d9%81%d9%8a-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%b3%d8%b9%d9%88%d8%af%d9%8a%d8%a9/)). The free freelance document does not appear to cover agriculture ([wdeftksa](https://www.wdeftksa.com/sa/guides/freelance)). Quick-commerce buyers will want a CR, so start as a sole establishment and convert to an LLC once app contracts and liability exposure grow.

## Aziziyah sets the floor price, not your margin

No dated 2025–26 Aziziyah carton prices or official daily bulletin were found. The best public wholesale signals are tomato crates jumping from **15 to 40 SAR in Riyadh** ([Al-Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1043104)) and **15–20 to about 65 SAR in four days** in the Eastern Province in 2026 ([Alyaum](https://www.alyaum.com/articles/6622564/)). GASTAT reported a **−21.4% month-on-month** fall in local tomato wholesale prices in December 2025 ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/business/saudi-inflation-edges-up-to-21-on-higher-rents-gastat-2629392)). Retail local tomatoes ran from 4.9 SAR/kg (March 2026) to 7.3 SAR/kg (July 2026) ([Alyaum](https://www.alyaum.com/articles/6678291/)). Glut-season crates therefore work out to about 1.5–2 SAR/kg against 5–7 SAR/kg at retail.

Riyadh Development Co. says about **60% of the Kingdom's produce trade** passes through Aziziyah ([riyadh.dev](https://new-design.riyadh.dev/en/?p=26879)). The rules are shifting. The market's assets moved to the subsidiary Hulul Salasil in January 2026 ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/bf/arriyadh/article/id/1870543)). A design-and-build contract for a new wholesale market was signed in November 2025 ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/bf/arriyadh/article/id/1860121/multi-business-riyadh-development-ink-deal-to-build-riyadh-wholesale-market)). The North (Muruj) market was evacuated by 30 October 2025 ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/bf/arriyadh/article/id/1843157/riyadh-s-north-market-evacuation-deadline-set-for-oct-30)). Four requirements are documented. **Farmers pre-register through Naama on the MEWA–Riyadh Development digital portal**, which traces produce from farm to consumer ([Sayidaty](https://www.sayidaty.net/node/1792767)). Since about September 2025, MEWA's packaging rules require **a product card on each package** with name, net weight, packing date, origin and the agricultural-register number, plus **food-grade, ventilated, corrugated cartons** ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2214211)). SFDA and MEWA **sample produce for pesticide residues** at Aziziyah, and growers over the maximum residue limit are penalised ([SFDA](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/ar/news/1809); [Alyaum](https://www.alyaum.com/ampArticle/6054254)). Finally, press reports describe trading controlled by expatriate labour and unlicensed stalls ([Sabq](https://sabq.org/article/Fa-Dd-K)). **The Riyadh dalal commission was not found.** The nearest benchmarks are **7% at the Buraidah date market** ([Al-Mowaten](https://www.almowaten.net/?p=378058)) and under 6% plus handling in Amman ([IGC](https://www.theigc.org/publications/understanding-fresh-produce-supply-chain-dynamics-and-price-wedges)). Plan on about 5–8% plus porterage until you confirm the rate on site.

Outside the market, wholesale produce is traded on **Nabt** (a B2B marketplace that raised US$34M; [Inc. Arabia](https://en.incarabia.com/nabt-raises-us34-million-to-scale-fresh-produce-infrastructure-across-saudi-arabia-807098.html)), **Farmi** ([Mubasher](https://www.sharikatmubasher.com/media-hub/experts-thoughts/16468?lang=en)), **Qawafel** (perishables B2B; [Builtin](https://www.builtinaustin.com/company/qawafel)) and **Grove**, which offers farmers an agri-marketing service with cold chain ([Mubasher](https://sharikatmubasher.com/media-hub/experts-thoughts/20593)). Sary is general FMCG, not a produce channel. Use GASTAT's monthly average-price releases ([GASTAT](https://www.stats.gov.sa/documents/d/guest/average-prices-of-goods-and-services-february-2026-ar-en-3-pdf)) as your retail reference, and collect your own Aziziyah dawn prices for three weeks before choosing crops.

## Quick-commerce rewards a brand but can freeze your cash

The buyer pool is real. **Ninja** raised about US$250m at a US$1.5bn valuation in July 2025 and runs roughly 100 dark stores ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1825981); [DealStreetAsia](https://media.dealstreetasia.com/stories/ninja-enters-unicorn-club-448181)). **HungerStation, Jahez, Keeta, Amazon and Noon** have all moved into grocery ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2624019)), and **Rabbit** entered Riyadh in 2025 ([MENAbytes](https://www.menabytes.com/rabbit-expands-saudi/)). The warning case is **Nana**: on **17 April 2026 the Riyadh Commercial Court opened financial reorganisation proceedings for its operator, Central Markets Co., suspending creditor claims** while the app kept running ([Jawlah](https://jawlah.co/en/53814); [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/business/5264854-after-hundreds-millions-investments-saudi-grocery-app-nana-faces-survival-test)). Any unpaid invoices to Nana were frozen. **No app publishes a supplier programme, commission, listing fee or payment term.** Comparable corporate buyers ask for a CR, a VAT certificate and an IBAN ([SALIC](https://www.salic.com/supplier)), so expect the same plus SFDA-compliant labels and barcodes. No evidence shows that apps require Saudi GAP or organic certification. Saudi GAP, introduced in 2018, is the cheaper credibility marker if one is needed ([MEWA](https://www.mewa.gov.sa/ar/MediaCenter/Events/Pages/43265.aspx)).

Pricing should sit just under the hydroponic incumbents. On Carrefour KSA, **hydroponic lettuce sells for 5.99–6.95 SAR a head** and hydroponic cucumbers for 9.95 SAR per 800 g ([Carrefour KSA](https://www.carrefourksa.com/mafsau/en/c/FKSA1209030)). Red Sea Farms deliberately pitches between cheap local produce and imports ([HortiDaily](https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9452320/red-sea-farms-produce-now-in-80-ksa-supermarkets-in-back-to-school-launch)). A reasonable shelf target is **4–6 SAR for a 100–150 g washed herb pack, 5–6 SAR for a lettuce head and 28–35 SAR for a baladi egg tray**. Assume, without a source, that the app buys at 60–70% of shelf price. The way to beat Dava, Bather and Arable is not price. It is **soil-grown baladi flavour, varieties they do not carry (purslane, local mint, molokhia in spring), a harvest date on every pack, and published residue-test results.** Limit risk structurally: run at least three buyers, keep any single app under 30% of sales, refuse consignment where possible, cap open credit at about two weeks of deliveries per app, and stop shipping to any app that pays late.

### Rough first-year scenarios (estimates, not forecasts)

These scenarios assume about 2,000 m² of greens harvested January–April plus 150 layers. Conservative: 4,000 kg of sellable greens, 25% sold premium at a net 14 SAR/kg and 75% wholesale at 2.5 SAR/kg, about 21,500 SAR, plus 500 egg trays at 25 SAR (12,500 SAR), for **~34,000 SAR revenue**. Base: 6,000 kg, 40% premium at 16 SAR/kg and 60% at 3 SAR/kg, about 49,000 SAR, plus 720 trays at 30 SAR (21,600 SAR), for **~71,000 SAR**. Cash costs come to about 84,000 SAR from the budget (excluding the reserve) plus about 8,000 SAR of extra layer feed: 150 birds at 0.12 kg/day is roughly 130 bags a year. That gives a **year-one result of about −58,000 SAR (conservative) and −21,000 SAR (base)**. About 25,000 SAR of the costs are one-off, so a base-case year two at a full October–April season approaches break-even or a small profit. Every price, yield and margin in this paragraph is an assumption to replace with real data.

## Conclusion

The business is not really a farm decision. It is a channel and brand decision attached to a cheap lease. Nationally, Saudi produce is a surplus market where small growers take whatever the auction pays. The margin exists only where freshness, provenance and packaging can be shown to a Riyadh buyer, and the cheapest crops to prove that with are fast-cutting winter herbs. Quick-commerce apps amplify a brand but also concentrate credit risk, as Nana's 2026 court proceedings show. The winning sequence is therefore direct customers first, apps second, and the auction last.

The plan's biggest unknowns are not agronomic. They are three numbers no public source revealed: the dalal's cut, the apps' buy-in price, and the real cost of a farm worker. A few weeks of dawn visits and supplier calls will resolve them for almost no money, and they decide whether year two turns a profit.
