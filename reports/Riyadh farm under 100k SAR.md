# Lease, don't buy: Riyadh farms at 100k

**Bottom line:** In late 2026, 100,000 SAR does not buy a legally titled farm with a well and electricity anywhere near Riyadh. The cheapest farm-classified listings seen within roughly 100 km of the city start at about **413,000 SAR for ~2,430 m²** (Durma), and the cheapest with a well and power cost about **1,000,000 SAR for 5,000 m²** (Al-Jilah). Close-in farmland trades at roughly **60–325 SAR/m²**, so 100k buys only about 300–1,700 m² of land with no water, which is smaller than any legal agricultural plot seen for sale. Anything advertised near Riyadh at or below 100k is very likely one of the things this report tells you to avoid: fenced state land (شبوك), possession-only land (وضع يد), an unfinished title claim (حجة استحكام), an illegal subdivision, or an undivided share in someone else's farm. The money goes much further as rent. Working farms in the Al-Kharj outskirts, Al-Jumum and Durma are advertised at **18,000–35,000 SAR a year**, so 100k covers about 3–5 years, and istirahas with a yard on Riyadh's edge rent from **25,000–30,000 SAR a year**. Free government land grants have been suspended until about 2030, and neither banks nor the Agricultural Development Fund finance a hobby farm purchase. **The practical recommendation is to sign a written annual lease on a working farm with a well, 50–90 km out, and keep part of the budget as a reserve.** Buy only if you can pool money with partners to reach about 400,000–600,000 SAR for a properly titled plot. **Important caveat:** the listing sites (Aqar, Haraj, Bayut, Dealapp, Wasalt, PropertyFinder, OpenSooq, Mstaml, Raghdan) were blocked during this research. Every listing price below comes from search-engine snippets of those pages. The pages could not be opened, so it is unknown whether each ad is still live, when it was posted, or exactly what it includes. Treat every figure as an asking price to verify, not a confirmed offer.

## What 100,000 SAR actually buys: a few hundred square metres and no water

Applying the observed per-m² prices to the budget shows how little 100k reaches. At the 170–325 SAR/m² asked in the Durma and Al-Muzahmiyah subdivision schemes, 100k equals **about 300–600 m²**. In Al-Hayathem (112–150 SAR/m²) it equals about 670–900 m². At the 40–71 SAR/m² seen on larger Al-Muzahmiyah parcels it equals about 1,400–2,500 m². Only in Shaqra, about 190 km out, would it reach 33,000–70,000 m², and only if a seller would partition a huge parcel, which none was advertising. The smallest legal agricultural plots seen for sale were about 2,000 m². A 300–600 m² "farm" near Riyadh is therefore a sign of an unapproved split or an undivided share.

Water and electricity are what make land a farm, and they belong to the 1-million-SAR tier. No listing at any price under about 1M paired a well with electricity and a deed. A well also cannot simply be added later. Since July 2025, MEWA licenses new wells on the non-renewable sedimentary aquifers that underlie Riyadh **only for limited-consumption specialised projects** such as poultry, greenhouses and hydroponics. The project must already be at least 50% built, it may have no more than two wells, and the land must be held under a valid deed or a long-term lease. Heavily depleted aquifers, **including the Saq aquifer near Riyadh**, are excluded altogether ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2198855); [Ajel](https://ajel.sa/9Rmp6c/)). In practice, a cheap plot with no licensed well near Riyadh will most likely stay dry. Even the Al-Kharj listing above needed a well 120 m deep.

Crop choice is also restricted. Green fodder has been banned since November 2018 ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/568056)), with fines of 4,000 SAR per hectare announced in 2019 ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/612910)). One source reports a halt on perennial fodder such as alfalfa from **16 November 2026** ([Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1944977)). Running costs are rising too. A new agricultural electricity tariff of **19 halalas/kWh up to 6,000 kWh a month and 23 halalas above**, plus fixed charges of 140–200 SAR a month, takes effect on 1 January 2027 ([Al-Eqtisadiah](https://www.aleqt.com/الأخبار/21-هللة-للكهرباء-السكنية-و9-ريالات-للمياه-في-السعودية-من-أول-يناير-15729)). Realistic small-farm uses are palms, vegetables, a greenhouse or a family weekend retreat, not fodder.

Transaction costs eat into a 100k purchase as well. Sales of agricultural land carry **5% Real Estate Transaction Tax** ([ZATCA RETT guide](https://zatca.gov.sa:443/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Simplified%20guide%20to%20the%20real%20estate%20transaction%20tax.pdf)). RETT is legally the seller's liability, but contracts often shift it to the buyer, so agree in writing who pays. Brokerage defaults to **2.5% of the deal value** unless agreed otherwise in writing ([REGA](https://rega.gov.sa/media/wkfdtv3y/تقرير-الوساطة-العقارية.pdf)). Plan for about 7,500–8,500 SAR on top of the price, or an agreed price of about 92,000 SAR if the all-in total must stay at 100k. Non-Saudis pay a further 2% fee on real-estate dispositions in Riyadh under the 2026 foreign-ownership rules ([Asharq Al-Awsat](https://aawsat.com/الاقتصاد/5287566-السعودية-تقر-اللائحة-التنفيذية-لتملك-غير-السعوديين-للعقار)). No source showed that law opening agricultural land near Riyadh to foreigners, so a non-Saudi buyer should assume it is closed to them and should never buy through a Saudi nominee.

At ~1,500 SAR every weekend that comes to about 78,000 SAR a year, so daily rental only makes sense for fewer than about 15–20 nights a year.

The other alternatives are weaker. **Government land grants** under the Fallow Land Distribution Law have been suspended since 1431H. Cabinet Decision 130 extended the suspension 10 years from 2/12/1441H, to about 2030 ([Tashree](https://tashree.app/ncar/regulations/2778)). The only state route now is **leasing** fallow land or plots in approved schemes through MEWA's Aradi platform ([Ajel](https://ajel.sa/local/ms6rtv)) or the Naama platform ([Naama](https://naama.sa/services/details/3a6dd09b-f49a-4ee6-be6f-e26117a53633)). Both target commercial investment projects, not weekend farms. **Financing** does not help either. Al Rajhi's published real-estate finance covers residential property, not agricultural land ([Al Rajhi](https://www.alrajhibank.com.sa/en/Personal/Finance/Real-Estate-Finance/Home-Finance)). ADF loans require you to **already own the land or hold a deed-registered lease**, plus an agricultural register ([ADF](https://adf.gov.sa/en/ServicesCatalog/Pages/Services100.aspx)). One older report says the lease must run at least 15 years ([Ajel](https://ajel.sa/local/8gnxwz)). ADF funds farm projects, not hobby land purchases. A single consultancy claims ADF finances land purchase, but no official source confirms it. No farm or istiraha listing offering **installments (تقسيط)** was found. Owner financing would have to be negotiated privately.

**Shared ownership** with friends or family is the one route that makes buying possible. But buying an undivided share (حصة مشاعة) gives you a percentage, not a defined piece of ground. The Ministry of Justice says owning a percentage does not mean owning a specific part until a formal subdivision (فرز) is completed, and incomplete transfers turn into disputes when an owner dies ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/local/na/2225490)). No partner can separate a share without everyone's agreement or a court order ([Al-Watan](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1188761)). In December 2025 the Ministry launched a Najiz procedure to process and transfer shared agricultural deeds electronically ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/local/na/2225490)), which helps, but a written partnership agreement covering exit, inheritance and a usage schedule remains essential.

## Five legal checks decide whether a farm is real

**First, the deed.** Buy only land with a current **electronic title deed (صك إلكتروني)** in the seller's name, and check it on Najiz using the "verify title-deed data" (التحقق من بيانات الصك العقاري) service ([amlak.net.sa](https://amlak.net.sa/?p=29629)). An old paper deed is usable only after the seller updates it to an electronic deed, before you pay. A حجة استحكام still in progress is not a title. The State Properties Authority (Ehkam) recognises possession-based claims **only where the land was revived before 9/11/1387H** (1968) ([Qanoniah](https://qanoniah.com/File/wkxV01WJv87ER1dn9r2jqQ3lp-قواعد-وآلية-عمل-لجان-النظر-في-طلبات-تملك-العقارات)), so recently fenced land will never get one. A Haraj ad that sold land "filed on the Ahkam platform" (مرفوعه منصه احكام) shows that unfinished claims are being marketed ([Haraj](https://haraj.com.sa/en/11165972392/%D9%85%D8%B2%D8%B1%D8%B9%D8%A9_%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B9_%D8%A8%D8%B5%D9%83_%D8%A7%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%88%D9%86%D9%8A_%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B6%D9%8A_%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B9_%D9%85%D8%B1%D9%81%D9%88%D8%B9%D9%87_%D9%85%D9%86%D8%B5%D9%87_%D8%A7%D8%AD%D9%83%D8%A7%D9%85/)). Parts of the Riyadh region are also being moved into REGA's **Real Estate Registry**, including districts in Al-Rain from November 2025 ([Al Arabiya](https://www.alarabiya.net/aswaq/realestate/2025/11/12/العقار-السعودية-تعلن-بدء-اعمال-السجل-العقاري-لـ-18-حيا-بمنطقة-الرياض)) and Durma from February 2026 ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/local/saudi-arabia/2227956)). Registered land carries a property number (رقم عقار) and a registration title (صك تسجيل ملكية) ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/ampArticle/2221856)). If the plot is in a registered district, ask for those.

**Second, the transfer.** Transfer must be electronic, with the price paid into the system's intermediary (escrow) bank account and RETT verified before completion ([Slaati](https://slaati.com/2021/06/07/p1864631.html)). A law-firm blog says transfers now begin on the **Real Estate Exchange (البورصة العقارية)** rather than the old Najiz path ([HD Law](https://hd-lawfirm.com.sa/blog/محامي-إفراغ-عقاري-إلكتروني/); [MoJ guidelines](https://www.moj.gov.sa/Documents/Sharing/GuidelinesForRealEstateStockExchangePlatform.pdf)). Confirm the current flow on moj.gov.sa. Agricultural transfers may also need ADF approval where the land carries an ADF loan, and a partial transfer needs a subdivision document (مستند التجزئة). This comes from MoJ documents seen only in search summaries, so confirm it too. Never pay cash or a deposit (عربون) outside this flow. Haraj itself advises verifying the electronic deed and avoiding cash or cheques ([Haraj](https://haraj.com.sa/tags/%D8%B4%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%A1_%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B6%D9%8A%20%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B9/)).

**Third, subdivision.** Agricultural land may not be sold as plots (شرائح / مخططات) without MEWA approval and a municipal subdivision approval under Cabinet Decision 119 of 1437H. MEWA has warned offices and individuals publicly against it ([Ajel](https://ajel.sa/local/2ssp8t); [MEWA](https://www.mewa.gov.sa/ar/MediaCenter/News/Pages/News10872020.aspx)). Most sub-100k "farm plots" fail this test. Converting agricultural land to residential is a separate municipal process ([Sabq](https://sabq.org/article/FL-6fCB)).

**Fourth, water and power.** Get the **well licence (رخصة البئر)** and confirm it matches the deed. Unlicensed wells are removed in encroachment campaigns ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/ampArticle/1501971)). Check that an electricity meter exists and its bills are paid. MEWA runs an e-service for connecting farms to the grid ([Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/business/5056149-saudi-environment-ministry-launches-electronic-service-agricultural-sector-connect?page=3)), but no source gave connection costs or timelines for remote plots.

**Fifth, the broker and the ad.** Every online property ad needs a REGA ad licence, issued through the FAL platform on the basis of a brokerage contract the owner approves by SMS ([Raghdan](https://raghdan.sa/ar/news/real-estate-brokerage-contract-licensing-guide-2025/)). An ad with no licence number is a red flag. The same 2.5% brokerage default applies to the first year's rent on a lease ([Sabq](https://sabq.org/saudia/7ljg9ftux2)).

## Sub-100k "farms" are where encroachment removals hit

The sub-100k price band near Riyadh is where state enforcement lands. In just one quarter (Q2 1446H), Riyadh-region committees cleared encroachments on about **39.87 million m²** of government land, including **657 cases over ~9.8 million m² in Riyadh city** alone ([Al-Eqtisadiah](https://www.aleqt.com/2025/07/26/article_2764442.html); [Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2206066)). Nazaha removed more than **20 million m²** of encroachments in Wadi Namar that had been falsely marked as "private property" ([Al-Yaum](https://www.alyaum.com/ampArticle/6044274)). Riyadh Municipality cleared 111 unauthorised sites and confiscated 34 livestock fences (شبوك مواشي) in one northern campaign ([Riyadh Municipality](https://alriyadh.gov.sa/en/news/riyadh-news-312)). Anyone who paid for that land lost the money.

The common traps follow from this. Sellers offer fenced desert land or "possessed" land with a handwritten sale paper (ورقة مبايعة) or a grant letter. They market an undivided share or an unapproved subdivision as a "farm" ([Okaz](https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2118192)). Some old deeds have been cancelled, are disputed, or overlap other deeds. Some sellers act under an expired or fake power of attorney (check the وكالة on Najiz). Some ask for a deposit before electronic transfer. Others sell land with unlicensed wells, unpaid power bills or an ADF lien, or land sitting in a flood channel, road reservation or planning-suspension zone. Planning rules around Riyadh also shifted in 2025. Restrictions were lifted on 81.48 km² of north Riyadh ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/saudi-crown-prince-orders-real-estate-reforms-caps-land-prices-to-stabilise-riyadh-market-1.500078195)) and on 33.2 km² of west Riyadh ([Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/ksa/2025/10/12/govt-unfreezes-lands-in-western-riyadh/)). Ask the municipality (via Balady or the governorate municipality) for a plot's planning status and whether any removal decision exists.

## Recommendation and action plan

**Recommendation:** Do not buy a farm near Riyadh with 100,000 SAR. Sign a **written annual lease on a working farm with an existing well and electricity meter, 50–90 km out**, in Al-Kharj (Al-Shanan, Al-Jumum, Al-Sahba, Al-Budaiya) or Durma. Budget about **20,000–35,000 SAR a year**, and keep 30,000–40,000 SAR in reserve for utilities, labour, fencing repairs and a possible move. That gives two to three years of real farm use while you learn which area suits you. If weekend space matters more than cultivation, a 25,000–30,000 SAR istiraha in Nemar or Al-Mahdiyah is closer and cheaper. If ownership is the real goal, form a written partnership (or company) with two to five people to reach about **400,000–600,000 SAR**. That buys a titled 2,000–5,000 m² plot in Durma, Al-Muzahmiyah or Al-Hayathem. Insist on a formal subdivision or a clear usage agreement.

## Conclusion

Near Riyadh, the real dividing line is between land with legal water and everything else, not between cheap and expensive farms. The 2025 well rules mean an unwatered plot near the city will most likely stay unwatered. That locks the value of a farm into the few properties that already hold a licensed well and a deed, and those trade at about ten times the budget. Renting such a farm hands you its most valuable and least replaceable asset, legal water, for 20–35% of the budget a year. Buying the cheap alternative pays full price for land that enforcement campaigns are actively clearing.

Two things would change this advice: a legal subdivision scheme near Riyadh selling small titled plots with shared wells, or the end of the grant suspension around 2030. Neither is visible today. Because every listing price here came from search snippets with the listing sites blocked, the first real task is to open those sites, confirm current asking prices and rents, and check whether any 18,000–35,000 SAR farm leases are still on offer.
