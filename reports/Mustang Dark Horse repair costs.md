# Bid low or walk: wrecked Dark Horse math

## ملخص

السيارة في الصور ليست "ضربة خلفية" عادية. الدعامات الخلفية (الشاص) يمين ويسار، وأرضية الشنطة، والرفارف الخلفية الاثنين، والقائم الخلفي الأيسر (C-pillar) كلها متضررة، ومنظومة التعليق الخلفي مع الدفرنس مقتلعة من مكانها. والكبوت منثني أيضاً، فغالباً فيه ضرر أمامي غير مذكور في القائمة. قطع الغيار الأصلية (وكالة) لبنود القائمة وحدها تكلف تقريباً **25,000 إلى 49,000 دولار (95,000 إلى 185,000 ريال)** بأسعار أمريكية. القطع التجارية أو المستعملة تنزل التكلفة إلى **16,000 إلى 34,000 دولار (61,000 إلى 129,000 ريال)**، ويضاف إليها أجور العمل والدهان وهامش للمفاجآت. ولا توجد قطع "تجاري" لهيكل الجيل الجديد S650، فالصاج الإنشائي أصلي فقط. سعر Dark Horse نظيفة في السعودية حوالي **285,000 إلى 290,000 ريال**، وبعد إصلاح هيكلي وعداد مجهول لن تُباع غالباً بأكثر من 160,000 إلى 190,000 ريال (تقدير). لذلك الإصلاح قطعة قطعة بقطع أصلية **خاسر**. الطريق الوحيد المعقول هو تركيب نص خلفي (قصّة خلفية) من سيارة متبرعة، أو تفكيك السيارة وبيعها قطع. **أقصى مزايدة معقولة: حوالي 35,000 ريال** قبل ضريبة 15% (حوالي 40,000 ريال شامل الضريبة). **التوصية: لا تشترِ إذا كان هدفك سيارة للاستخدام**. الشراء مقبول فقط لتاجر قطع أو ورشة عندها قصّة خلفية جاهزة، وبسعر لا يتجاوز هذا الحد.

Fixing this car with new Ford parts costs more than it will be worth afterwards. The 17 items on the listing look like a rear-end job, but the photos show a **rear clip–level structural loss**: both rear rails are crushed, the trunk floor is deformed, both quarter panels are destroyed, the left C-pillar is crumpled, and the IRS subframe, differential and a halfshaft have been torn out. The buckled hood also points to front damage that the list does not mention. US-sourced OEM parts for the listed items alone come to about **$25,000–49,000 (SAR 95,000–185,000)**. Used and copy parts bring that to about **$16,000–34,000 (SAR 61,000–129,000)**, before labor, contingency and the usual Saudi markup over US prices. No aftermarket S650 structural sheet metal exists, so the costliest items have no cheap version. A clean low-mileage 2024 Dark Horse is listed at **SAR 285,000–290,000** in Saudi Arabia. With structural-repair history and unverifiable mileage, the realistic resale is an estimated SAR 160,000–190,000. On those numbers only two plans make money: grafting on a donor rear clip (half-cut), or parting the car out. Both put a sensible maximum hammer bid near **SAR 35,000 (about $9,300)**. The 15% VAT and any mazadat fees come on top of that. For anyone who wants a car to drive, this is a no-buy.

> **How to read the numbers.** Figures with a link come from the research notes, almost all of them US online Ford-dealer prices (sale vs. MSRP) or US retailers. Anything marked **(est.)** is my estimate, used to fill gaps where no source was found. Several Ford parts sites could only be read through search snippets, and **no Saudi dealer (Al Jazirah Ford) or Haraj part prices could be found online**. Every OEM figure therefore needs a VIN-based quote, and Gulf-spec part numbers (for example the tail lamps) may differ from US ones. SAR = USD × 3.75. A Saudi counter price is often the US MSRP plus freight, 5% duty and 15% VAT. A rough working multiplier of US MSRP × 1.3–1.6 is unsourced and is not applied in the table.

## The photos reveal a rear-clip loss the list understates

The listing's wording (ضربة على الشاص خلفي يمين ويسار, "rear chassis hit right and left") sounds like a fixable bumper-and-rails job. The photos show more. According to the coordinator's photo notes, the rear bumper beam and the rear body panel are crushed and exposed, the trunk lid is folded upward, and the spare/subwoofer well is deformed. The left quarter and C-pillar/roof edge are crumpled, and the IRS has been pulled out of position with a CV axle, knuckle and differential hanging on the ground. Tail lamps, quad tips and mufflers are dislodged, and many rear harness wires are dangling. Repairing all of that is not a matter of bolting on new panels. The car would have to go on a frame bench and be rebuilt from the C-pillar back, either by sectioning in new rails, floor and quarters or by joining on a donor rear clip.

Three details on the front and in the paperwork change the economics further. First, the **hood is buckled at the front-center edge and the grille is pushed in**, which suggests a secondary front impact. The radiator support, radiator and AC condenser are not on the damage list, but they have to be assumed damaged until someone checks. Second, the matte-black wrap means the original paint cannot be inspected, so a full refinish is the safe assumption. Third, the car is **Saudi-registered with a valid periodic inspection (فحص دوري)**. That makes a US flood or salvage import less likely, and the mud inside fits crash debris and yard storage better than flooding. The flip side is that, as a local accident car, its history is a domestic one that a later buyer can look up.

The listing also says the hammer price is subject to **15% VAT** (خاضعة للضريبة). Every bid figure in this report is the hammer price before that VAT.

Three features of the table matter more than any single line. First, **the sheet metal has no cheap version**. No aftermarket S650 quarter panels were found, and cut quarters from such a new model rarely come up used. The structural line therefore stays at OEM cost whichever way the car is repaired. Second, **the most expensive bolt-on items have the cheapest substitutes**: tail lamps, mufflers, MagneRide shocks and the key. Used Dark Horse tail lamps at $500 each replace $1,300–1,450 OEM units, and a MagneRide-delete kit replaces about $1,000 of dampers for $480 (the car then loses its adaptive damping). Third, some lines cost almost nothing. The transmission mount is about $55–149, and a used rear cradle pulled from a Dark Horse with 6,000 miles sold for $500.

### Labor is the other half of the bill

No S650-specific flat-rate times were found, so the labor figures are built up from proxies. Replacing one quarter panel takes 8–12 hours by book, and some sources say 15–30 hours. Shops charge $75–150 an hour, and a single-side job typically totals $3,000–6,000 ([JD Power](https://www.jdpower.com/cars/shopping-guides/how-much-does-it-cost-to-fix-the-quarter-panel-on-my-car), [Jerry](https://jerry.ai/?p=25644)). Book time often leaves out test-fitting ([BodyShop Business](https://www.bodyshopbusiness.com/charge-for-what-you-do-replacing-a-quarter-panel/)). A shop quoted about 13 hours to drop the IRS cradle on a 2018 GT ([StangNet](https://stangnet.com/mustang-forums/threads/13-hr-labor-to-swap-gears.903707/?amp=1)). On that basis I estimate the whole rebuild at **110–215 hours**:

| Work | Hours (est.) |
|---|---|
| Both rails, trunk floor and both quarters on a frame bench | 80–150 |
| Rear drivetrain and suspension | 10–20 |
| Harness repair | 10–30 |
| Front end | 8–15 |

At US rates that is $8,000–32,000 (SAR 31,000–121,000). At an assumed Saudi independent-shop rate of SAR 100–200 an hour (unsourced), it is about **SAR 11,000–43,000**. Ford dealer body shops will not be cheap.

The donor-clip path wins on cost because one assembly replaces the whole list from rows 4, 6–8, 11, 12 and 15–17 at used-parts prices. It has several practical limits. First, the donor must be a coupe, not a convertible. If it is a GT rather than a Dark Horse, the Dark Horse-specific pieces have to be kept or swapped: the 3.73 or 3.55 Torsen differential, the Brembo-type knuckles (PR3Z-5B759-B), the active exhaust and the bumper cover. Second, no S650 half-cut prices or Saudi availability were found, so the clip figure above is an estimate. Fourth, a sectioned car carries a lasting stigma at resale, and the joins must follow Ford's sectioning procedures for the repair to be safe.

## Saudi clean-car values cap a sensible bid near SAR 35,000

The ceiling is set by Saudi resale prices, not US prices. Low-kilometer 2024 Dark Horses are listed in Saudi Arabia at **SAR 285,000–290,000** ([SaudiSale Khobar](https://cars.saudisale.com/en/listings/0A83wv), [SaudiSale Jeddah](https://cars.saudisale.com/en/listings/Zf9aWc/2024-ford-mustang-dark-horse)), against a new price of **SAR 315,900 for the automatic** ([SayaraBay](https://www.sayarabay.com/new-cars/ford/mustang-2022-2024/price?amp=1)). In the US, by comparison, a clean 2024 Dark Horse is worth about $46,200 (SAR 173,000) on KBB ([KBB](https://www.kbb.com/ford/mustang/2024/dark-horse-coupe-2d)). Asking prices are not transaction prices. This car will also carry a major structural-repair history and an odometer that cannot be read. I therefore assume, as an estimate, a **35–45% haircut, giving a repaired resale of SAR 160,000–190,000 (midpoint SAR 175,000)**.

The maximum bid is the repaired resale minus four things: the repair cost, a 20% contingency on the repair, and a 10% margin for risk and time, all divided by 1.15 to strip out the VAT charged on the hammer price. That leaves the following:

The US salvage market gives a cross-check. This car belongs closer to the bottom of that range than the top. Taking the donor-clip and part-out results together, the sensible **maximum hammer bid is about SAR 35,000** (≈ $9,300; about SAR 40,250 with VAT). There is a hard ceiling of about **SAR 40,000** hammer, and only for a bidder who already has a donor clip or buyers lined up for the engine and front clip. Mazadat buyer fees were not researched and come on top.

## Red flags that could push the value lower

The biggest unknown is **why the car doesn't run, has no key, and has an unreadable odometer while its harness is cut**. That combination is what a crash produces, but it is also what a stripped car looks like when modules have been pulled. Until the battery is connected and a scan shows which modules answer, the cost of rows 1–3 and 12 could be small or could be thousands. Key programming in particular cannot succeed until the BCM, PCM and RF receiver network is alive. Mileage is stored in more than one module, so a scan tool can often recover it even with a dead cluster ([Ranger6G](https://www.ranger6g.com/forum/threads/instrument-cluster-failed.11574/page-4)). Until it is recovered, treat the mileage as unknown.

**Rust under a 2024 car** is abnormal in a dry climate. It points either to bare metal exposed by the crash or to water exposure. Combined with mud in the cabin, it justifies checking for silt under the carpets and corrosion on seat-rail bolts and connectors. Flood restoration of a module-heavy car often doubles the expected electrical cost ([EngineerFix](https://engineerfix.com/can-a-flooded-car-be-fixed-the-real-costs/)). The crash was also violent enough to tear out the IRS, so check whether any airbags or restraint modules deployed. The listing doesn't mention them, and they would add a large bill. **Recall 24V593** covers fractured rear knuckles on 2024 Mustangs, so run the VIN for open recalls ([NHTSA](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2024/RCLRPT-24V593-3301.PDF)). Finally, **Gulf-spec part numbers can differ** from the US numbers used throughout this report, notably the tail lamps and possibly the key fob frequency. And because no Saudi dealer or Haraj part prices could be found online, every SAR figure is a US price converted at 3.75, not a Riyadh counter quote.

## Conclusion

The damage list prices out to roughly the value of a used Mustang GT, but the decision does not hinge on that list. It hinges on two things the list leaves out: there are no copy parts for S650 structure, and the photos show front damage. Together they turn a "rear hit" into a rear-clip transplant. This car is therefore worth what its intact front half and drivetrain can earn, either as the base for a donor-clip rebuild or as parts. As a project you repair and drive, it is not worth any price.

**Recommendation: no-buy for personal use.** For a dismantler or a shop with a sourced S650 coupe rear clip, the car is a conditional buy at a hammer price of **SAR 35,000 or less** (SAR 40,000 at most). Before bidding, get three inexpensive answers if you can: whether the car powers up and its modules respond to a scan, whether any airbags deployed, and a phone quote from Al Jazirah Ford on the two quarter panels and the tail lamps. These are the facts most likely to move the numbers in this report.
